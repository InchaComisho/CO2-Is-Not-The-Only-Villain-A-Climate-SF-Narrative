## Afterword

Part 5 Episode 12, as the final episode of Part 5, gathered once the themes built up so far.

If we organize what Part 5 has depicted, it becomes the following six points.

### 1. Continuing

Auxiliary wheels do not end once they have moved.

They need maintenance, inspection, updating, records, and the reason to continue being put back into words.

Station-front mist, park benches, rain gardens, retention basins, and fishing-port water-temperature charts do not continue simply because they were made once.

They remain only when someone looks at them, repairs them, records them, and hands them to the next year.

### 2. Spreading

To bring a mechanism that worked in one town into another town, its principle had to be translated.

Not increasing the same form, but raising the same purpose in another form.

That was what Part 5 meant by spreading.

### 3. Fitting in

Auxiliary wheels that have been received are borrowed things at first.

They gradually become the town’s standard as they are translated into the town’s own words.

How to run the mist.

The size of the rain garden.

The guidance for the retention basin.

How to read the port’s water-temperature chart.

By being tried under that town’s conditions, failing in small ways, and being corrected, the auxiliary wheels fit into the town.

### 4. Passing on

Auxiliary wheels are weak if they remain only in booklets and explanations.

But they also fade if they are left only as habits of speech.

That is why both words and records were needed.

“A mist day.”

“The bench whose shade shifts in the afternoon.”

“The rain garden where we leave the grass.”

“The detour when the river is high.”

“The water-temperature-color morning meeting.”

Those town words were born, and at the same time, people could return to booklets, diagrams, and photographs.

In that way, auxiliary wheels approached ingenuity that can be passed on.

### 5. Becoming ordinary

The more auxiliary wheels fit into a town, the more ordinary they become.

That is also a success.

But at the same time, it is also an entrance to becoming harder to see and easier to cut away.

That is why the reason to continue had to be put back into words every year even after they became ordinary.

Not to erase heat, but to increase by a few minutes the time people can remain standing.

Not to stop all water, but to receive it once before letting it flow.

Not to predict the future, but to prepare a little ahead.

Auxiliary wheels are not there to erase everything, but to bend things a little.

That reason had to keep being updated every year.

### 6. Continuing even after becoming invisible

At the end of Part 5, what we saw was invisible continuation.

On a morning when no one praises it, the mist is inspected.

On a forenoon when no one notices, the bench’s shadow is checked.

On an evening when no one is watching, the rain-garden grass is maintained so it is not cut too far.

Even on days when no one is present, the retention-basin records are updated.

In a sea where no one makes a fuss, the water-temperature chart is quietly rewritten.

Being invisible does not mean it is not working.

Some things become hard to see precisely because they have dissolved into daily life.

Part 5 thoroughly examined how auxiliary wheels can remain.

Auxiliary wheels do not end when they are made.

They do not end when they are moved.

They continue, spread, fit in, are passed on, have their reasons spoken again after becoming ordinary, and keep being supported after becoming invisible.

Part 5 depicted that whole process.

In Part 6, these accumulated layers will be seen inside a wider landscape.

One town.

One port.

After the auxiliary wheels in each of them have continued, how does the outline of the region change?

How far do they reach?

Where do they fail to reach?

Where do new burdens appear?

We will look at what comes after that in Part 6.
