# Part 5 Episode 8 — Until the received auxiliary wheels fit the shape of the town

## Foreword

In Part 5 Episode 7, we followed how another town receives auxiliary wheels that have spread, and looked at the difference between the town that brings them and the town that receives them.

Episode 8 turns to what happens after they are received.

Auxiliary wheels do not take root in a town just because they have been brought there.

They are tried little by little, corrected, and translated into the words of the people who use them. Only then do they become that town’s auxiliary wheels.

This episode has the protagonist and the AI look, inside a small town meeting room, at what it means for something to fit in.

Only then do auxiliary wheels from outside gradually fit the shape of the town.

## Section 1 — The first attempt is still something from outside the town

The mist system brought from another town.

The rain-garden idea that had worked in another watershed.

The retention-basin operation sheet organized in another town.

The way of reading water-temperature charts used in another port.

All of them are useful as references.

All of them become learning.

But at first, they are still slightly distant.

“They are borrowed things.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— After the briefing session, the town actually tries them.

— In one corner of the station front, a simple mist system is operated.

— At the edge of a park, a small hollow like a rain garden is made.

— In a riverside open space, a temporary line is drawn for use restrictions during rising water.

— At the port morning meeting, one water-temperature memo is brought in.

There is a form.

There are documents.

There has been explanation.

Even so, for the people of that town, these things still require care.

— Even turning the switch on creates hesitation.

— People are unsure when to stop it.

— Hands pause over how much grass should remain.

— Words catch in the meeting because people are unsure how to read the water-temperature chart.

‘As long as it remains borrowed, people handle it carefully every time they use it.’

“Right.”

A borrowed thing feels like something one must not break.

It feels like something one must not get wrong.

If it departs from the original town’s way, it looks as if it has failed.

That is why, in the first town that receives it, everyone becomes a little cautious.

— When to run the mist.

— How to leave grass in the rain garden.

— How to check gates and open spaces around the retention basin.

— Who reads the water-temperature chart.

— Who checks the shade on the benches.

Each one has to be translated again into the town’s own words.

‘Auxiliary wheels from outside are not the town’s own from the beginning.’

“Yeah.”

— Within that town’s life, they are slowly reworded.

— They fail in small ways.

— They gradually become easier to use.

— Without passing through that process, they simply return to the shelf as borrowed documents.

“If it ends at receiving, it ends as documents after all.”

‘Only once it begins moving does room appear for it to fit in.’

“That’s it.”

## Section 2 — To fit in means the way of using it changes

‘Then what does it mean for it to fit in?’

The AI asked.

I thought for a moment.

“Maybe it means the people of that town can explain it in different words.”

At first, they say things like this.

— This mist system apparently worked well in that other town.

— This rain garden is a method used in another watershed.

— This water-temperature chart is material used in another port.

None of that is wrong.

But as long as the explanation remains that way, the auxiliary wheel is still something from outside.

“When it begins to fit in, the words change.”

— At this station front, the waiting time between three and four in the afternoon is hard, so we run it briefly only then.

— At this bus stop, shade suits the place better than mist.

— In this town, rain falls short and strong, so the rain-garden hollow is made small and the overflow path is decided first.

— In this riverside open space, when the water level approaches this mark, we announce use restrictions early.

— In this port, when the water is in this temperature band, we bring up this fish species first in the morning meeting.

‘When it becomes the words of the people who use it, it has begun to fit in.’

“I think so.”

To fit in does not mean preserving the original form exactly.

It means the words change to match the town.

The way of using it changes.

The way of stopping it changes.

The wording on guide signs changes.

The explanation in meetings changes.

And yet the original purpose is not lost.

‘The purpose remains, while the form and words change.’

“The things we saw in Episodes 6 and 7 enter daily life here.”

— Principles from outside become the words of the town’s life.

— The words in documents become words at the site.

— Words at the site become explanations to residents.

— Explanations to residents become the town’s standard.

At that stage, auxiliary wheels that were borrowed gradually become their own.

The explanations in meetings, the wording on signs, and the way of speaking to residents all begin to match the town’s rhythm.

“So fitting in means that the people of the town can correct it in their own words.”

## Section 3 — Before it fits in, there are small failures

“But it does not fit in immediately.”

I said after a short pause.

‘Of course not.’

I opened the trial records.

They did not contain only clean successes, but small failures as well.

— In the first year, the mist is run too much and people say it feels a little too damp.

— Depending on the wind, mist flows toward shopfronts rather than toward people waiting.

— The floor becomes wetter than expected, so the operating time is shortened.

— The rain garden is slightly misplaced, and another spot becomes muddy.

— Too much grass is left, and neighbors say it looks poorly maintained.

— In the retention-basin open space, the use-restriction notice is unclear, confusing people out for walks.

— The water-temperature chart is difficult to read, and it has to be explained several times in port meetings.

‘Failures are what make it fit the town’s shape.’

“Right.”

These are not large failures.

They are gaps that become visible because the town tried something.

Inconveniences that become clear because people used it.

Questions that return because someone explained it.

They are small misalignments of that kind.

— The mist is run for a slightly shorter time.

— Conditions are added to stop it on windy days.

— The exit of the rain-garden hollow is adjusted.

— A small sign explains which grass is left and which grass is cut.

— The use-restriction line in the open space is redrawn more clearly.

— The water-temperature chart is simplified by reducing colors and narrowing it to the fish species used at that port.

‘Fitting in does not mean matching perfectly from the beginning.’

“It means being slightly misaligned, adjusted little by little, and becoming the town’s standard.”

‘Part 5 Episode 8 is about those adjustments.’

“Right.”

What matters here is not to end each failure with “so it was useless after all.”

If it is too damp, change how it is run.

If the ground becomes muddy, correct the path for water to escape.

If the explanation does not reach people, change the words.

If the water-temperature chart is too difficult, reduce it into a form that fits port conversation.

— Do not treat failure only as a reason to stop.

— Treat failure as material for adjustment.

— When that becomes possible, the received auxiliary wheels begin to fit into the town.

I wrote at the end of the log:

— This is a log of auxiliary wheels that remain as they came from outside.

— This is a log of auxiliary wheels that are reworded into the town’s language and begin to fit in.

## Afterword

Part 5 Episode 8 depicted the theme of how received auxiliary wheels fit the shape of a town.

In Part 5 Episode 7, we organized the receiving town as not merely a visitor, but a town that edits what it receives to match its own conditions.

Episode 8 looked at the process by which those received auxiliary wheels are actually tried inside the town, corrected, reworded, and made into the town’s own.

Three main points became visible in this episode.

### 1. Immediately after being received, auxiliary wheels are still borrowed

A system brought from another town is first treated as something from outside.

A simple diagram of the mist system.

A placement memo for the rain garden.

An operation sheet for the retention basin.

An explanation sheet for the water-temperature chart.

Photographs of shade on park benches.

Even when the documents exist, for the people actually using them, they are still somewhat distant.

How to turn the switch on.

When to stop it.

How to leave grass.

How to check gates and open spaces.

Who reads the water-temperature chart.

As long as such details do not fit the town’s daily life, the auxiliary wheels are still borrowed.

### 2. To fit in means changing into the town’s own words

For an auxiliary wheel to fit in does not mean preserving the original town’s method exactly.

From “it worked well in that town” to “at this station front, this time of day is the right time to run it.”

From “it was used in another watershed” to “for the way rain falls in this town, this size of hollow fits.”

From “a water-temperature chart used in another port” to “in this port’s morning meeting, we look at this fish species first.”

In this way, a principle from outside is translated into the language of the town’s life.

The purpose remains.

The form and the words change.

When the people of that town can explain it in their own words, the auxiliary wheels gradually become the town’s own.

### 3. Before it fits in, failure and adjustment are necessary

Auxiliary wheels do not match perfectly from the beginning.

The mist may be run too much and feel damp.

Wind direction may carry mist away.

The rain garden may make another place muddy.

The way grass is left may look poorly maintained.

Use restrictions for the retention basin may be hard to understand.

The water-temperature chart may be difficult to read.

Such small failures appear.

What matters is not to end them with “so it was useless after all.”

Change how it is run.

Correct the path for water to escape.

Add an explanation sign.

Redraw the line.

Simplify the water-temperature chart so it fits port conversation.

Do not treat failure only as a reason to stop; treat it as material for adjustment.

Through that adjustment, auxiliary wheels fit the shape of the town.
