# Part 5 Episode 4 — Handover papers, “last year’s town,” and “this year’s town”

## Foreword

In Part 5 Episode 3, we focused on the visibility of the roles around who turns the auxiliary wheels, and saw that the more visible the people who turn them are, the more easily those auxiliary wheels become part of the town.

Episode 4 deals with what comes next: handover.

Auxiliary wheels do not continue simply because someone turned them once.

Last year’s way must be handed to this year’s people.

This year’s records must be handed to next year’s people.

Using the thin bundle of papers left in the shopping-street meeting room as a clue, the protagonist and the AI follow how “last year’s town” can be handed to “this year’s town.”

---

“In the end, handover is paper, isn’t it?”

I said that while looking at the pile of notes on the desk.

Mist inspection records.

Rain-garden maintenance notes.

Retention-basin gate check sheets.

Copies of fishing-port water-temperature charts.

None of them is flashy.

They do not look good in photographs.

They do not look like new technology.

There is just paper.

‘Paper is plain, but it remains.’

“It remains. But remaining is not enough.”

‘If it is not read, it is close to not remaining at all.’

“You went straight to the core.”

‘Because this is Part 5.’

“That again.”

I straightened the edges of the paper bundle.

In Part 5 Episode 1, we looked at the conditions needed to keep auxiliary wheels going.

In Episode 2, we looked at inspection and records.

In Episode 3, we looked at the people who turn and support them.

Then, when those people change, what remains?

How does this year’s person receive the town that last year’s person was watching?

“In Episode 4, I want to look at the difference between remaining and being handed over.”

‘A story about handing last year’s town to this year’s town.’

“That’s right.”

## Section 1 — The reality-line town where last year’s way is not handed over

The shopping-street meeting room.

On the shelf by the window, slightly faded files are lined up.

Inside are last year’s inspection records, meeting notes from several years ago, and samples of notices that have already been used.

“They do remain.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— Mist-equipment inspection records.

— Rain-garden maintenance notes.

— Retention-basin check sheets.

— Water-temperature chart update history.

— Park-bench position check notes.

The file spines have plausible names written on them.

But they are only there.

It is not clear who is looking at them.

— The person in charge of the mist is different this year from last year.

— But last year’s file is not opened.

— This year’s person asks the contractor to explain the same things again.

— The nozzle that clogged easily last year, and the reason operations were stopped last year, both have to be found from the beginning.

‘Last year’s town does not overlap with this year’s town as it is.’

“Exactly.”

The same thing happens in the park.

— Last year, it became clear that the bench moved out of the shade at summer noon.

— A proposal was made to shift its position slightly.

— But that note remains buried inside a bundle of meeting materials.

— At this year’s meeting, nobody mentions it.

— As a result, the bench remains in the sun again.

The same thing happens with the rain garden.

— The previous person had written, “Do not mow all of this grass.”

— They had also written that the outlet of the shallow channel where water flows tends to clog with fallen leaves.

— But that note remains only in a desk drawer and is not handed to this year’s maintenance person.

— This year’s person mows the grass too neatly.

— The rain garden returns, a little, to an ordinary garden.

The same thing happens with the retention basin.

— The gate check sheet exists.

— But it sleeps on the shelf as an old version.

— The changed water-level guide and the changed contact address have not been reflected.

— When someone tries to check it, they cannot tell which version is latest.

‘So this is a world line where paper exists, but it has not been handed over.’

“Yeah.”

It is not that there is no paper.

It is not that there are no records.

On the contrary, a fair amount remains on the shelves.

But it has not reached this year’s people.

It has not become this year’s procedure.

It has not been reconnected with the site.

— This year’s people do not know what last year’s people looked at or where they struggled.

— Last year’s failures and last year’s ideas do not become this year’s first step.

— So the same failures and the same detours are repeated again.

‘A town where records sleep.’

“I don’t like the phrase, but it fits.”

## Section 2 — The slightly bent world-line town where paper is handed over

‘Then let us look at handover in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI said that.

I opened the right-hand log.

And first, I wrote one line.

— From here on, this is a world line of a town where last year’s papers are properly handed to this year’s people.

The same meeting room.

The same shelf.

Similar files.

But in this world line, the files are not merely placed there.

“For example, there is a system like this.”

— At the end of spring, people from the shopping street, neighborhood association, park management, watershed team, and port office gather once.

— Last year’s mist inspection record, rain-garden maintenance notes, retention-basin check sheet, water-temperature chart update history, and park-bench shade check notes are spread out on the table.

— Each document has one red sticky note attached.

‘What is written on the sticky notes?’

“‘The first thing to check this year.’”

— For the mist, the number of the nozzle that clogged easily last year.

— For the park, the bench that moved out of shade at summer noon.

— For the rain garden, the line beyond which grass should not be cut too much.

— For the retention basin, the water-level guide for opening the gate and this year’s contact address.

— For the fishing port, the day of the week on which the water-temperature chart is updated, and notes on the forecast that missed last year.

‘It is not only the paper itself. It shows where to look first.’

“Right.”

If the documents are too thick, they are not read.

If they are too detailed, they are not handed over.

So at least the first place to look is made visible with a red sticky note.

“‘Read everything’ does not last.”

‘Just knowing where to look first makes a difference.’

“Yeah.”

In this town, people always walk the site once on handover day.

— In the shopping street, they go under the mist nozzles.

— This year’s person actually sees the place that clogged easily last year.

— In the park, they check the shade over the bench.

— In the rain garden, they look at the shallow depression, the direction of drainage, and the band of grass to leave.

— At the retention basin, they look at the gate, the edge of the open ground, and the place where access-restriction signs will be placed.

— At the fishing port, they look at the water-temperature chart in the office and the flow of boxes lined up on the quay.

‘The paper is reconnected to the site.’

“Exactly.”

Paper alone does not hand over the town.

The site alone does not easily preserve last year’s memory.

Paper and the site are seen on the same day.

Only then does last year’s town overlap a little with this year’s town.

— This year’s person reads last year’s paper.

— With last year’s paper in hand, they walk this year’s site.

— On the spot, they confirm one by one, “We should watch this again this year.”

— Where they do not understand something, they ask the predecessor or the responsible department.

— Old information is crossed out in red and updated.

‘Handover is not only preservation. It is also updating.’

“That’s right.”

There is no need to worship last year’s paper as it is.

Weather, people, and equipment conditions all change between last year and this year.

So last year’s paper is the starting point for this year’s judgment, not the answer itself.

— Look at last year’s record.

— Look at this year’s site.

— Correct the parts that are different.

— Change it into a form this year’s people can use.

That is what handover means in this world line.

## Section 3 — The protagonist’s words after comparing the two handovers

I placed the two meeting rooms side by side in my mind.

The left-hand meeting room.

There is paper.

There are files.

There are notes.

But the handover is thin.

Last year’s town left on the shelf and the town trying to move this year stand side by side without overlapping.

The right-hand meeting room.

There is paper.

There are sticky notes.

There are update dates.

There is time to walk the site.

There is someone to ask when something is unclear.

Last year’s town is handed, a little, to this year’s town.

‘How are they different?’

The AI asked.

“In the left-hand town, documents remaining and documents being handed over are different things.”

I said that while straightening the bundle of notes with my fingers.

“There is paper.”

‘Yes.’

“But it is not used.”

‘Yes.’

“Because it is not used, this year’s people begin from zero again.”

‘And on the right?’

“On the right, paper remaining directly becomes the first step of this year.”

— Documents become the entrance to the next year.

— Last year’s worries become this year’s check items.

— Last year’s failures become this year’s procedures.

— Last year’s town appears once more in the eyes of this year’s people.

‘So the continuity of an auxiliary wheel is decided by the thickness of paper and the time spent walking.’

“Not the thickness of paper alone.”

‘Then?’

“Usable paper and time spent walking.”

‘I see.’

— An auxiliary wheel is not an event that ends in one year.

— Whether last year’s way is handed to this year’s hands changes how that town’s future bends.

— Having paper is not enough.

— Only when paper is opened, sticky notes are read, the site is walked, and the information is revised into this year’s procedure does a record become continuity.

‘This episode shows the work of changing records into continuity.’

“I want to write that clearly in Part 5 Episode 4.”

I wrote at the end of the two logs.

— This is the log of a town where last year’s papers sleep on the shelf and are not handed to this year’s people.

— This is the log of a town where last year’s papers are handed to this year’s people and become usable records through a walk around the site.

“I want to write the latter as the better auxiliary wheel.”

‘Handover may be plain, but it might be one of the most effective auxiliary wheels.’

“Maybe so.”

I looked again at the paper bundle.

Thin paper.

Red sticky notes.

Dates.

Names of people in charge.

Replaced parts.

Grass that was not cut.

A gate that was not opened.

A water-temperature forecast that missed.

Each of them is small.

But each tells this year’s town what last year’s town was looking at.

“Last year’s town does not simply disappear.”

‘It depends on how it is left.’

“If this year’s people open it, it comes back a little.”

‘And if this year’s people add to it, it goes to next year.’

“I see.”

At the end of Part 5 Episode 4, I wrote one line.

— An auxiliary wheel begins turning again when last year’s paper reaches this year’s hands.

Part 5 Episode 4 ends here.

## Afterword

In Part 5 Episode 4, we used “handover papers” to draw the difference between last year’s town and this year’s town.

Part 5 Episode 3 dealt with the visibility of the roles around who turns the auxiliary wheels.

Episode 4 looks at what is needed when those roles are handed from one person to another.

The main points that appeared in this episode are three.

1. Paper remaining and paper being handed over are different things
   Even if files and notes are on the shelf, they do not connect to the next year unless they are seen.

Mist inspection records.

Rain-garden maintenance notes.

Retention-basin gate check sheets.

Copies of fishing-port water-temperature charts.

Park-bench shade check notes.

Such documents do not support the continuation of auxiliary wheels merely by remaining.

What the predecessor saw, where they struggled, what they repaired, and what they stopped.

Only when those become this year’s first step does a record become handover.

2. Handover needs not only documents, but a walk around the site
   In the world line that bent slightly, at the end of spring, people from the shopping street, neighborhood association, park management, watershed team, and port office gather once.

They spread last year’s documents across the table and attach red sticky notes to each of them.

The sticky notes say, “The first thing to check this year.”

For the mist, the nozzle that clogged easily last year.

For the park, the bench that moved out of shade.

For the rain garden, the line beyond which grass should not be cut too much.

For the retention basin, the gate-opening guide and this year’s contact address.

For the fishing port, the day on which the water-temperature chart is updated and notes on last year’s missed forecast.

Then this year’s people actually walk the site.

Paper alone is not enough to understand.

The site alone does not preserve last year’s memory.

By reconnecting paper and site, last year’s town overlaps a little with this year’s town.

3. Records become continuity only when they are updated
   Last year’s paper is not the answer itself.

This year’s weather, equipment condition, people, contact addresses, and use patterns differ from last year’s.

So handover does not mean preserving last year’s paper unchanged.

Look at last year’s record.

Look at this year’s site.

Correct what is different.

Change it into a form this year’s people can use.

That is the work of changing records into continuity.
