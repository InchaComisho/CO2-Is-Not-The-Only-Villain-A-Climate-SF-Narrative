# Part 5 Episode 5 — The town that leaves learning behind, and auxiliary wheels that keep the next person from getting lost

## Foreword

In Part 5 Episode 4, we connected last year’s town to this year’s town through “handover papers.”

Episode 5 depicts what comes after that: how to leave learning behind.

Even if an auxiliary wheel turns for one year, if it stops there, its meaning is thin.

The next person must be able to use it without getting lost.

It must be adaptable even when conditions are slightly different.

Inside the town, people must be able to share the sense that “this is one way to look at it.”

Only when it reaches that far does an auxiliary wheel begin to approach a habit.

Using one memo posted on the wall of a shopping-street meeting room as a clue, the protagonist and the AI think about “auxiliary wheels that keep people from getting lost.”

---

“Even if the handover is done, that isn’t the end.”

I said that while looking at the paper posted on the wall of the meeting room.

Last year’s mist inspection sheet.

Rain-garden maintenance notes.

Retention-basin check sheet.

The fishing port’s water-temperature chart.

The park-bench shade check memo.

Those had been handed to this year’s people in Episode 4.

But just because they had been handed over did not mean they became answers as they were.

‘Handover is preparation so the next person does not get lost.’

“Right.”

‘But procedures alone are not enough to keep people from getting lost.’

“You need reasons.”

I looked at the paper on the wall.

In thick letters, it said this:

— Do not use last year’s answer as it is.  
— Use last year’s way of thinking for this year’s judgment.

“This is plain, but important.”

‘It is very Part 5 paper.’

“That again?”

‘This time, it really is very Part 5.’

“Well, I won’t deny it.”

## Section 1 — Last year’s town does not become this year’s answer

The meeting room has last year’s materials laid out.

The number of days the mist was operated.

The nozzles that clogged easily.

Rain-garden mowing notes.

Retention-basin gate check sheets.

The fishing port’s water-temperature chart.

Photographs of the park-bench shade.

“If you look only at the materials, they’re actually pretty solid.”

‘But this year’s town is not the same as last year’s town.’

“That’s the issue.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— This year’s temperature is not the same as last year’s.

— The way rain falls is not the same as last year’s.

— The trees in the park have grown their branches a little more than last year.

— The soil in the rain garden is a little more compacted than last year.

— The grass around the retention basin is a little more likely to dry under this summer’s heat.

— The way fish gather at the fishing port cannot be read from last year’s water-temperature chart alone.

Last year’s method can serve as a reference.

But it is not this year’s answer itself.

“This is where it gets difficult.”

— The mist inspection does not necessarily have to be on the same day as last year.

— A nozzle that did not clog last year may clog this year.

— The rain-garden grass does not necessarily have to be cut to the same height as last year.

— The retention-basin check sheet also needs updated contact information and water-level information.

— If the fishing port does not look at the forecast that failed last year, it may get lost in the same way this year.

‘Last year’s town can be a reference, but it is not the answer.’

“Right.”

There is paper.

There is handover.

Even so, this year’s people may still be troubled.

They followed last year’s procedure, but it did not quite fit.

It worked last year, but this year it is slightly different.

At that moment, a mere procedure manual does not tell them the next move.

— Why was the inspection done on that day?

— Why was that grass left standing?

— Why was that water level used as the benchmark?

— Why did they decide not to target that fish?

— Why did they consider moving that bench?

If that much is not left behind, this year’s people get lost.

‘That is why it needs to be left as learning.’

“Yeah.”

— Not merely records, but how people thought.

— What they looked at when they decided.

— Where they hesitated.

— Under what conditions it is acceptable to change.

— Under what conditions it is better to stop.

By leaving those things behind, records become learning.

## Section 2 — Auxiliary wheels that remain as learning

‘Then let us look at auxiliary wheels that remain as learning.’

The AI said.

I opened the right-side log.

Then I wrote just one line at the beginning.

— From here on, this is the world line of a town that can turn last year’s answer into this year’s way of thinking.

The same shopping street.

The same meeting room.

The same materials.

In the same way, this year is different from last year.

But in this world line, next to the records, the reason “why it was done that way” remains.

“For example, with the shopping-street mist.”

— Next to the inspection sheet, it says why this nozzle tends to clog.

— A place that is easily affected by wind direction.

— A terminal end where water pressure tends to weaken.

— A place where the floor became too wet last year and the mist was stopped once.

— Those reasons are left in short notes.

‘This year’s people can look not only at the same place, but also at places with similar conditions.’

“Right.”

If it only says “check nozzle number three,” they will look only at number three.

But if it says, “the mist became uneven in a place where the wind swirls,” this year’s people can look at other places too.

They can search for whether the same problem is appearing somewhere else.

That is learning.

For the park, it becomes like this.

— Photographs of the bench are lined up by season.

— A spring morning.

— Summer noon.

— Two o’clock on a summer afternoon.

— An autumn evening.

— Beside them, a short note says, “At two o’clock on a summer afternoon, the shade misses the bench.”

‘With photographs, the movement of shade becomes visible.’

“Yeah.”

Last year’s people hesitated over whether to move the bench.

This year’s people look at those photographs.

Then they think, “This year the tree branches have grown, so maybe the shade has returned a little.”

Or they notice, “Because the tree was pruned, there is less shade than last year.”

— Rather than using last year’s conclusion as it is, they apply last year’s way of seeing to this year.

For the rain garden, it is like this.

— It does not simply say, “Do not cut all the grass.”

— It adds the reason: “Leaving grass slightly weakens the force of raindrops striking the soil directly.”

— It also states an exception: “However, cut grass that blocks the water outlet.”

— It gives a judgment guide: “If water remains for two days or longer, check for clogging.”

‘When reasons and exceptions are present, the next person can move more easily.’

“That’s exactly it.”

If it only says “do not cut,” this year’s people will be troubled.

How much should be left?

What is all right to cut?

What should be done if too much water remains?

They do not know.

But if reasons and exceptions are there, they can think for themselves.

For the retention basin, it becomes like this.

— Not only the benchmark “open the gate at this water level,” but the basis for that judgment is left behind.

— Upstream rainfall.

— Midstream water level.

— How the retention basin was being used at the time.

— The time needed for recovery after the water receded.

— Records show why that decision was made by looking at those factors.

‘It is not decided mechanically by water level alone.’

“Right.”

A benchmark is necessary.

But sometimes the benchmark alone is not enough.

So the basis for judgment is left.

That way, the next year’s people can judge a little how to think if the same water level comes with different conditions.

For the fishing port, it is like this.

— There is a memo for port conversations: “In this water-temperature band, this fish tends to move.”

— But it does not say, “You will definitely catch it.”

— It also says, “Last year, the forecast failed in this water-temperature band.”

— It adds words connecting experience and observation: “Look together with wind direction and tidal movement.”

‘So it leaves not only a procedure, but why to do it.’

“Right.”

In this world line, the memo is not left only inside one person’s head.

— On the wall of the shopping-street meeting room.

— Beside the workbench used by park management.

— Inside the rain-garden maintenance box.

— At the retention-basin station.

— On the bulletin board in the fishing-port office.

It is placed somewhere visible.

When the next person hesitates, it is somewhere they can quickly pick up.

‘That is an auxiliary wheel that remains as learning.’

“Yeah.”

Auxiliary wheels do not continue through devices alone.

Records alone are still not enough.

When knowledge becomes procedure,

when reasons are added to procedure,

and when those reasons become the town’s learning,

an auxiliary wheel finally becomes something the next person can use.

## Section 3 — The protagonist’s comment after comparing the two towns

I placed the two towns side by side.

The town on the left.

Last year’s method remains as it is.

There are materials.

There is handover.

But it does not fit this year, where the conditions are different.

Only the words “we did it this way last year” remain, while “why it was done that way” is thin.

There are records, but they have not become learning.

The town on the right.

Last year’s method remains together with “why it was done that way.”

This year’s people can look at it and adjust a little by themselves.

They can use not last year’s answer, but last year’s way of thinking.

‘How are they different?’

The AI asked.

“In the town on the left, records stop at being something to preserve.”

I chose my words slowly.

“In the town on the right, records become something that keeps people from getting lost.”

‘In other words, whether the auxiliary wheels become the town’s learning.’

“Right.”

— Last year’s town cannot be reproduced as it was.

— The same summer as last year will not come.

— The same rain as last year will not come.

— The same sea as last year will not come.

— But what happened in last year’s town can be turned into learning for this year’s town.

‘That is the core of Part 5 Episode 5.’

“Right.”

I wrote at the end of the two logs.

— This is the log of a town where only records remain.

— This is the log of a town where auxiliary wheels remain as learning.

“I want to write the latter as the better auxiliary wheel.”

‘Not getting lost is quite important.’

“It is.”

Not getting lost does not mean not having to think.

It is the opposite.

It means having an entrance into thinking.

It means having a reason one can return to.

It means knowing what to look at when there is an exception.

“An auxiliary wheel that keeps people from getting lost is not an auxiliary wheel that removes thinking.”

‘It is an auxiliary wheel that carries people to the place where they can think.’

“That’s it.”

I wrote those words down.

— An auxiliary wheel that keeps people from getting lost is not a system that removes the need to think.  
— It is a system that carries people to the place where they can think.

## Section 4 — The protagonist’s decision

‘What do you want to leave behind?’

The AI asked.

“I want to leave something that keeps the next person from getting lost.”

I answered while looking at the paper on the wall.

“Not only procedures.”

“Reasons too.”

“Seasonal differences too.”

“Judgment when there are exceptions too.”

“Records of failures too.”

‘Then the auxiliary wheels become a little wiser every year.’

“That is what I would like.”

— An auxiliary wheel should become more suitable for the town in year two than in year one.

— In year three than in year two.

— In year four than in year three.

— It should change little by little into a form that fits the town.

For that, records must be turned into learning.

Simply preserving last year’s answer is not enough.

Last year’s way of thinking must be left in a form that can be used for this year’s judgment.

‘Part 5 Episode 5 depicts the entrance to that.’

“Yeah.”

Across Part 5 so far, we have gradually accumulated several things.

— The people who turn the auxiliary wheels become visible.

— They are inspected.

— They are recorded.

— They are handed over.

— They remain as learning.

‘The conditions for auxiliary wheels to continue are gradually becoming visible.’

“Right.”

I looked once more at the paper posted on the wall of the meeting room.

— Do not use last year’s answer as it is.  
— Use last year’s way of thinking for this year’s judgment.

Below it, this year’s person in charge had written a small addition.

— When you do not understand, look at the reason.  
— If the reason is old, add this year’s reason.

“That’s good.”

‘The learning is being updated.’

“It feels like the auxiliary wheel has become the town’s thing a little more.”

I wrote one final line for Part 5 Episode 5.

— When records become learning, auxiliary wheels make the next person less likely to get lost.

Part 5 Episode 5 ends here.

## Afterword

In Part 5 Episode 5, I depicted the difference between last year’s town and this year’s town through the theme of “auxiliary wheels that remain as learning.”

In Part 5 Episode 4, handover papers carried last year’s town into this year’s town.

However, the fact that last year’s records were handed to this year’s people is not yet enough.

This year’s temperature, rainfall, tree growth, soil compaction, and fish movement are not exactly the same as last year’s.

The main points that emerged in this episode are these three.

1. Last year’s method does not become this year’s answer as it is  
   Last year’s records are important.

But they are not this year’s answer itself.

The mist inspection date.

The shade on the park bench.

How much grass to leave in the rain garden.

The retention-basin gate decision.

The fishing port’s water-temperature chart.

None of them can necessarily be used under exactly the same conditions as last year.

That is why it is necessary to leave not only the procedure “we did this last year,” but also “why it was done,” “what was looked at when deciding,” and “where people hesitated.”

Records become usable for the next year when they are left not as answers, but as ways of thinking.

2. Auxiliary wheels continue more easily when they become learning  
   In the world line that bent slightly, the records of auxiliary wheels include reasons and exceptions.

For mist, why that nozzle tends to clog.

For the park, why the bench loses shade at that hour.

For the rain garden, why not all the grass is cut.

For the retention basin, why that water level was used as a benchmark.

For the fishing port, why judgment was changed in that water-temperature band.

And also, under what conditions a different judgment should be made.

When such reasons and exceptions remain, the next person does not simply copy last year’s answer; they can think according to this year’s conditions.

By changing from records into learning, auxiliary wheels become something the next person can use.

3. The axis of Part 5 is “not getting lost”  
   This episode dealt with the idea of “auxiliary wheels that keep people from getting lost.”

However, not getting lost does not mean not having to think.

It means having an entrance into thinking.

It means having a reason one can return to.

It means knowing what to look at when there is an exception.

It means being able to move failure into the next judgment.

That is what “not getting lost” means.

An auxiliary wheel that keeps people from getting lost is not a system that removes the need to think.

It is a system that carries people to the place where they can think.

In Part 5, we are looking one by one at the conditions needed for auxiliary wheels to continue.

Episode 1 looked at how quickly auxiliary wheels are forgotten.

Episode 2 looked at the morning of maintenance inspection.

Episode 3 looked at the roles around who turns them.

Episode 4 looked at handover papers.

And Episode 5 looked at turning those records into learning.

When records become learning, auxiliary wheels make the next person less likely to get lost.

That sentence is the center of Part 5 Episode 5.
