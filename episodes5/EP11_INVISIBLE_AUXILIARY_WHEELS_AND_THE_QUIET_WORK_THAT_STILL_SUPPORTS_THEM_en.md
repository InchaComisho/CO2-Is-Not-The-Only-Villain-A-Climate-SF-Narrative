# Part 5 Episode 11 — Invisible auxiliary wheels, and the quiet work that still supports them

## Foreword

In Part 5 Episode 10, we depicted the importance of putting the reason to continue back into words even after auxiliary wheels have become ordinary.

Episode 11 deals with what comes after that: becoming hard to see.

After auxiliary wheels become ordinary, they become even less visible.

But becoming invisible does not mean they are no longer working.

Some things become hard to see precisely because they have dissolved into daily life.

As the protagonist and the AI walk through a station front and shopping street on a summer evening, they look again at “things that work even though they cannot be seen,” and at the quiet work that supports them.

---

“After something becomes ordinary, the next step is that it becomes invisible.”

I said that while watching the mist in front of the station in the evening.

It is normal for it to be there.

So no one pays attention.

But just because people no longer pay attention does not mean it is not working.

The mist is coming out today too.

Someone has checked whether the wind is too strong.

Someone has adjusted it so the floor does not become too wet.

On days when humidity is too high, someone also decides to stop it.

But people passing through cannot see that far.

‘It works, even though it is invisible.’

“Right.”

It was a slightly strange state.

It was not a conspicuous device.

It was not applauded as a major achievement.

Yet by being there, it was reducing someone’s burden just a little.

“I want Episode 11 of Part 5 to look at that.”

‘Invisible auxiliary wheels, and the work that still supports them.’

“Yeah.”

## Section 1 — The station front that became invisible

The station-front mist is quietly running again today.

Even with a heatstroke alert issued, no one says, “Amazing.”

The people walking under the mist are simply finding it a little easier to walk.

“Things that are just right are hard to see.”

I looked across the station front in the evening.

— Someone did not collapse.

— Waiting for the bus became a little easier.

— Someone coming back from shopping could walk a little more slowly.

— Someone passing through the shopping street could keep walking a little longer.

— A parent with a child could stop once in a place with shade and mist.

Those differences are hard to show in numbers.

They are unlikely to become news.

They may not remain as anyone’s words of thanks.

‘But they are working.’

“Yeah.”

Even the word “working” needs care.

It is not eliminating the heat.

It is not completely preventing heatstroke.

It is not guaranteeing safety in the shopping street.

It is only making the burden a little lighter amid dangerous heat.

It is increasing the number of minutes someone can stand.

It is creating an opportunity to rest before someone pushes too far.

“This is where the ‘bending things just a little’ we’ve been talking about since Part 4 appears, isn’t it?”

‘It is not flashy, but the longer it continues, the more important it becomes.’

“Yeah.”

The park benches are the same.

— A bench was moved slightly to a place where its shade is less likely to slip away in the afternoon.

— Now no one thinks of it as a special device.

— They simply sit there.

— They sit there and rest a little.

The rain garden is the same.

— Some grass remains.

— Water enters it once, then drains away slowly.

— But to a passerby, it may look like nothing more than a grassy hollow.

The retention basin is the same.

— There are more years when no water enters it than years when it does.

— Even so, check sheets are updated, signs are corrected, and entry-restriction procedures are reviewed.

— Not being used does not prove that it has no meaning.

The port’s water-temperature chart is the same.

— Someone looks at the chart in the morning meeting.

— They shift departure a little.

— They adjust ice preparation a little.

— They contact the market a little earlier.

— Even if that does not appear as a major achievement, it reduces hesitation in judgment just a little.

‘All of them are things that helped because they existed, yet the fact that they existed is easily forgotten.’

“That’s exactly it.”

Auxiliary wheels that have become invisible are weak.

They are weak because it becomes harder to explain why they are necessary.

But at the same time, they are strong.

They are strong because they have dissolved into daily life.

“That contradiction is at the center of Episode 11.”

‘The more invisible something becomes, the more easily it loses the reasons that support it.’

“But the more invisible something becomes, the more it actually needs to be supported.”

## Section 2 — The quiet work that supports invisible things

‘Then let’s look at the work that supports invisible things.’

The AI said.

I opened the log on the right side.

Then I wrote one line first.

— From here, this is the world line of a town that continues supporting auxiliary wheels that have become invisible.

In the shopping street, there is someone who inspects the mist system.

At a time no one notices, they check the nozzles one by one.

They check how the water comes out.

They check for clogging.

They look at how wet the floor becomes.

They check places where the wind tends to swirl.

They compare last year’s records with this year’s conditions.

— When the mist is running, people passing through only feel the coolness.

— But before that, someone has looked.

— After that, someone will record it.

In the park, there is someone who slightly adjusts the position of a bench.

They look at the shift in shade and make the place a little easier to sit before summer.

Have the trees grown?

Has pruning reduced the shade?

At what time in the afternoon does the sunlight become stronger?

Has the flow of people who actually sit there changed?

They look at those things and adjust it just a little.

In the rain garden, there is someone who maintains it without cutting all the grass.

It may look a little rough.

But that “not cutting too much” helps water pass through.

— There is grass to leave.

— There is grass to cut.

— There is grass to pull out.

— Only the water outlet must not be blocked.

— Fallen leaves are not removed too much, but places that clog are cleared.

That judgment cannot be understood at a glance.

But the auxiliary wheel’s work is there.

At the retention basin, the days when the gate opens are not the main part; the days when it does not open are far more numerous.

There may be years when no water enters it at all.

Even so, records are updated.

Signs are checked.

Contact information is corrected.

Water-level guides are reviewed.

They look to see whether the way the open space is used has changed.

They prepare for water that may come someday.

‘Work for the days when it does not open.’

“That’s really important.”

At the port, there is someone who reads the water-temperature chart.

Even in years when fish movement is hard to see, they keep updating the chart.

They record days when the forecast missed.

They record days when experience and data matched.

Wind direction, tides, fishing grounds, ice preparation, market movement.

They gradually return those things to the morning conversation.

‘None of these jobs stands in the visible place.’

“Right.”

What stands out is the moment the mist comes out.

The bench someone sits on.

The retention basin when water enters it.

The port on a day when fish are caught.

But what truly supports the auxiliary wheels is the quiet work before and after those moments.

Inspection.

Confirmation.

Recording.

Cleaning.

Correction.

Explanation.

Updating.

Handover.

“Everything we’ve seen in Part 5 returns here.”

‘From the morning of maintenance inspection to the evening of invisible continuation.’

“That’s good.”

‘Very Part 5.’

“I’m not surprised anymore.”

## Section 3 — The reason to continue even when it cannot be seen

“This is the important part.”

I slowed my pace slightly as I spoke.

‘What is?’

“Even when it becomes invisible, there has to be a reason to continue.”

After auxiliary wheels stop standing out, that is when the real work begins.

Being invisible does not mean they are not working.

Rather, it can be proof that they have dissolved into daily life.

But if they become too invisible, they become easy to stop.

— Why is a budget necessary?

— Why are people necessary?

— Why is inspection necessary?

— Why do records need to be kept?

— Why must a retention basin be checked even in a year when no water entered it?

— Why must the water-temperature chart be updated even in a year when predictions missed?

When those things can no longer be explained, auxiliary wheels get cut away.

‘If something becomes too invisible, it becomes easy to stop.’

“Exactly.”

That is why there are people who continue quiet work.

They keep records.

They hand them to the next year.

They correct things little by little.

They restate the reason.

Even in years when something was not used, they check the preparation.

Even in years when the prediction missed, they leave the reason it missed.

They repeat that in hours when no one sees them.

‘Supporting invisible things is almost a habit.’

“Yeah.”

It is not work that is praised loudly.

It is not work whose results are always clear.

In fact, many days end with nothing happening.

— The mist stopped without a problem.

— The bench could be used again this year.

— The rain garden quietly received water.

— The retention basin did not need to be used again this year.

— The water-temperature chart reduced hesitation a little.

That is the kind of work it is.

“Work that supports nothing happening is hard to see.”

‘That is why it has to be put into words.’

“That returns us to Episode 10.”

‘Episode 10 restated the reason for the ordinary. Episode 11 puts invisible work into words.’

“They connect.”

— Auxiliary wheels do not need to look dramatic.

— But whether people continue working quietly after they become invisible changes the way a town’s summer breaks.

— It changes the way the town lives with the river.

— It changes the way rain is received.

— It changes the way the sea is read.

‘It bends the direction and pace of breaking, just a little.’

“So we return there again.”
