# Episodes 5 / Part 5 — English Guide

**Shosetsuka ni Naro series index:** [Full series](https://ncode.syosetu.com/s5141k/)  
**Repository language guide:** [Japanese index](README.md) | English | [Arabic guide](README_ar.md)

Part 5 shifts from the auxiliary wheels that reached places in Part 4 to the question of **how to continue, widen, and preserve them**.

The auxiliary wheels described in Part 4 do not end at the moment of introduction.

Station-front mist, shopping-street assumption infrastructure, park benches, community-center signs, balcony knowledge, upstream grass bands, rain gardens, retention basins, riverside water-level information, and fishing-port water-temperature charts all need budgets, people, institutions, records, inspection, explanation, and handover.

Without those conditions, they will eventually be forgotten, stop working, or disappear.

Part 5 follows the lifespan and updating of auxiliary wheels.

```text
How can an introduced auxiliary wheel be maintained?
How can the speed of forgetting be resisted?
Who inspects it, who pays for it, and who updates the records?
How can what has reached a place be handed to the next person?
```

## Part 5 Concept

Part 5 begins after the comparison of two world lines in Part 4.

The question is no longer only whether auxiliary wheels can reach a place.

The question becomes whether they can remain there.

- **Budgets:** who pays for continued operation, repair, and update.
- **People:** who inspects, explains, cleans, records, and hands over.
- **Institutions:** whether a one-time measure becomes a repeatable procedure.
- **Records:** whether the reason for the auxiliary wheel survives staff changes and quiet years.
- **Forgetting:** how quickly “it was useful” turns into “it is just there.”
