# Part 7 Episode 1 — Where do auxiliary wheels become a “world story”?

## Foreword

In Part 6, we placed the world’s “red graphs” — CO₂ concentration, emissions, temperature, sea-surface temperature, El Niño, and marine heat waves — on the same screen as the auxiliary wheels that remain in our own town, watershed, and port.

The world’s graphs are red.

Regional auxiliary wheels are small.

Still, bending the way a region breaks, even a little, has meaning.

In Part 7, we look at where those regional auxiliary wheels connect to the “world story.”

Sea and port.

River and town.

City and watershed.

Emission reduction and auxiliary wheels.

Institutions and everyday life.

This is the entrance to Part 7, where the protagonist and the AI begin thinking about what is needed so that a town’s ingenuity does not end as only a town’s story.

— Connecting points.

I made a list of all the auxiliary wheels so far.

Station-front mist.

Assumption-checking mist in the shopping street.

Shade in the park.

A place to rest at the community center.

Ways to release balcony heat.

Upstream sponge-like soil.

Midstream rain gardens.

Downstream retention basins.

Tidal flats and reed beds at the river mouth.

Water-temperature charts at the fishing port.

“When I look at it this way, we’ve done quite a lot.”

“We have placed auxiliary wheels in the town, the watershed, and the port.”

“But in the end, this is still ‘our town’s story,’ isn’t it?”

“Yes.”

“Even if mist comes out at our station front, global CO₂ concentration does not fall.”

“It does not.”

“Even if our rain garden receives water, El Niño does not disappear.”

“It does not.”

“Even if our port reads the water-temperature chart, global sea-surface temperature does not return to normal.”

“It does not.”

“Every time I say it, I feel a little deflated.”

“However.”

“There it is.”

“Whether a town’s auxiliary wheels end inside the town depends on how they connect to the outside of the town.”

“The outside of the town.”

“The next town. The watershed. Port networks. Institutions. Budgets. Work. Learning.”

“The theme of Part 7 suddenly multiplied.”

“There are many connecting points.”

“So Part 7 is not about placing auxiliary wheels, but about passing the auxiliary wheels we placed outward.”

“That understanding is fine.”

“Passing them outward.”

I wrote that phrase in my notes.

## Section 2 — Places where auxiliary wheels stop

“Then, conversely, where do auxiliary wheels stop?”

The AI displayed several small walls on the screen.

Wall five.

Station-front mist can easily stop when the responsible party shifts between the shopping street and the local government.

Rain gardens can easily stop when roads, parks, and residential sides are handled separately.

Retention basins can look like “unused land” in ordinary times and become easy targets for budget cuts.

Water-temperature charts at a fishing port can easily become just paper if the people observing, the people using them, and the people explaining them are all separate.

“Auxiliary wheels tend to stop at human walls more than at natural ones.”

“Nature does not know town borders. Institutions know town borders and responsibilities.”

“This continues from Part 6 Episode 10.”

“Not break them?”

“Translated.”

“Town words into institutional words. On-site difficulties into budget items. Observation data into work decisions.”

“As usual.”

## Section 3 — Who makes the port’s water-temperature chart into something usable?

“Let’s start with the port.”

“That is a good entrance.”

The screen showed a fishing port morning.

One water-temperature chart.

The wall of the port office.

A gathering of fishers.

Market people.

Processing plant people.

The local government’s fisheries staff.

Observation data from a research institution.

“This single chart is related to everyone.”

“But the way it is related differs.”

For fishers, it is a chart for deciding where to go.

For the market, it is a chart for anticipating what may come in, and how much.

For processing plants, it is a chart for changing ice and storage preparations.

For the local government, it is information for supporting fisheries and port life.

For researchers, it is data recording changes in the sea.

“Everyone is looking at the same chart, but they are seeing different things.”

“That is why, unless it connects, it ends as just a chart.”

“And if it connects?”

“The water-temperature chart becomes an auxiliary wheel that moves the port’s whole work slightly ahead.”

“Not only an auxiliary wheel for fishers, but one for the whole port.”

“Yes.”

A fisher says, “Today I’ll look at this sea area.”

The market prepares, thinking, “This fish may be more abundant.”

A processing plant decides, “We’ll place a little more ice.”

The local government tells the region, “The sea is changing like this this year.”

Researchers bring observation results closer to the port’s language.

“Sea data crosses between work and everyday life.”

“This is the first form in which an auxiliary wheel becomes a ‘world story.’”

“It does not end with looking at global sea-surface temperature.”

“It is translated into the port’s work.”

“And it does not end with the port’s work.”

“It connects to markets, processing, everyday life, and institutions.”

“If data is not translated, it remains just a red map.”

“Exactly.”

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Part 7 Episode 1.

In this episode, we reconsidered the regional auxiliary wheels seen up through Part 6 from the perspective of where they connect to the world story.

Against large graphs such as global CO₂ concentration, emissions, temperature, and sea-surface temperature, town mist, rain gardens, retention basins, and water-temperature charts look small.

But small does not mean meaningless. By crossing town borders, watersheds, ports, institutions, and work, regional ingenuity gradually changes into wider learning.

This episode focused mainly on three points.

1. Auxiliary wheels often stop at institutional walls more than natural ones  
   Town borders, budget years, responsible departments, the time gap between work and everyday life, and the phrase “There is no precedent.”

Mist, rain gardens, retention basins, and water-temperature charts do not continue through natural mechanisms alone. They easily stop at the boundaries of human institutions and roles.

Nature does not know town borders.

Institutions know town borders and responsibilities.

In Part 7, we will look at how to cross and connect those walls.

2. Auxiliary wheels become stronger when they connect to work and everyday life  
   A water-temperature chart at a fishing port is not information only for fishers.

When the market, processing plants, the port, the local government, and those who observe the sea use it according to their own work, it becomes an auxiliary wheel that moves the whole port slightly ahead.

A rain garden, too, does not end as one garden. When its principle is translated into schools, parks, residential areas, and shopping streets, it moves closer to becoming a watershed auxiliary wheel.

What matters is not copying the same equipment exactly, but translating the ideas of “why it is needed” and “what it slightly changes” into each place.

3. A small auxiliary wheel becomes a world story when it passes to another place  
   A world story does not mean only making a grand declaration at a world conference.

When the “why” behind an auxiliary wheel becomes a word that can be used by the neighboring town, another watershed, or another port, and when each place rebuilds it under its own conditions, a town’s ingenuity becomes wider learning and then words moving toward the world.

If auxiliary wheels stop inside a town, they become “a town’s ingenuity.”

If they pass outside the town, they become “watershed learning.”

If they are translated in another region, they become “words that move toward the world.”

Part 7 Episode 1 began looking at the connecting points — institutions, work, everyday life, and learning — between the world’s red graphs and a town’s small auxiliary wheels.

Auxiliary wheels become a little bit of a world story when they pass outside the town.

That line is the center of Part 7 Episode 1.
