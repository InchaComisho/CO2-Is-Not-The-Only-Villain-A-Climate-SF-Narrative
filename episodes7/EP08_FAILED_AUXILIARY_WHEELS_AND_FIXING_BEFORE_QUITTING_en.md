## Afterword

Thank you for reading Part 7 Episode 8.

This episode focused on what happens when an auxiliary wheel fails: do we stop immediately, or do we fix it?

Mist, rain gardens, retention basins, and water-temperature charts do not fit a town perfectly from the beginning.

Wind direction, water flow, insufficient explanation, and changing seas can all cause small failures.

The main points that appeared in this episode are three.

### 1. Do not turn one failure into the failure of the whole auxiliary wheel

Mist dampened a storefront.

A rain garden overflowed somewhere unexpected.

The explanation of a retention basin was insufficient, leaving nearby residents anxious.

A water-temperature chart missed.

These failures are not necessarily proof that the auxiliary wheel itself is unnecessary. They may show which part needs adjustment.

If failure is turned into a single judgment document, even the parts that can be fixed are lost.

### 2. Break down the failure and look for the part that can be fixed

Check wind direction, nozzle position, ground height, downspout outlets, timing of explanations, changes in currents and wind.

Instead of reducing the choice to “do it or quit,” ask where to fix, who will support it, and what to record next time.

Breaking down failure is not for blaming someone.

It is for correcting the next form.

### 3. Preserving failures without hiding them helps the next town

If you take only success stories to another town, the same failures are likely to be reproduced.

What did not work?

How was it fixed?

What should the next town check first?

By preserving those points, failure becomes a handrail for the next town.

If failure is hidden, it becomes a pitfall.

If it is fixed and preserved, it becomes a handrail for the next town.

Part 7 Episode 8 reinterpreted failure not as a reason to stop an auxiliary wheel, but as information for correcting it into a less fragile form.

To continue auxiliary wheels, the decision to quit is also necessary.

Some forms are too dangerous.

Some are not worth the cost.

Some keep concentrating burdens no matter how many times they are adjusted.

Those auxiliary wheels must either change shape or be stopped.

But the important posture of Part 7 is not to end everything because of one failure or one complaint, but to check what can be fixed before choosing the next step.

Before the failure of an auxiliary wheel becomes a reason to quit, it tells us where to fix.

That sentence is the center of Part 7 Episode 8.

Next time, we will likely look at how to hand a corrected auxiliary wheel to the next town.

By handing over not only success, but also failure and correction, the next town can begin not from the first step, but a little farther ahead.

Part 7 now moves toward records and sharing that allow auxiliary wheels to pass outside the town.
