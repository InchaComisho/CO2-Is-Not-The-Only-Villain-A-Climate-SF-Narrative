# Part 7 Episode 9 — Taking the failure map to the next town

## Foreword

In Part 7 Episode 8, we looked at how, when an auxiliary wheel does not work well, we should not immediately decide, “It failed, so let’s stop.” Instead, we should break down what happened and look for the parts that can be fixed.

Mist was carried by wind and dampened a storefront.

A rain garden overflowed in an unexpected direction.

At the retention basin, the explanation was insufficient and anxiety remained.

At the port, the reading of the water-temperature chart missed.

But the town that recorded its failures, looked at the sites, and adjusted the forms little by little did not remain stuck in the same place.

In Episode 9, the protagonist and the AI take the “failure map” made by that town and head to the next town.

However, the new town is not the same as the previous town.

The wind is different.

The low ground is different.

People’s lives are different, and so is the work at the port.

If the previous town’s answer is placed unchanged, it will fail again.

But if the previous town’s failures are carried along, the next town can begin a little wiser.

This is the episode for “handing over” auxiliary wheels, not “copying” them.

---

“Let’s go to the next town.”

I closed the file on the desk.

On the cover, thick letters said:

Records of auxiliary wheels — what worked, and what did not work.

Inside were not only success stories.

The day mist nozzles were pushed toward storefronts by wind.

The day water escaped sideways from a rain garden.

The day people struggled to clean mud after water entered the retention basin.

The morning the fish were missed even after looking at the water-temperature chart.

“We’re taking this with us?”

“We are.”

“It feels much heavier than a brochure full of success stories.”

“Failure is packed with notes of caution.”

“Notes of caution?”

“So the next town does not stumble in the same place.”

I held the file in my arms.

“All right. Part 7 Episode 9. Let’s go to the next town.”

“This time, you are not carrying an answer.”

“Then what are we carrying?”

The AI paused for a moment.

“Questions.”

“Questions?”

“We begin by checking what is the same in this town, and what is different.”

“I see. We don’t start by installing things.”

“To hand over an auxiliary wheel is not to force an installation location onto someone.”

“You’re saying something very Part 7 right from the start.”

“It is Episode 9.”

With a file full of success and failure, I opened the map of the next town.

## Section 1 — The next town is different from the “previous town” from the start

The next town lay a little away from the sea.

Its station front was small.

Its shopping street was short.

But summer was hot.

The roads were not wide.

The houses stood close together.

When strong rain fell, water tended to gather near the intersection.

The river was far away, but there was a lot of low ground.

“It’s completely different from the previous town.”

“That is why you must not place the auxiliary wheels unchanged.”

At the station-front square, a municipal officer was waiting.

People from the shopping street.

Nearby residents.

A schoolteacher.

A disaster-prevention officer.

Someone who worked in landscaping.

And instead of port workers, people from a distribution center.

“There’s no port.”

“The sea auxiliary wheel cannot be used as it is.”

“What about the water-temperature chart?”

“It can be used if translated into ‘how to place on-site experience beside new data.’”

“I see. We don’t carry the chart. We carry the way of thinking.”

“That’s right.”

The officer said:

“In this town as well, heat and heavy rain are issues. We are considering whether we can place mist at the station front, increase rain gardens in residential areas, and do something about the water gathering at the low intersection.”

I opened the file.

“We tried similar things in the previous town.”

A person from the shopping street leaned forward a little.

“Then do you have the answer?”

For a moment, I was at a loss for words.

We have an answer.

If I said that, the conversation would move quickly.

But.

“I would like to say we do.”

The AI glowed faintly at the edge of the screen.

“But placing the previous town’s answer unchanged would be dangerous.”

“Dangerous?”

“Mist dampened storefronts because of wind. The rain garden misread the water flow. The retention basin almost lost trust because the explanation was insufficient.”

The room went quiet.

“So today, before asking ‘what should we place,’ we have come to see ‘what should we check.’”

The officer nodded slowly.

“Not answers, but check items?”

“The first auxiliary wheel can also be a checklist.”

“So you did not bring the previous town’s successes?”

“We brought the successes too. But we also brought the failures.”

“The failures too?”

“Yes. Because that is where the marks are that can help the next town avoid stumbling.”

## Section 2 — Mist betrays you if you place it without listening to the wind

The first place we went was the station front.

It was not as large as the previous town’s.

There was no arcade.

The bus-stop roof was low, and a bakery stood right beside it.

“We’d like to attach mist here.”

A person from the shopping street pointed to the bus-stop post.

It was certainly hot.

The faces of people waiting already looked tired in the morning.

The bench was hot too.

“In the previous town, we would line up nozzles here.”

I started to say that, then stopped.

“Open the file.”

I opened the “mist” page.

Beside records from successful days, red text said:

Mist drifted toward storefronts depending on wind direction.

Paper goods became damp.

The nozzle position was changed.

A stopping threshold was set for strong-wind days.

The focus shifted from storefronts to places where people wait.

“Does this bakery use bags and paper boxes?”

“We do. Every day.”

“If the wind blows this way?”

A person from the shopping street looked at the sky.

“In summer, it often moves through from this direction.”

“Then if we put it here?”

Someone looked at the bakery entrance.

“The mist might drift there.”

In the previous town, we noticed that only after something became damp once.

“We can avoid the bakery entrance from the beginning.”

“The failure map avoided the first hole.”

“But we can still place mist.”

I pointed to the opposite side of the bus stop.

“Here, it is close to the people waiting. It is a little away from the shop. The nozzle direction can also be adjusted to the wind.”

“What about the water bill?”

Another voice came immediately.

“It would be hard for the shopping street alone to pay.”

The previous town’s file had an answer to that too.

But not as an answer.

As questions.

Who uses it?

Who pays for it?

Who inspects it?

Who decides when to stop it?

“If this is used as part of the town’s heat measures, it should not be treated as equipment belonging only to the shopping street.”

“Look at it jointly?”

“At the very least, it is better to think from the beginning about a form supported jointly.”

The bakery worker said:

“If you ask from the beginning, I am not against it.”

“Really?”

“I’m not opposing it. I’m saying I’d be troubled if the form only troubles the shop.”

I smiled a little.

“We heard the same thing in the previous town.”

“Opposition is a note of caution written at the edge of a design drawing.”

“That’s a good way to put it.”

“We are reading the failure map.”

## Section 3 — A rain garden looks for an “escape route” before water

Next was a residential intersection.

When rain fell, water gathered there.

The gutter was narrow.

The edge of the road was slightly low.

Nearby, there was a small vacant lot.

“We are thinking about turning this into a rain garden,” the landscaping worker said.

“We would make a hollow to receive some of the water from roofs and roads, then plant grasses and low shrubs.”

At a glance, it looked similar to the previous town’s rain garden.

But the AI drew red lines on the screen.

“Water comes from here.”

Roofs.

Roads.

Parking lots.

Gutters.

“And it escapes here.”

The edge of the vacant lot.

A neighboring house’s walkway.

Beside a low wall.

“The previous town’s rain garden overflowed sideways,” I said.

“So first, we look at where the water will escape.”

A nearby resident frowned.

“Will it come into our walkway?”

“As it is now, that is possible.”

“That would be a problem.”

“So before making the rain garden, we change the water’s escape route.”

Adjust the depth of the hollow.

Create an outlet for safe overflow.

Adjust the edge height so water does not flow onto walking paths.

Check water movement during heavy rain together with neighbors.

“A rain garden is not just a place to hold water, is it?” the schoolteacher said.

“It is a place that stops water from rushing,” I answered.

“But it does not imprison the water. You also think about where it escapes when it overflows.”

“Even water needs an auxiliary wheel.”

“A water auxiliary wheel?”

“Do not make it rush. Do not make it wander. Do not send it where people are.”

“AI, you’re a little poetic today.”

“Because rain is coming.”

“That sounds like an explanation, and not quite like one.”

I wrote in the white file:

—Before making a rain garden, look at the entrance and exit of water.
—Do not only receive water. Decide where to let it escape.

## Section 4 — The biggest hole is “we were not told”

In the afternoon, we gathered at the town’s low intersection.

Here, the previous town’s retention basin experience became the topic.

However, this town did not have land for a large retention basin.

It was far from the river.

There was no large grassland.

“In the previous town, you made a place to receive water, correct?” the disaster-prevention officer said.

“Here, we have an idea for an underground storage facility.”

“That would be an invisible auxiliary wheel.”

“Yes. But there will be construction, and its operation is hard to understand. We need residents to understand it.”

I opened the retention basin page.

There, in letters larger than the water-level diagram, red text said:

The equipment worked. But the explanation did not arrive in time.

“In the previous town, receiving the water itself worked,” I said.

“But people nearby did not know how far the water would come, when roads would close, or who would clean up after the water receded.”

Someone drew a small breath.

“And because of that, trust was nearly lost.”

The disaster-prevention officer gripped the materials again.

“In this town, if it is an underground facility, the water will not be visible. But construction will be visible. Roads will change. There will be periods when they cannot be used.”

“Then ‘it will be safe once it is completed’ is not enough.”

“What should we tell people?”

“What changes during construction. How it works in heavy rain. Who inspects it. If the machinery stops, how people will be notified.”

“And create a place where residents can ask questions before completion.”

“Before completion?”

“Yes. If you explain only after it is complete, ‘we were not told’ remains.”

Someone from the shopping street said:

“But if we explain that much, won’t people oppose it?”

I remembered the grassland in the previous town, with mud left behind.

“If you trouble people without explaining, they will oppose it even more.”

It became quiet.

Then the first person to speak was the bakery worker.

“Then let’s hold a site visit.”

“A site visit?”

“Let’s go see the previous town’s auxiliary wheels. Not only the successful parts. Let’s ask them to show us the parts they fixed.”

The AI placed a small circle on the screen.

“The failure map became a route map.”

I wrote that into the new file.

—Ask before completion.
—Show before completion.
—Create a place where people can ask questions before completion.

## Section 5 — Not copying, but handing over

Evening.

A small community room in the town.

On the table lay a new file.

Its cover was still white.

I took a pen.

“We will begin this town’s auxiliary-wheel record here.”

“Aren’t we going to use the previous town’s file as it is?” the schoolteacher asked.

“We will use it. But we will not copy it.”

“What is the difference?”

I placed the previous town’s file beside the new white file.

“The previous town’s file says, ‘This is how we placed mist.’”

“Yes.”

“But in this town, the wind and shops are different. So we turn it into: ‘Before placing mist, check the wind and shop positions.’”

“I see.”

“The previous town says, ‘We dug the rain garden here.’”

“But in this town?”

“We first look at where water comes from and where it escapes.”

“And the retention basin experience?”

“It is not about making a large pond. It is handed over as: ‘explain in advance what will happen during construction and during emergencies.’”

“The form changes. But the cautions gained from failure remain.”

I wrote on the first page of the white file:

—This town’s auxiliary wheels will be made to fit this town’s wind, water, and lives.
—Do not imitate the previous town’s success. First check the previous town’s failures.

“So this is what handing over means.”

“Yes.”

“It is not copying.”

“It means making it possible for the next town to turn the wheels by itself.”

“If you copy, you hand over the form.”

“If you hand over, you hand over questions and notes of caution.”

“And the next town writes its own answers.”

“In that white file.”

I looked at the white file, still almost empty.

Though it was white, it looked a little stronger than before.

## Section 6 — Auxiliary wheels become a little lighter when they cross towns

On the way home.

The sky had darkened slightly.

The bench at the station front.

There was still no mist.

There was still no rain garden in the vacant lot.

The underground storage facility was still only in planning.

“Nothing has been done,” I said.

“Nothing has been placed.”

“Isn’t that the same?”

“It is different.”

The AI displayed the white file.

Look at the wind.

Look at the places that would trouble shops.

Look at the paths where water comes and escapes.

Explain construction and emergency use in advance.

Do not leave troubled people outside the plan.

If it fails, do not hide it. Fix it.

“The auxiliary wheels are not here yet.”

“But there are auxiliary wheels before placement.”

“Questions?”

“Questions.”

In the previous town, many things were understood only after failure.

This town had not failed yet.

But it had the previous town’s records.

So from the beginning, it could look at a slightly different place.

“The failure map was not a scary story.”

“If you know where the holes are, you can walk even at night.”

“That’s a good ending.”

I wrote the final line.

—To hand an auxiliary wheel to the next town is not to carry the same form. It is to tell where the previous town stumbled, so the next town can choose its own path.

“Part 7 Episode 9 ends here.”

“Next?”

“The new town places its first auxiliary wheel.”

“At last, the first failure will be written into the white file.”

“That’s unlucky.”

“And the first improvement will be written too.”

“That’s better.”

A new heading appeared on the AI’s screen.

Part 7 Episode 10 — The first auxiliary wheel begins not with a perfect plan, but with the first trial run.

I closed the white file.

This time, it felt a little lighter than the previous town’s record.

But perhaps its contents were a little stronger than before.

Part 7 Episode 9 ends here.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Part 7 Episode 9.

In this episode, we looked at how to hand the “failures” recorded in Episode 8 to the next town.

The important point here is not to copy the auxiliary wheels that worked in the previous town unchanged.

When the town changes, the wind direction, land height, shop circumstances, water flow, and the anxieties people carry also change.

The previous town’s experience is handed over not as an “answer,” but as “questions to check first.”

The main points of this episode are three.

### 1. The previous town’s failures become notes of caution for the next town

The experience of mist drifting in the wind and dampening storefronts becomes the question: “Before installation, check wind direction and product locations.”

The experience of a rain garden overflowing sideways becomes the question: “Before holding water, check where water escapes.”

The experience of insufficient explanation at the retention basin becomes the question: “Before the facility is completed, explain what happens during construction, emergencies, and restoration.”

The experience of a missed water-temperature chart becomes the attitude: “Do not decide by data alone or experience alone; preserve the reason for the miss.”

Failure is not a scary story meant to stop the next town.

It becomes a note of caution that tells the next town what to look at first.

### 2. Spreading auxiliary wheels is not copying, but handing over

Instead of placing the previous town’s mist unchanged, the new town checks its own wind, shop positions, water bills, and maintenance roles.

Instead of reproducing the previous town’s rain garden, it looks at how water enters, where it escapes when it overflows, and how it affects neighbors.

Instead of imitating the previous town’s retention basin, it hands over the idea: “Do not leave the people who may be troubled outside the plan.”

The form changes.

But the cautions gained from failure remain.

That is handing over, not copying.

### 3. Before sharing “what to place,” share “what to check”

An auxiliary wheel is not only the equipment itself.

Looking at wind.

Checking the path of water.

Listening to the voices of people who will receive burdens.

Recording failure without hiding it.

These checks are also auxiliary wheels that help a town prepare for the future.

Part 7 Episode 9 showed that spreading auxiliary wheels to another town does not mean forcing a correct answer onto it. It means helping that town test, fix, and continue under its own conditions.

To hand an auxiliary wheel to the next town is not to carry the same form.

It is to tell where the previous town stumbled, so the next town can choose its own path.

That sentence is the center of Part 7 Episode 9.

In Part 7 Episode 10, the new town begins writing its own auxiliary wheels onto the first page of the white file.

Instead of waiting for a perfect plan, it will enter the stage of running a small trial, recording it, and growing it to fit the shape of the town.
