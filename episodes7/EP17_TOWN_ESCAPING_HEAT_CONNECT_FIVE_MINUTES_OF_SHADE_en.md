# Part 7 Episode 17 — A town escaping heat: connect five minutes of shade

## Foreword

In Part 7 Episode 16, the town with no vacant lots used the supermarket rooftop as an “invisible vacant lot” and tested an auxiliary wheel that made rainwater falling on the roof wait for a little while.

One small tank is not magic that can stop the town’s heavy rain.

Even so, it showed the possibility of delaying the roof water from concentrating into the gutters at the same time as road water, and of returning a little decision-making time to the people on the ground.

At the same time, the rooftop auxiliary wheel had other issues.

Checking the drains.

Drawing a safety line so no one overdoes it in strong wind.

Providing an outlet when the tank becomes full.

Dividing roles among the store, the management company, and the disaster-prevention officer.

After the rain stopped, the town became humid again.

The ground had absorbed rain.

Moisture remained in the air.

Sunlight came through gaps in the clouds.

The concrete in front of the station began to return heat again.

In Episode 17, the rooftop water auxiliary wheel is connected to heat auxiliary wheels on the ground.

There is no large park.

No broad shade.

No spare space for one huge resting place.

But the station front has small eaves.

There are shops where people can buy water.

There are places where someone can sit for a short time.

There are places where someone can ask for help.

The protagonist and the AI try to connect places where people can rest for just five minutes across the town, creating a small path for escaping heat.

It was humid.

The sun was only slightly visible through a gap in the clouds, but the air felt heavy.

Heat rose from the sidewalk.

The bus-stop bench was a little drier than yesterday, but I still did not feel like sitting on it.

“On the roof, we made water wait,” I said.

“On the ground, heat is waiting,” the AI replied.

“It does not need to wait.”

Around the station front, there were small margins we had found earlier.

The wall beside the drugstore.

The thin shade in front of the bank.

The information board beside the police box.

The bus-stop roof.

Near the shopping association warehouse.

The unused space on the first floor of the apartment building.

“Today, we connect these.”

“We draw a line for escaping heat.”

“Part 7 Episode 17. Find five minutes of shade — or rather, five minutes under the eaves.”

## Section 1 — Heat is hard to see until just before someone collapses

Someone heading to work.

Someone with a child.

People waiting for the bus.

Someone pushing delivery goods.

People in a hurry.

From a distance, you cannot tell who is suffering from the heat.

“Rain becomes puddles,” I said.

“And heat?”

“It accumulates inside the body.”

The AI marked red circles on the station-front map.

The bus stop.

Before the crosswalk.

The supermarket entrance.

A sidewalk with no shade.

A place where people wait at the traffic light.

“Hot places are scattered through the town.”

“Then resting places should be scattered too.”

“Not one big cooling shelter?”

“This town has no room for that.”

A person from the shopping association said:

“But maybe shops can each share a little space in front of them.”

The supermarket manager looked at the space beside the entrance.

“Here, two or three people could stand.”

The drugstore worker said:

“We can place one chair.”

“We can put a map of resting places here.”

“A map of resting places?” the schoolteacher said.

“Let’s use symbols children can understand too, so when the heat feels difficult, people do not have to wonder where to go next.”

The AI placed blue dots on the map.

Five-minute cooling points.

“Five minutes?”

“Not to cool down perfectly. Five minutes to reconsider the next action.”

“Drink water. Sit down. Cool the body. Talk to someone.”

“Go home. Call for a ride. Change where to wait for the bus.”

“Do not keep walking through it.”

“Auxiliary wheels are not for continuing endurance. They are for stopping endurance.”

“That is strong.”

“In front of heat, endurance is not a strategy.”

“That sounds like another famous line.”

“It is better to stop before collapsing.”

## Section 2 — The first cooperating shop sets conditions

The manager raised one hand.

In Part 7, conditions matter.

“That is important.”

“It is dangerous to place a heat auxiliary wheel and simply write, ‘Come here if you are in trouble.’”

The manager continued:

“If more people come, the aisle may be blocked. It may affect products inside the store. We also cannot provide unlimited water.”

## Section 4 — At two in the afternoon, five minutes becomes long

At two in the afternoon, the temperature rose.

The humidity did not fall.

An older woman was sitting on the chair we had placed at the drugstore as a trial.

She had set her shopping bags at her feet and was pressing her forehead.

“Are you all right?” the manager called to her.

“A little... I was walking, and suddenly...”

We approached too.

“Can you drink water?”

“A little.”

“Let’s rest here.”

The manager glanced at the staff.

But there was only one staff member.

There were customers waiting at the register inside.

“So this is the next problem,” I said quietly.

“When auxiliary wheels are actually used, shortages appear.”

The woman sat in the chair.

She drank a little water.

But even after five minutes, she did not stand up.

“It is supposed to be a five-minute cooling point,” the schoolteacher said.

“Some people need more than five minutes.”

“What do we do?”

The manager looked a little troubled.

“We do not have a place inside the store for someone to rest farther in today.”

“The police box?”

The police-box person said:

“We will look at the situation. If necessary, we will judge including whether to call emergency services.”

“But can she walk to the police box?”

The delivery-center worker said:

“It may be better not to move her unnecessarily.”

The AI displayed a short line:

A five-minute cooling point is not a substitute for medical care.

“It is obvious, but we forget unless we write it down,” I said.

“A cooperating shop is the first resting place. If someone does not recover, it has to connect them to the next help.”

After a while, the woman calmed down a little.

Until someone came to pick her up, the manager and the police-box person watched over her.

It did not become a major incident.

But the white file gained new red lines.

Do not leave physical-condition checks and decisions to cooperating shops alone.

Do not move people unnecessarily; make the criteria for contact or emergency calls visible when needed.

In addition to chairs, shade, and water, lines for watching over and contacting others are necessary.

“Yes.”

## Afterword

In Part 7 Episode 17, the town with no vacant lots worked not by creating one large cooling shelter, but by connecting small cooling points scattered throughout the town to protect people from heat.

When it rains, the town thinks about where water flows.

When it is hot, the town thinks about where people can stop, where they can drink water, and where they can ask for help.

Even without a large park or a big tree shade in front of the station, the town can create a path for not continuing endurance by connecting shop eaves, chairs for short rests, hydration information, bus-stop adjustments, communication with police boxes or public facilities, and signs for cooperating shops.

The main points of this episode are five.

### 1. Heat is hard to see as a danger

In heavy rain, puddles and wet roads become visible signs of danger.

Heat, however, accumulates its burden inside the body.

It is hard to tell from outside whether a walking person is near their limit, and the person themselves may continue pushing on, thinking, “I can still go a little farther.”

That is why the town needs not only help after someone collapses, but also more places where people can rest and change their decision before that happens.

### 2. A “five-minute cooling point” is an auxiliary wheel for choosing the next action again

The goal is not to lower the temperature of the whole town at once.

Even a few minutes in shade, drinking water, sitting down, checking one’s condition, contacting family, or changing where to wait for the bus can help someone stop pushing themselves.

It is important to connect places for resting, hydration, watching over, and asking for help at short distances.

Auxiliary wheels are not for continuing endurance. They are for stopping endurance.

### 3. Do not concentrate responsibility on cooperating shops

Even if a shop provides a chair or eaves, the system cannot continue if shop staff alone must carry decisions about unwell people, emergency contact, crowding, or water provision.

A cooperating shop’s role is to provide the first place to rest.

If someone does not recover, the town needs shared lines for connecting that person to the police box, family, medical care, emergency services, or other support.

This applies to heat measures the same “outlet for management and responsibility” learned from rain gardens.

### 4. Five minutes of rest is not enough for everyone

A cooperating shop’s chair can help, but for someone whose condition has worsened, a short rest may not be enough.

The town as a whole, not only cooperating shops, needs procedures for watching over the situation, not moving people unnecessarily, and connecting them to family or appropriate support.

A small cooling point is not a substitute for medical or emergency services.

That is why designing the line to the next help matters.

### 5. Water auxiliary wheels and heat auxiliary wheels connect when roles are checked

Rainwater temporarily held on the roof may sometimes be used for planting strips or potted plants.

However, using it without checking water quality, hygiene, equipment, local rules, and management responsibility can create new problems.

When connecting a system that makes water wait with a system that softens heat, the town must first decide who manages it, how far it is used, and what happens when something is abnormal.

Part 7 Episode 17 described the heat auxiliary wheel not as a huge facility, but as a combination of small places where people can rest for five minutes and lines that connect them to the next help.

A heat auxiliary wheel is not one large shade.

It connects places where people can rest for five minutes, places where they can call for help, and places where they can drink water, creating a path where people can stop enduring.

Five minutes of shade is not enough if it merely exists.

It becomes an auxiliary wheel only when people can find it, know they may enter it, and connect from there to the next help.

These two lines are the center of Part 7 Episode 17.

In the next episode, Part 7 Episode 18, the town turns the blue dots on the map into guidance that people can actually use.

When people cannot read text.

When they are in a hurry.

When a child or an older person is alone.

When someone is not good at Japanese.

An auxiliary wheel that exists but cannot be found cannot be used.

The town faces a quiet but powerful enemy: the signboard.
