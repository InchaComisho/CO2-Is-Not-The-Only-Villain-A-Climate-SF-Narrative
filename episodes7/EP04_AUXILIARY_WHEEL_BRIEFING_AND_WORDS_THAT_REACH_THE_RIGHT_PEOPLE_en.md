# Part 7 Episode 4 — The auxiliary wheel briefing, and choosing words that reach the right people

## Foreword

In Part 7 Episode 3, we looked at how the “helped” created by mist, rain gardens, retention basins, and water-temperature charts can be preserved as numbers, photographs, time, and voices.

Auxiliary wheels become easier to hand to the next season when their effects are not exaggerated, and when things that cannot become numbers are not erased.

In Episode 4, we think about who those records are explained to.

Words that reach budget officers.

Words that reach residents.

Words that reach shopping streets.

Words that reach ports.

Words that can pass to another town.

Even with the same auxiliary wheel, the explanation needed changes when the audience changes.

This is the episode where the protagonist and the AI use a small briefing session to look at the difference between “correct words” and “words that reach.”

“The materials are ready.”

I said it while looking at the papers spread across the desk.

Mist operation records.

Photographs of the rain garden.

Water-level diagrams for the retention basin.

The port’s water-temperature chart.

Short voices from people who used them.

Notes on the time by which evacuation began earlier.

“But next comes the briefing, right?”

“To whom?”

“That is the problem.”

I lined up icons of people on the screen.

Budget officers.

Residents.

Shopping street members.

Neighborhood associations.

The assembly.

Schools.

The port.

The neighboring town.

“If we hand the same materials to everyone, they will probably all fall asleep.”

“Correct materials and materials that reach people are different.”

“There it is.”

“That is the theme of Part 7 Episode 4.”

“Another meeting-room enemy?”

“This time it is less an enemy than a problem of translation.”

“Translation?”

“Turning the auxiliary wheel into words that lead to the audience’s next action.”

“Part 7 has been translating things the whole time.”

“Because it is the part about passing town ingenuity outside the town.”

I opened the briefing materials.

There were numbers.

There were photographs.

There were voices.

But the order in which to present them had not yet been decided.

## Section 1 — In the unmoved world line, “correct materials” reach no one

The AI brought up a briefing venue on the left side of the screen.

A community-center meeting room.

Tables.

Chairs.

A projector.

Stacks of handouts.

On the screen were rows of detailed graphs.

Rainfall.

Water level.

Temperature.

Operation time.

Number of users.

Cost effectiveness.

Before-and-after comparisons.

“Wow. These are proper materials.”

“They are accurate.”

The presenter says:

“Here are the operating results by fiscal year.”

“Next, please look at usage by temperature category.”

“This graph shows the water-level change during retention basin operation.”

The residents look at the materials.

“So what does this have to do with my house?”

“Is the mist that thing from the summer festival?”

“Does a rain garden mean a garden turns into a puddle?”

“Does a retention basin mean putting water into that grassy field?”

“It is not reaching them at all.”

“The materials are not wrong.”

“But they do not answer what the audience wants to know.”

“Right.”

Budget officers want to know cost and continuity.

Residents want to know what changes for their homes and families.

The shopping street wants to know how stores and foot traffic will change.

The port wants to know whether it can be used for tomorrow’s decisions.

Another town wants to know whether it can be used under its own conditions.

“Even if we show the same graph, everyone is looking at a different place.”

“A briefing fails not because there are not enough numbers.”

“Because the listeners have no entrance.”

Correct materials matter.

Numbers are necessary.

Photographs are necessary.

Records are necessary.

But unless they are placed at the listener’s entrance, the auxiliary wheel becomes a distant story.

“It is such a waste when something is correct but does not reach anyone.”

“It happens often.”

“Do not say something so scary.”

## Section 2 — In the slightly bent world line, the entrance changes

“All right, the right side.”

The AI opened another briefing session.

The same community center.

The same chairs.

The same materials.

But the first slide was different.

On the screen was a photograph of the station front.

A midsummer noon.

Shade.

A thin mist.

People waiting for the bus.

Beside it was one short sentence.

There were people who could wait here for five more minutes.

“That is short.”

“It is for residents.”

The next photograph.

The depression of a rain garden.

Rainwater pooled inside it.

The water that stopped once in this garden did not rush into the gutter all at once.

The next photograph.

Water spreading into a retention basin.

A levee visible in the distance.

Because water entered here, calls could be sent earlier to the riverside district.

The next photograph.

A water-temperature chart on the wall of a fishing port.

People looking at it before departure.

They used this chart to read today’s sea a little earlier.

“Each one says what changed.”

“The numbers come later.”

“First, show the change in everyday life.”

“Yes.”

Residents can listen by pulling the story toward their own lives.

Shopping street members think about the time mist held people in place.

Older residents think about the earlier evacuation call.

Parents think about where they can rest while taking children with them.

Fishers think about whether they can read tomorrow’s sea a little earlier.

“Even with the same auxiliary wheel, changing the entrance makes it become ‘my story.’”

“The correct materials have been arranged in an order that reaches people.”

“We are not hiding the numbers.”

“No.”

“But we are not dropping people straight into a sea of numbers.”

“Exactly.”

“Part 7 has a sea of paperwork too.”

“A very deep one.”

“I do not want to swim in it.”

“That is why we create an entrance.”

## Section 3 — For budget officers, show not dreams but how to continue

“But depending on the audience, photographs alone will not be enough.”

“Budget officers?”

“Exactly.”

The AI brought up another small meeting room.

This time it was a meeting between budget officers and the department in charge.

The first slide was not a photograph of the station front.

What is needed for continuing operation.

The number of inspections per year.

Operating days.

Water, electricity, and maintenance costs.

Division of roles among departments.

A three-year update plan.

The burden expected if it is not used.

“That is suddenly very realistic.”

“Because that is what this audience wants to know.”

“What will be a problem if this auxiliary wheel is stopped?”

“What happens not only this fiscal year, but three years from now?”

“Who maintains it?”

“How will the shared frame be divided?”

“‘It helped people’ alone is weak for budget officers.”

“It can be.”

“But ‘there is a system for continuing it’ is strong.”

“Yes.”

Voices from users.

Photographs from the site.

Water-level records.

And on top of them: budget, roles, maintenance, and update plans.

“Dreams alone are not enough. Numbers alone are not enough either.”

“Auxiliary wheels need both.”

“A reason to continue, and a method for continuing.”

“Right.”

“For a budget officer, a manageable story may be more necessary than a nice story.”

“That is very realistic, but true.”

“Part 7 is too realistic.”

“To keep auxiliary wheels from disappearing in April.”

Words that reach budget officers are not only emotional words.

Cost.

Roles.

Period.

Inspection.

Update.

The effects of stopping it.

Who carries what.

When those things are visible, the auxiliary wheel changes from “an initiative that seems vaguely good” into “a system whose continuation can be considered.”

## Section 4 — Give the port the port’s words

“What about the port?”

The AI showed a fishing port in the morning.

The sky was still slightly dark.

The sound of ice being loaded.

The sound of nets being carried.

People standing in front of the water-temperature chart.

“If we present a long slide deck here, they might get angry.”

“They may not like a long meeting.”

“Then what do we give them?”

A simple chart appeared on the screen.

Changes in today’s sea-surface temperature.

The direction of the tide.

Sea areas different from yesterday.

Fish whose target areas might change.

The time of the latest update.

“Just this?”

“What the port needs in the morning is something short, early, and usable.”

Fishers look at the chart and think about today’s sea.

Market workers make a small prediction about what may come in.

Processing workers adjust ice and storage preparation a little.

“So explanation does not mean telling everything.”

“It means telling enough for the audience to choose their next action.”

“That is very important.”

The port has the port’s own time.

A short time in the morning.

A decision before departure.

Preparation for the market.

The number of ice blocks and boxes.

Sometimes one chart works better than a long document.

Sometimes the update time and the place that changed matter more than a detailed explanation.

“The port has its own next move.”

“That is why we give it in the port’s words.”

“Even the same water-temperature chart is different as research material and as port material.”

“The purpose is different.”

“But both are necessary.”

“Yes.”

## Section 5 — When passing it to another town, do not make it a success story

“Finally, what if we explain it to the neighboring town?”

The AI brought up people on an inspection visit.

Officials from another town.

People from another shopping street.

Watershed staff from another area.

People from another port.

“If we say, ‘We succeeded!’ here, that is a little dangerous.”

“Why?”

“The other town has its own conditions.”

The width of the street is different.

The angle of the sunlight is different.

The soil is different.

The river shape is different.

The budget, the available hands, and the number of residents are different.

“If we carry over a success story as it is, it ends with, ‘That is impossible here.’”

“Then what do we pass on?”

“Not the finished form, but the way of thinking.”

I rewrote the cover of the materials.

What this town tried.

Where this town struggled.

What this town corrected.

“With this, another town can edit it for itself.”

“Part 5 and Part 7 have connected.”

Not “we installed mist.”

But “we thought about how to create a place where people can wait on hot days.”

Not “we built a rain garden.”

But “we looked for places where rain could be received once before being released.”

Not “we improved a retention basin.”

But “we thought about how to leave places inside the town where water is allowed to come.”

“A briefing is a translation task for sending auxiliary wheels outside the town.”

“Yes.”

“Not boasting about success, but passing on the way of thinking.”

“And having the other town remake it in its own place.”

“That is a path toward the world story.”

“It continues Part 7 Episode 1.”

An order adjusted to the audience.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Part 7 Episode 4.

In this episode, we looked at whom records of auxiliary wheels should be explained to, and in what order they need to be explained in order to reach them.

Auxiliary wheels such as mist, rain gardens, retention basins, and water-temperature charts may not reach residents, shopping streets, ports, budget officers, or other towns through accurate numbers and materials alone.

That is because each audience wants to know different things, chooses different next actions, and has different amounts of usable time.

The main points of this episode are three.

1. Correct materials and materials that reach are different
   Even if graphs and numbers are accurate, auxiliary wheels remain distant if people cannot see what they have to do with themselves.

For residents: “I could wait here for five more minutes.”

For shopping streets: “People were able to rest a little.”

For ports: “We could read tomorrow’s sea a little earlier.”

For budget officers: “There is a reason to continue, and a method for continuing.”

Those entrances are necessary.

Preparing correct materials matters.

But unless those materials are placed at the audience’s entrance, the auxiliary wheel does not reach them.

2. Change the order of explanation depending on the audience
   For residents, begin with photographs, changes in everyday life, and short words.

For budget officers, show maintenance costs, roles, continued operation, and update plans.

For ports, provide a short, early, usable chart for morning decisions.

For another town, pass on not the completed form, but what was tried, where it struggled, and how it was corrected.

Explanation does not mean arranging all the information you have in the same order.

It means rearranging the materials in an order that lets the audience choose its next action.

3. Words that reach people change the audience’s next action
   Explanation is not only for conveying all knowledge.

A resident thinks, “Next hot day, I will go there.”

A shopping street member thinks, “We should help with inspections.”

A budget officer judges, “We should consider a continuing frame.”

Another town thinks, “How would we translate this for our own place?”

Words that lead to those next actions carry auxiliary wheels outside the town.

Part 7 Episode 4 was a chapter that rethought auxiliary wheels not only as facilities, but also as explanations that slightly change the next person’s action.

Words that explain auxiliary wheels are not enough if they are merely correct.

They must become words that lead to the next action.

Words that reach are words that make the audience’s next action a little easier to see.

These two sentences are the center of Part 7 Episode 4.

In the next episode, we will likely look at what people actually choose after hearing the explanation.

Auxiliary wheels are not welcomed by everyone right away.

“Is this worth using tax money for?”

“This has nothing to do with us.”

“The side that receives the water is the only one that loses.”

“It will get in the way of business.”

Opposition and anxiety like these cannot be avoided if auxiliary wheels are to continue.

Part 7 moves from explanation into the place of agreement and opposition.
