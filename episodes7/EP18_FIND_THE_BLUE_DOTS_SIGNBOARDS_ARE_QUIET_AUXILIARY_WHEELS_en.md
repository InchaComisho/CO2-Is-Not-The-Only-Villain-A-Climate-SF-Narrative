# Part 7 Episode 18 — Find the blue dots: signboards are quiet auxiliary wheels

## Foreword

In Part 7 Episode 17, the town with no vacant lots began connecting “five-minute cooling points” across the town so people could escape the heat.

The eaves of the supermarket.

A chair at the drugstore.

Guidance beside the police box.

A small patch of shade at the bus stop.

Water guidance placed near the shopping association’s storage space.

And the line of contact that connects someone whose condition does not recover to the next kind of help.

There is no large cooling shelter.

There is no single piece of equipment that solves the town’s heat.

Even so, for people who had no choice but to keep walking in the heat, the town created places to rest for five minutes and choices that let them stop pushing themselves.

But the AI pointed out the next problem.

An auxiliary wheel is not enough just because it exists.

If people cannot find it, it is the same as not placing it at all.

People who do not have time to read text.

People whose thinking is blurred by heat.

Children.

Older people.

People who are not comfortable with Japanese.

People visiting the town for the first time.

In Episode 18, the protagonist and the AI try to turn the blue dots drawn on the map into guidance that people can truly find.

However, a signboard was not a quiet board that ended once it was installed.

There is too much text.

The arrows point the wrong way.

They are hidden by shop signs.

They are needed only on hot days, so people do not notice them on cloudy days.

The auxiliary wheels begin another trial run from the moment people have to find them.

## Section 1 — A signboard full of text is not read

The first signboard was impressive.

It was tall.

The colors were clean.

It had a map.

It listed the cooperating shops.

It had contact points.

It had caution notes.

It explained heatstroke.

It included information for rainy days.

It even mentioned the rooftop rainwater tank.

“We put everything in!”

The disaster-prevention officer said this with a little pride.

I fell silent.

The AI also fell silent.

The schoolteacher looked at the signboard.

“…It is long.”

“It is necessary information.”

“It is necessary. But will a person suffering from heat read all of it?”

The disaster-prevention officer’s smile stiffened a little.

“Maybe not.”

“What about a child?”

“They won’t read it.”

“What about someone in a hurry?”

“They won’t read it.”

“What about someone who does not understand Japanese?”

“They cannot read it.”

The police-box officer stood in front of the signboard.

“Does this arrow point to the supermarket or the drugstore?”

“Huh?”

“On the map, it points right. But on the actual street, it points left.”

Everyone stepped closer.

“You’re right.”

“The arrow is only pointing right on the map.”

“It is not the town’s right.”

I held my head.

“The signboard is lost from day one.”

The AI quietly displayed a line.

Guidance must be tested through the eyes of someone passing through for the first time, not through the eyes of the person who made it.

“Another trial run?”

“Auxiliary wheels are not complete until they are tested by the people who will use them.”

“Then let’s do it.”

I stood in front of the signboard.

“Let’s reduce the text.”

The disaster-prevention officer was surprised.

“Reduce it?”

“Yes.”

“But the explanation—”

“Put the explanation somewhere else.”

“Where?”

“On the signboard, write only where to go next.”

The AI split the signboard screen into three parts.

Hot / feeling bad.

Look for the blue mark.

Go to the nearest resting place.

“That’s all?”

“For now, that is all.”

“A signboard is not a teacher that explains everything. It is a hand pointing to the next step.”

“Not knowledge, but a destination.”

“Yes.”

“Making a hot person read a long text is not an auxiliary wheel.”

“It becomes more like a hill.”

“The quiet strong enemy is already tough.”

## Section 2 — Children run before the arrow can work

“Even with the same guidance, the entry point changes.”

“An auxiliary wheel cannot always reach everyone with one set of words.”

“A blue circle reaches before language.”

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Part 7 Episode 18.

This episode showed the process of turning the “five-minute cooling points” that had spread across the town into guidance that people can actually find and use.

Auxiliary wheels are not useful just because they exist as equipment or places.

People whose judgment has weakened in the heat, children, older people, people visiting the town for the first time, and people who are not comfortable with Japanese need to understand quickly where they should go.

A signboard may look like a small thing, but it is an important mechanism that determines whether auxiliary wheels can actually be used.

The main points of this episode are five.

### 1. A signboard is not a place to put every piece of information

The first signboard gathered many kinds of information: cooperating shops, contact points, heatstroke explanations, rainy-day information, and even an explanation of the rooftop rainwater tank.

But people who are suffering from heat or in a hurry cannot read long text.

The role of a signboard is not to explain the whole town system.

It is to tell people, briefly and without confusion, where to go next.

A signboard is not a teacher that explains everything.

It is a hand pointing to the next step.

### 2. Guidance must be tested by users, not only by the people who made it

Children missed the signboard because it was at the waist height of adults.

The right side of the map did not match the right side of the real street, and a first-time visitor walked the wrong way.

By testing guidance in the actual town with children, older people, and first-time visitors, problems such as arrow direction, height, current-location display, and sign placement become visible.

Guidance also needs a trial run.

### 3. A shared mark helps people find blue dots

Placing the same blue mark at cooperating shops and resting places makes it easier to understand, even without reading words, that “this may be a place where I can rest for a little while.”

However, not every cooling point can provide the same support.

A place where people can sit.

A place where people can buy water.

A place where people can call for help.

It is also important to show each role with simple pictures, so people do not misunderstand the blue dot as meaning “this place can do everything.”

### 4. Guidance must also work when language does not

Short sentences, clear pictures, unified colors and symbols, and large arrows can help people who are not comfortable with Japanese, or people who are too affected by heat to understand long explanations.

Guidance is not for displaying knowledge.

It exists so people in trouble can move toward the next safe place.

When someone needs help, a short picture and one arrow may be kinder than a long explanation.

### 5. Signboards must also be managed according to weather and time

On days of heavy rain, eaves or bus stops that are usually usable as cooling points may not be safe.

The town needs a mechanism for replacing guidance, checking sites, and updating contact information according to hot days, rainy days, and construction days.

Old information and wrong arrows can, like mud after rain, send people toward dangerous places.

Checking signboards must also be included in the maintenance of auxiliary wheels.

Part 7 Episode 18 treated signboards not as boards that simply list information, but as quiet auxiliary wheels that show people lost in heat or rain the next safe place and the choice to stop pushing themselves.

A signboard is not just a board.

It is a quiet auxiliary wheel that helps people lost in heat or rain find the next safe place and the choice to stop pushing themselves.

A signboard exists not to increase information, but to reduce the time spent lost.

These two lines are the center of Part 7 Episode 18.

More people will come.

Heat, sudden rain, crowds, lost children, and people feeling unwell may happen at the same time.
