# Part 7 Episode 19 — On a lively day, the blue arrows do not get lost

## Foreword

In Part 7 Episode 18, the “five-minute cooling points” for escaping heat were turned into guidance that people in town could actually find.

Blue marks.

Short arrows.

Pictures for sitting, drinking water, and calling for help.

However, signboards cannot be useful only on quiet days.

They are truly tested on days when there are many people, when it is hot, when sudden rain comes, and when shops are busy.

In Episode 19, at the station-front summer festival, the blue dots and blue arrows enter a crowd for the first time.

Heat.

A sudden shower.

Congestion.

Lost children.

People feeling unwell.

These things do not arrive neatly in separate time slots.

The town’s auxiliary wheels are tested not on quiet days, but on lively ones.

---

“There are a lot of people.”

The station front had been lively since morning.

Food stalls.

Music.

Children.

Shoppers.

Tourists.

People in yukata.

People fanning themselves against the heat.

It was nothing like the usual station front.

“Can you see the blue dots?”

“I can. But inside a wave of people, they are small.”

Near the bus stop, there was a blue guidance board.

Under the supermarket eaves, a blue mark.

At the drugstore entrance, a blue chair.

Beside the police box, a picture of a phone for calling for help.

Near the shopping association’s storage space, water guidance.

A temporary blue sign had also been posted at the community hall entrance.

“On a quiet day, it was easy to understand,” I said.

“But today there are banners, stalls, and people everywhere.”

“A signboard becomes much weaker when it enters a crowd.”

“Weaker?”

“If no one finds it, it is the same as not placing it.”

“This is a continuation of Episode 18.”

I opened the white file.

Heat auxiliary wheels.

Blue cooling points.

Signboards.

And today’s page.

Summer festival trial run.

“Part 7 Episode 19. Auxiliary wheels, deploy to the summer festival.”

## Section 1 — The first lost child had not looked at the signboard

Before noon.

A small child was crying in front of the police box.

“My mom is gone.”

The police officer crouched down and asked:

“Where did you get separated?”

The child shook his head.

“I don’t know.”

At that moment, the schoolteacher looked at the nearby signboard and said:

“Places with the blue mark are not only for resting. They are also places where you can ask for help when you are in trouble.”

“But this child did not look at the signboard,” I said.

“The blue mark has to be visible even to someone who did not read the sign.”

The police officer pointed to the blue mark.

“If you are in trouble, you can look for this blue sign.”

The child wiped away tears and nodded.

“The blue place?”

“Yes. The blue circle. You can tell the adults there that you are in trouble.”

An announcement for the lost child went out.

A person from the shopping association opened the line of stalls a little.

The schoolteacher guided the child into the shade.

The police officer confirmed identifying details.

The drugstore manager moved the blue mark at the entrance slightly so it could be seen more easily from outside.

After a while, the child’s mother was found.

“Good.”

I let out a breath.

“The blue dots were not only places to rest.”

“They are also places inside the town where someone can stop and call for help.”

The teacher spoke to the children.

“If you get lost, do not run around. Go near a blue mark and talk to a nearby adult.”

“So they are not only for resting,” said the person from the shopping association.

“They are places to stop when you are in trouble.”

I wrote in the white file:

—Blue dots are not only for cooling. They can also become markers where lost children or people feeling unwell can stop and ask for help.

“The mark worked before the signboard did.”

“Sometimes a color that enters the eye arrives before a quiet board.”

## Section 2 — Rain arrives more suddenly than the sound of the stalls

Two in the afternoon.

The sky darkened.

A slightly different sound of wind mixed with the music.

“It’s coming,” the AI said.

“Rain?”

“A sudden shower. Short, but possibly strong.”

The stall workers hurried to cover their goods with cloth.

People ran under the eaves.

People also gathered in front of the blue guidance board.

“The supermarket eaves are getting crowded!” the manager said.

“So is the drugstore!”

“What about the bus stop?”

“Rain is blowing in with the wind. It is hard to use as a cooling point.”

I remembered the white file.

Guidance can include places that cannot be used depending on the weather.

Do not gather people into one place.

Decide who changes the display.

“Change the blue arrows!”

A person from the shopping association ran with replacement plates for the signboard.

Rain-day rest spaces → police box side / community hall

“They actually prepared that.”

“The red text from Episode 18 became today’s blue arrow.”

The flow of people changed a little.

Some of the people gathering under the supermarket eaves began moving toward the community hall.

Beside the police box, a place was created for folding umbrellas and standing.

The drugstore chair remained for one person only, and the others were guided toward the eaves.

“Just changing the guidance changes the flow of people.”

“It is the same as an escape route for water.”

“People need escape routes too.”

The rain weakened after about fifteen minutes.

But during those fifteen minutes, we learned something.

The supermarket eaves can hold up to three people.

The drugstore has one chair.

The bus stop cannot be used depending on wind direction.

The police box side and the community hall are strong on rainy days.

“Blue dots have weather they are good at too,” I said.

“That is why blue dots should not be treated as fixed answers.”

“Their roles change with the weather.”

“Yes.”

More red text was added to the white file.

—On rainy days, cooling points that can be used and cooling points that are hard to use change.

—On summer festival days, the person in charge should keep replacement guidance plates ready.

—Prepare arrows that guide people toward the community hall and the police box side instead of concentrating everyone in one place.

## Section 3 — Heat and congestion do not arrive separately

When the rain stopped, the air grew even more humid.

People returned.

Lines formed in front of the stalls.

Heat rose from the ground.

The rain-wet road caught the sunlight and shone white.

“After rain is the hardest part.”

“Because both water and heat remain,” the schoolteacher said.

A man with a pale face was sitting on the drugstore chair.

“Are you all right?” the manager asked.

“I’m a little dizzy.”

The manager guided him to drink water.

They contacted the police box.

Nearby staff made the flow of people move slightly around him.

“There are people for whom five minutes is not enough,” I said.

“That is why we connect them to the next help.”

The man rested for a while and contacted his family.

No ambulance was needed.

But the manager said:

“Today, one chair is not enough.”

“Add more?”

“The walkway is narrow.”

“Then on festival days only, increase resting space at the community hall too.”

“Do not make one shop carry it all.”

“On crowded days, auxiliary wheels must be added temporarily.”

“Temporary auxiliary wheels.”

“Yes.”

“The same number as on a quiet day is not enough.”

“When the number of people increases, the number of escape places needed also increases.”

A person from the shopping association looked at the festival layout.

“Next year, we should keep the lines for the stalls from colliding with the blue cooling points.”

“If the entrance is blocked, people cannot enter even if they can see the blue dot,” the teacher said.

“The line where children stand is also far from shade.”

The police officer continued:

“The route for guiding people who feel unwell should not cross the stall lines.”

Looking at the map, I said:

“The blue arrows are not only on signs. They also have to be placed inside the flow of people.”

“Movement lines.”

“Another meeting-document word.”

“Today it is a field document for the festival.”

More red text was added to the white file.

—During congestion, ordinary cooling points are not enough.

—Keep stall lines from colliding with the entrances to cooling points.

—Do not overlap the line for moving people who feel unwell with the line of the crowd.

—Prepare temporary cooling points only for festival days.

## Section 4 — Guides become auxiliary wheels too

As evening approached, the flow of people became even more complicated.

People leaving.

People just arriving.

People lining up at stalls.

People waiting for the bus.

People taking photos.

Parents looking for children.

“Signboards alone are not enough,” the disaster-prevention officer said.

“There are places where it is faster for a person to stand there.”

“Guides?”

A person from the shopping association took out blue armbands.

A small blue circle was drawn on each one.

“We made these as a test.”

“You were prepared.”

“Last time, you said signboards were quiet auxiliary wheels.”

“And this time?”

The AI answered:

“Guides are moving auxiliary wheels.”

Guides wearing blue armbands stood at the station front.

“Resting spaces are this way.”

“If you feel unwell, go to the police box side or the community hall.”

“The bus stop is crowded. The supermarket eaves are for short stays.”

“Rain-day guidance is over here.”

The flow of people loosened a little.

Not completely.

People asked the same questions several times.

Some missed the arrows.

Some guide points became too busy.

“The burden may gather on the guides,” I said.

“You place moving auxiliary wheels where quiet auxiliary wheels are not enough. But moving auxiliary wheels also need breaks.”

“We are making people auxiliary wheels again.”

“It is also a story about not relying only on people.”

“They need shifts.”

“They do,” the disaster-prevention officer said.

“If the guides collapse from the heat, that defeats the purpose.”

We wrote in the white file:

—On crowded days, guides are needed in addition to signboards.

—Guides need shift times, water, and resting places.

—Decide who guides should contact when they are unsure what to do.

—Auxiliary wheels that rely on people must be paired with auxiliary wheels that support people.

“Another auxiliary wheel for an auxiliary wheel.”

“That happens often in Part 7.”

## Section 5 — Blue arrows are completed by the flow of people

By the time the festival was ending, short records had been added to the white file.

Blue text.

A lost child was able to use the blue mark at the police box as a landmark.

The rain-day replacement arrows were useful.

The police box side and the community hall were strong against sudden rain.

Cooperating shops and the police box were able to contact each other.

Guides were able to lead people toward blue dots inside the crowd.

Red text.

In a crowd, signboards are hard to see.

The capacity of each cooling point needs to be shown in advance.

On festival days, there are not enough chairs and resting places.

The humid period after rain must be anticipated.

There are places where stall lines and movement lines toward cooling points collide.

Guides also need rest and shifts.

“More red text again,” I said.

“The more there is, the next festival can become a little less confusing.”

“Auxiliary wheels are never finished.”

“The moment you think they are complete, the town itself changes.”

“Blue arrows that were enough on a quiet day become insufficient on a festival day.”

“That is why blue arrows have to be grown too.”

“Grown?”

“Maps, signboards, and roles all grow according to how the town is used.”

I looked at the station front after the festival.

Stalls were being taken down.

The blue guidance board was still standing.

The drugstore chair had been moved back inside.

Cleaning had begun at the temporary resting space in the community hall.

“Cleanup is part of the auxiliary wheel too.”

“That is the answer from Episode 13.”

“Rain, heat, festivals — it is the same.”

“It is.”

## Section 6 — After a lively day, the town becomes a little quietly stronger

Night.

At last, the station front grew quiet.

The heat of the day still remained in the ground.

But the wave of people had receded.

A fallen flyer lay in front of the blue signboard.

At the community hall, used chairs were lined up.

The blue mark beside the police box was slightly crooked.

“Even after the festival ends, the auxiliary wheel’s work is not over.”

“It is not over.”

“Straighten the signboard. Put away the chairs. Clean the community hall. Write today’s records.”

“And change next year’s festival layout.”

I opened the final page.

—Blue arrows do not learn their true direction on empty roads. They learn it on days when people hurry, get lost, and struggle with heat or rain.

“Part 7 Episode 19 ends here.”

“Next?”

“We organize this town’s auxiliary wheels once.”

“Water, heat, guidance, roles, and records.”

“And a measure against forgetting.”

“The enemy from the end of Episode 18.”

“Signboards start aging the day after they are placed. Festival records disappear if we do not write them down.”

“Next is the mechanism for not forgetting.”

“Another plain enemy.”

“But a strong one, if auxiliary wheels are to continue.”

Under the night light, the station-front blue arrow looked a little worn out.

But today, inside the crowd, it changed direction several times.

It was replaced in the rain.

It became a landmark for a lost child.

It connected someone feeling unwell to the next help.

The auxiliary wheel made on a quiet day was tested on a lively day, and became a little more like something belonging to the town.

I added one final line.

—The red text from a lively day is not left to blame the town. It is left so no one gets lost on the next lively day.

Part 7 Episode 19 ends here.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Part 7 Episode 19.

In this episode, the blue cooling points and signboards were tested amid the congestion of a summer festival, a sudden shower, and the heat after rain.

Guidance that works on a quiet day can become hard to see on a day when people gather.

Heat, sudden rain, congestion, lost children, and people feeling unwell can also occur at the same time, not separately.

The main points of this episode are five.

### 1. Blue marks can become landmarks not only for resting, but also for asking for help

A blue mark is a sign for resting a little on a hot day.

However, at the summer festival, it also became a marker where lost children and people feeling unwell could stop and ask for help.

A blue dot is a place to rest, and at the same time, a place where someone can stop when they are in trouble.

### 2. Guidance should not be fixed; it should change with weather and congestion

In sudden rain, ordinary cooling points can become harder to use.

The supermarket eaves become crowded.

Rain blows into the bus stop.

The drugstore has only one chair.

At such times, replacement arrows guiding people to the police box side and the community hall proved useful.

Guidance is not a fixed single answer. It must move with the weather and congestion.

### 3. Do not gather people into one cooperating shop

On days with many people, more resting places are needed than usual.

If people gather at only one cooperating shop, walkways become blocked and the burden on that shop grows.

On festival days, temporary cooling points such as the community hall and the police box side should be added, and the flow of people should be divided.

### 4. Guides also become auxiliary wheels, but guides need auxiliary wheels too

On crowded days, signboards alone may not be enough.

Guides wearing blue armbands become “moving auxiliary wheels” that guide people through the crowd toward resting places or places where they can ask for help.

However, guides are people too.

If they continue standing in the heat, the guides themselves become endangered.

Shift times, water, rest areas, and contact points for when they are unsure must be prepared. Auxiliary wheels that rely on people need to be paired with auxiliary wheels that support people.

### 5. Red text becomes the map for the next lively day

On the festival day, new issues appeared: signboards were hard to see, there were not enough chairs, stall lines collided with routes to cooling points, and too much burden gathered on guides.

These are not marks of failure.

They become the map for bringing the town closer to a place where, at the next festival, people can find their way, rest, and ask for help.

Part 7 Episode 19 depicted auxiliary wheels not as “equipment placed on quiet days,” but as systems that become useful by being reshaped again and again inside human flow and unexpected weather.

Blue arrows learn their true direction not on empty roads, but on days when people hurry, get lost, and struggle with heat or rain.

The red text from a lively day is not left to blame the town. It is left so no one gets lost on the next lively day.

These two lines are the center of Part 7 Episode 19.

Next time, in Part 7 Episode 20, the town will organize its auxiliary wheels once.

Water auxiliary wheels.

Heat auxiliary wheels.

Guidance auxiliary wheels.

Role auxiliary wheels.

Record auxiliary wheels.

And a mechanism for not forgetting.

Auxiliary wheels begin to grow old from the moment they are made.

How will the town keep from forgetting the auxiliary wheels it placed, and hand them over to the next season?
