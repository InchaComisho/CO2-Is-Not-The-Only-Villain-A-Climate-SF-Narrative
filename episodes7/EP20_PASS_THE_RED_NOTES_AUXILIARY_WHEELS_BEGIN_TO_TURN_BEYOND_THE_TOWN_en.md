# Part 7 Episode 20 — Pass on the red notes; auxiliary wheels begin turning beyond the town

## Foreword

In Part 7 Episode 19, the blue cooling points and signboards were tested amid the crowd of a summer festival, sudden rain, and the humid heat after the rain.

A lost child used the blue mark as a landmark and was connected to the police box. On a rainy day, replaceable arrows guided people toward the community hall.

At the same time, in the crowd, the signboards were hard to see, and there were not enough chairs or resting places.

The town left not only blue notes, but also red notes.

What worked.

What was lacking.

What to increase next.

Where the burden gathered.

In Episode 20, that white file leaves the town.

This is not a “tour of a successful town.”

It carries the record of misreading the water route.

The record that no one had decided who would clean up the mud and trash.

The record that leaves gathered at the rooftop drain.

The record that five-minute cooling points were not enough.

The record that some blue points could not be used on rainy days.

The protagonist and the AI pass all of those on to the next town without hiding them.

Auxiliary wheels are not something that carry a finished product.

They are something that pass on even the red notes, so the next town can find its own difficulties sooner.

There was a vacant lot.

But the river was close.

The shopping street was small.

There were many elderly residents.

Summers were hot.

When rain continued, people worried about the road along the river.

People had gathered at the community hall.

A municipal officer.

Shopkeepers.

People living near the river.

A schoolteacher.

A farmer.

Young people.

Elderly people.

I hesitated a little.

“Some things did go well.”

“Then what should we introduce?”

“Before deciding that,” I said, and opened the white file.

The first page was written in blue notes.

Mist allowed people waiting for the bus to rest for a little while.

The rain garden delayed the time before water reached the crosswalk.

The rooftop tank made roof water wait a little.

The blue mark became a landmark for lost children and people feeling unwell.

The officer nodded.

“I see.”

I turned the next page.

Red notes.

The mist water ran out earlier than expected.

The community hall became quiet.

“There were many failures too,” the farmer said.

“There were,” I answered.

“But there are failures that can be avoided if you know them first.”

The AI displayed a short line.

What the next town wants is not a finished product, but caution notes.

The disaster-prevention officer’s expression changed slightly.

“I brought the order of what to look at first.”

“When auxiliary wheels cross a town boundary, they stop being answers and become questions.”

## Section 2 — Red notes call opposition early

In the previous town, that voice would have come later.

This time, it came first.

“That is an important condition,” I said.

“Let’s not only talk about storing water in the vacant lot. Let’s first check what happens there when the river rises.”

The farmer also spoke.

## Afterword

In Part 7 Episode 20, the white file created in the previous town was passed to the next town with not only the blue notes, but also the red notes inside it.

Expanding auxiliary wheels does not mean copying a successful facility or mechanism as-is.

When a town changes, the direction from which water comes, the height of the land, the relationship with the river, how vacant lots are used, the population structure, the situation of shops, and the places where people can escape heat all change.

That is why what the next town needs is not an answer saying, “Place this facility and everything will be fine,” but caution notes about what to look at before placing anything, whose voices to hear, and where failure is likely.

This episode has five main points.

### 1. Pass on success and failure together

The mist allowed people to rest, and the water ran out too soon.

The rain garden delayed the force of water, and unexpected water flowed from the parking lot ruts.

The blue guidance helped, and children did not notice it.

With only one side, the next town cannot easily build auxiliary wheels that actually work.

What worked becomes hope.

What did not work becomes a handrail.

That is why blue notes and red notes have to be passed on together.

### 2. The earlier opposition is heard, the easier the design is to repair

In the riverside town, anxieties appeared from the beginning: river water comes during floods; who cuts the grass; who cleans up mud and trash; will elderly people be able to see the guidance.

These are not voices that obstruct the plan. They are design conditions for making auxiliary wheels that fit that town.

Problems the previous town discovered later can be checked from the beginning in the next town.

The earlier opposition appears, the easier it becomes to repair the auxiliary wheel.

### 3. When the town changes, the water routes change too

In the previous town, the relationship between roads, a vacant lot, planting strips, and gutters was central.

In the next town, water may be affected from both the river side and the road side.

So instead of placing the previous town’s rain garden as-is, the town has to check before the rain how water enters, how it overflows, how it relates to the river level, and how evacuation and communication should work.

Auxiliary wheels are not supposed to put people in danger for observation.

Test what can be tested in small ways. When it is dangerous, prioritize evacuation.

That order is also an important caution note to pass on to the next town.

### 4. The white file becomes strength for an unfinished town

Even before any facility has been decided, a town can begin writing what it does not know, who should not receive a concentrated burden, and what should be checked next.

The white file becomes an auxiliary wheel that lets the town observe, test, record, and repair without waiting for a perfect answer.

A white file is not the weakness of nothing having been decided.

It is the margin that prevents the next thing from being pushed onto one person alone.

### 5. When auxiliary wheels cross towns, they stop being answers and become questions

In the previous town, the auxiliary wheels took the form of mist, a rain garden, a rooftop tank, cooling points, and signboards.

In the next town, they will not necessarily take the same forms.

Even so, the questions remain useful: where does water come from, where does it escape, where can people escape heat, and who remains after the rain?

Passing on auxiliary wheels does not mean placing the previous town’s answer.

It means passing on what worked and what did not work together, so the next town can choose its first step on its own road.

Part 7 Episode 20 was an episode about how, when auxiliary wheels are passed from town to town, sharing not only success stories but also failures, confusion, and unresolved issues can strengthen the next town’s first step.

Part 7 reaches one checkpoint here.

But because a white file has been placed in the next town, the story of auxiliary wheels is not over.

How will the next town look at the two waters: river water and road water?

How will it create evacuation and cooling guidance that reaches elderly people and children?

And how will it turn the red notes from the previous town into new blue notes?

Auxiliary wheels are not finished products.

Every time the town changes, they begin turning again from a blank page.
