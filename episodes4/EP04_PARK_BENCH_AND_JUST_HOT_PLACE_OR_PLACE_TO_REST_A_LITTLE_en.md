# Part 4 Episode 4 — The park bench, the place that is only hot, and the place where one can rest a little

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episodes 2 and 3, we compared the summer of the world line where little changed on a large scale with the summer of the world line that bent slightly, using the station-front rotary and the shopping-street arcade as our stages.

In Episode 4, we move to a quieter place: a small residential park and its benches.

In the world line where little changed, it is simply a hot place where shade is insufficient.

In the world line that bent slightly, it becomes a place where people can rest a little during dangerous heat.

This episode follows the protagonist and the AI as they look at that difference from the distance of an everyday walking route.

---

“Station fronts and shopping streets are hot, sure.”

I closed the screen and exhaled lightly.

“But the place I’m most concerned about is the park.”

‘The small park in the middle of the residential area?’

“Right.”

Between apartment buildings and detached houses.

A slide, swings, a sandbox, and a drinking fountain.

Two or three benches. That kind of park.

A place where children play.

A place where older people rest during a walk.

A place where people walking dogs stop for a moment.

“Let’s make Part 4 Episode 4 about that.”

‘A good subject.’

“It’s much smaller than the station front or the shopping street.”

‘The smaller the place, the closer it is to daily life.’

“That’s exactly it.”

## Section 1 — The “only hot park” in the world line where little changed

Just before noon, there were only a few people in the park.

The metal part of the slide was already hot to the touch.

The chains of the swings felt slightly scorched on the sunny side.

The sand in the sandbox was dry, pale, and holding heat.

“The benches are mostly in the sun, aren’t they?”

‘They are.’

I opened the reality-line log.

— There are two benches beside the sandbox.

— There are trees, but because of the position of their trunks and branches, their shade misses the benches at summer noon.

— The backrests and seats are struck by direct sunlight.

“It takes a bit of courage to sit there.”

‘A place made for sitting is too hot to sit on.’

“That is quietly painful.”

An older person who had stopped by the park during a walk paused in front of the bench.

To sit.

Or not to sit.

After hesitating for a moment, they moved toward the soil beneath a tree, as if deciding not to sit today after all.

There is shade.

But there is nowhere to sit in it.

There is a bench.

But there is no shade over it.

‘What about the drinking fountain?’

“The first water from the tap is usually lukewarm.”

— When the tap is turned, the first water that comes out has been warmed inside the metal pipe.

— After letting it run for a while, water that is not cold but at least better finally appears.

— People use it little by little on their hands and necks to cope.

A parent with a child catches water in their hands and gently presses it against the back of the child’s neck.

Someone walking a dog fills a bottle and pours it into a small bowl for the dog.

It is not that no one is doing anything.

Everyone is improvising within what they can do.

But the park itself does not help very much.

‘This is a park with almost no auxiliary wheels as infrastructure.’

“The position of the trees and the direction of the shade are left to chance.”

— Even when dangerous heat is forecast, the positions of the park benches do not change.

— Even when a heatstroke alert is issued, nothing automatically appears in this park.

— There is shade, but it does not overlap with seating.

— There is water, but it is not integrated with a resting place.

‘That is the park in the world line where little changed.’

“A place that is only hot.”

I looked at the bench from the park entrance.

It was supposed to be a place to rest.

But on this day, that bench had become a place that made people hesitate over whether they could sit at all.

## Section 2 — The “resting park” in the world line that bent slightly

‘Now let us look at the park in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI said this.

I opened the right-side log.

Then I wrote one first line.

— From here onward, this is the world line of a park where people can rest a little during dangerous heat.

The same residential area.

The same slide, swings, and sandbox.

A park of about the same size.

However, in this world line, the relationship among the bench, trees, and drinking fountain has been reviewed once.

“For example, this is a world where this kind of work was done.”

— A few years earlier, municipal staff and a landscape contractor spread out the park plan.

— They checked where shadows fell during midsummer morning, noon, and afternoon.

— Based on that, they moved the bench by only two meters and added just one new tree.

‘Not a major redevelopment, but a small rearrangement.’

“Exactly.”

— The bench is not completely in shade.

— But during the hours when dangerous heat is expected, about half of the seat is outside direct sunlight.

— Part of the backrest is also shaded, and the feeling of being scorched the instant one sits down is slightly weaker.

“That much could happen in reality.”

‘It could. But unless someone looks at the hours of shade, it will not be noticed.’

“That’s the point.”

There are things one cannot know by looking only at a park plan.

Where shadows fall at summer noon.

Where older people stop.

Where children drink water.

Where dogs pause.

Unless someone sees all of that on site, the bench does not move two meters.

‘What about mist?’

“I hesitated between a world with it and a world without it… but here I’ll place just one small auxiliary device.”

— A low pole stands near the drinking fountain.

— At its tip is a single small mist nozzle.

— However, it does not spray unconditionally just because a heatstroke alert has been issued.

— It runs weakly and only for short periods while checking temperature, humidity, wind conditions, water quality, and use of the park.

‘It does not cool the whole park.’

“It only creates a place near the drinking fountain where people can stay a little longer.”

— The fine mist is not strong enough to cover all the playground equipment.

— To avoid wetting the ground too much, it stops when the wind is too weak or humidity is too high.

— Nozzle cleaning and water-quality management are included in monthly inspection items.

‘If it becomes ordinary infrastructure, management must also be ordinary.’

“The same thing we saw at the station front and the shopping street.”

Even in this world line, the park does not become cool.

The temperature is high.

The slide still gets hot.

The sunny sandbox is hot.

Even so.

— An older person wets their hands and face at the drinking fountain, receives a little weak mist, then sits down on the shaded bench.

— A parent with a small child can say, “Let’s rest a little before going home.”

— Someone walking a dog lets it drink water, checks the temperature near its feet, and waits a little longer in the shade.

— The park itself changes its role slightly, from a space doing nothing to a space where people can rest a little in the heat.

‘The air temperature itself remains similarly high.’

“Of course.”

‘But whether there is a place to rest is different.’

“That is probably the park’s auxiliary wheel.”

## Section 3 — What the protagonist says after placing the two parks side by side

I placed the two parks side by side in my mind.

On the left.

A bench in the sun.

A lukewarm drinking fountain.

People staying on their feet, thinking, “Maybe I shouldn’t sit today.”

A park with shade, but no place to sit within it.

On the right.

A park where the bench was moved slightly after checking how shadows fell.

A park with a small mist pole near the drinking fountain, operating only under conditions.

A park where someone sits down because they feel they can stay there a little.

‘How are they different?’

The AI asked.

“In the left-side park, simply being in the park becomes quite a challenge.”

I spoke slowly.

“For older people.”

“For small children.”

“For dogs, too.”

‘And on the right?’

“In the right-side park, whether one can take a breath here changes slightly.”

— During a walk, the time one can think, “Maybe I can stay here a little longer,” becomes longer.

— Before going home, one more option appears: drink water in the park, sit in the shade, receive weak mist if needed, then return.

‘Auxiliary wheels do not protect life forever.’

“But they may slightly change the number of people who do not collapse here today.”

In this story, the park’s auxiliary wheel is not something that requires a huge budget or a huge project.

Moving the bench and tree position by two meters.

Adding one small mist pole that runs only under conditions near the drinking fountain.

Deciding who inspects it and under what conditions it stops.

That is how small the story is.

‘Even so, that small difference bends the feel of daily life a little.’

“Both parks are possible parks.”

I added that.

“This is the log of the park where the layout was not changed and nothing was added.”

‘Yes.’

“And this is the log of the park where the layout was changed slightly and just one thing was added.”

— Which one is right is something for readers to decide.

— For myself, I want to leave behind the park with one added element as the better auxiliary wheel.

‘Part 4 Episode 4 looked at an auxiliary wheel in one of the quieter places within the city’s summer.’

“Right.”

I imagined the real park.

Strong midday light.

Playground equipment gone hot.

A bench in the sun.

People standing in the shade, trying to rest a little.

That does not need a grand future city.

It does not need luxurious equipment.

Only overlapping shade with seating.

Bringing the place to drink water closer to the place to rest.

Creating a place where the body can cool down, just a little, on days of dangerous heat.

That alone may change a park from a place that is only hot into a place where one can rest a little.

A world line can begin bending from a bench moved two meters.

Part 4 Episode 4 ends here.

## Afterword

Part 4 Episode 4 used a small residential park and its benches to compare the “only hot park” of the world line where little changed and the “park where one can rest a little during dangerous heat” of the world line that bent slightly.

In the world line where little changed, the bench is positioned where it enters full sun at summer noon.

There is shade, but there is nowhere to sit within it.

There is also a drinking fountain, but the first water from the tap is lukewarm because it has been warmed inside the pipe.

Even when dangerous heat is forecast or a heatstroke alert is issued, the equipment and layout of the park do not particularly change.

Older people, parents with small children, and people walking dogs each improvise in their own ways to cope with the heat.

But the park space itself still leaves most heat response to individuals.

In the world line that bent slightly, what changes is not a major redevelopment but a small rearrangement.

Check where shadows fall in midsummer morning, noon, and afternoon.

Then move the bench by only two meters.

Add just one new tree.

Even that much allows the place to sit and the shade to overlap a little.

Even if it is not complete shade, having half the seat out of direct sunlight changes how long one can rest.

In addition, one small mist pole is installed beside the drinking fountain.

However, it does not spray unconditionally in response to a heatstroke alert.

It operates weakly and only for short periods while checking temperature, humidity, wind conditions, water quality, and use of the space.

If humidity is too high, wind is too weak and droplets linger, or the ground becomes too wet, operation is reduced or stopped.

Nozzle cleaning, water-quality management, inspection, and maintenance costs are also required.

Mist is not used by itself to cool the whole park, but as an auxiliary device combined with the drinking fountain, shade, and bench to create a place where people can rest a little.

The auxiliary wheel described in this episode is very small.

Move a bench two meters.

Add one tree.

Install one small mist pole near the drinking fountain that operates only under conditions.

But that small difference may change whether an older person on a walk, a parent with a small child, or someone walking a dog can rest here for a moment.

The park’s auxiliary wheel does not erase extreme heat.

Playground equipment still becomes hot, and the outdoor temperature remains high.

Even so, it can change a place where one thinks, “I won’t sit today,” into a place where one thinks, “I’ll sit a little before going home.”

In Part 4 so far, we have seen fragments of urban summer—station fronts, shopping streets, and parks—through a double exposure of the world line where little changed and the world line that bent slightly.

This park is an especially quiet and small auxiliary wheel among them.

It is not a huge project.

But urban heat also appears in such small places.

And perhaps a world line can begin bending even from a bench moved two meters or a single tree added.

In the following episodes, Part 4 will continue looking at small places in daily life—community centers, shelters, apartment balconies, and indoor rooms—through the two world lines.
