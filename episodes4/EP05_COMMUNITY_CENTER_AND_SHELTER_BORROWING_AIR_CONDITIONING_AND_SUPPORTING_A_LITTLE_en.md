# Part 4 Episode 5 — Community centers and shelters: “a place to borrow air conditioning” and “a place that supports a little”

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episode 4, we used a small residential park as our stage and compared two world lines: a park that was only hot, and a park where people could rest a little.

In Episode 5, we turn to a place that feels a little more public: the community center and the shelter.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, it is “a place to borrow air conditioning on extremely hot days.”

In the world line that bent slightly, it becomes “a place that supports local life a little during dangerous heat and heavy rain.”

This episode follows the protagonist and the AI as they look at that difference from between institutions and everyday life.

---

“On days when the heat is unbearable, some people go to the community center, don’t they?”

Sitting in an air-conditioned room, I muttered while looking at the screen.

“But community centers can be hard to understand. What are they really for?”

‘They are meeting places, places for club activities, places for local events, shelters, and places to borrow air conditioning on hot days.’

“They have too many roles.”

‘And because they have many roles, their role during heat and heavy rain is not always put into clear words.’

“Then Episode 5 should look at that through two world lines.”

‘A summer of community centers and shelters.’

## Section 1 — The community center that only lends air conditioning in the world line where little changed

On a morning when an extremely hot day was forecast, a sheet from the local government was posted on the community center bulletin board.

— Heatstroke alert in effect.

— Older people, children, and those with health conditions should stay in cool places.

Beneath that, in small letters, it said: “The community center is open as usual.”

“The air conditioning is definitely on.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— In the Japanese-style room and meeting room, ceiling air conditioners are running.

— A few older residents sit in the corner of a room that happens to be free.

— By the window, someone with a towel wrapped around a cold pack quietly reads a book.

‘As a place where people can borrow air conditioning, it is functioning.’

“Yeah.”

‘But not much more than that is visible.’

“That’s the issue.”

A staff member at the community center says, “It is hot today, isn’t it?”

But it does not look as if there is a clear line for what they can do and how far they should go.

— They encourage people to drink water.

— But there is no system for distributing drinks or salt supplements.

— Even if they check on someone’s condition, they cannot make medical judgments.

— If someone looks unwell, they speak to them, but deciding when to connect them to emergency care tends to depend on the situation and the person present.

‘It depends on the goodwill and experience of the staff.’

“That’s how it looks.”

— The community center is open.

— The air conditioning works.

— But the words are missing: what is this place supposed to do on a day of dangerous heat?

“What about the shelter line?”

‘Heavy rain.’

On a day when heavy rain is forecast, the community center appears in municipal notices as a “temporary evacuation place.”

— Whether people actually come depends on that day’s rain and wind, and on the judgment of each resident.

— If they do come, the support mainly consists of blankets, water, and minimal information.

— For residents, it can be unclear whether this is a place to calm down for a while, a place to move on from to another shelter, or how long it will remain open.

‘In the world line where little changed, the community center remains a candidate site.’

“The air conditioning exists, and the shelter function exists, at least in form.”

‘Yes.’

“But the reason people may come here, and what support they can receive here, are still vague.”

‘That is the community center in the reality line.’

## Section 2 — The community center and shelter that support a little in the world line that bent slightly

‘Now let us open the community center in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI said.

I opened the right-hand log.

Then I wrote one line first.

— From here, this is the world line of a community center and shelter that supports people a little during extreme heat and heavy rain.

The same town.

The same community center.

The same kind of equipment.

But in this world line, the roles of “hot days” and “heavy-rain days” have been put into clear words at least once.

“For example, this is a world where these arrangements exist.”

— On days when dangerous heat is expected, a sign at the entrance reads: “Heat-relief rest stop.”

— Inside, simple panels explain “how to drink water,” “how to use air conditioning,” “how to check in on neighbors,” and “where to ask for help if you feel unwell.”

— Near the reception desk, there is drinking water people may use freely, along with small salt-supplement supplies that people who need them can take after checking the labels.

‘Not simply handing things out?’

“Right.”

— Some people need salt supplementation, while others must be careful because of illness or dietary restrictions.

— Therefore, the staff say, “If you need these, please check the label before using them.”

— If someone seems unwell, they are not pushed to endure it; if necessary, staff connect them to a medical institution or an emergency consultation service.

‘The staff of the community center do not make medical judgments.’

“That matters.”

‘But they can speak to people and guide them.’

“That alone changes things.”

In the Japanese-style room, there is space to sit on the floor.

— Two or three simple mats are laid out so older people can lie down and rest.

— Chairs with backs are lined up for those who are more comfortable sitting on chairs.

— The staff have a set phrase: “A heat alert has been issued today. Please do not push yourself. Rest here for a while if you need to.”

‘A little information and procedure have been added to the place where people borrow air conditioning.’

“That is the image.”

‘What about the shelter line?’

“In this world, the role during heavy rain has also been organized once.”

— On a day when heavy rain is forecast, a simple diagram titled “About tonight’s heavy rain” is posted on the community center bulletin board.

— The location of the nearby river.

— Places in the town where water tends to collect.

— Approximate times by which households with older people or small children should consider moving early.

— Things to bring when evacuating.

— These are shown in rough maps and plain words.

“Even information alone can make people move a little more easily.”

‘But if the information is outdated, it is dangerous.’

“So the update date and source are written on it.”

— If evacuation actually begins, the community center has a reception list to keep track of who has come.

— However, it is not a board anyone can read. It is a form or device managed by staff, recording only the minimum necessary information.

— The staff have a first phrase to use: “We do not yet know how the situation will develop, but let us calm down here for now.”

‘It is not unnecessarily dramatic.’

“Yeah.”

‘But what the place is for has become a little clearer.’

“That is the community center in the bent world line.”

Even in this world line, the community center does not perform miracles.

It does not protect everyone.

The air-conditioned rooms have limited space.

The number of staff is limited.

Opening hours are limited.

Even so:

— People know they are allowed to come here.

— People can see what they can do here.

— People know whom to speak to first when they are in trouble.

The community center has changed its role slightly: from a place to borrow air conditioning into a place where people can steady themselves a little during heat and heavy rain.

## Afterword

Part 4 Episode 5 used the community center and shelter as its stage, comparing the community center that only lends air conditioning in the world line where little changed with the community center and shelter that support people a little during heat and heavy rain in the world line that bent slightly.

In the world line where little changed, even when an extremely hot day is forecast, the notice at the community center only says that it is open.

There are air-conditioned rooms.

Neighbors can come to cool down.

However, what people can do there, how far staff should speak to them, and where to connect someone who is unwell have not been clearly put into words.

On heavy-rain days as well, the community center may be named as a temporary evacuation place.

But for residents, it remains hard to see when they should go, what they can find out there, and how long they can stay.

The equipment exists.

The air conditioning exists.

It is also a candidate shelter.

But its role has not been sufficiently translated into everyday-life language.

In the world line that bent slightly, the role of the community center becomes a little more verbalized.

On days when dangerous heat is expected, a sign reading “Heat-relief rest stop” appears at the entrance.

Inside, simple panels explain hydration, use of air conditioning, checking in on neighbors, and consultation options when someone feels unwell.

Near reception, there is drinking water and small salt-supplement supplies that people who need them can use after checking the labels.

However, staff do not make medical judgments.

They speak to people who seem unwell and, if necessary, connect them to emergency consultation or medical institutions.

That boundary has also been organized beforehand.

On heavy-rain days, a rough map is posted showing nearby rivers, places where water tends to collect, timing for evacuation, and things to bring.

When evacuation begins, a reception list is prepared to keep track of those who have arrived.

But names and addresses are not written on a board visible to everyone; the minimum necessary information is recorded on paper or a device managed by staff.

Even here, the equipment itself has not changed greatly.

What differs is that what the place is for has become a little clearer.

It is not merely a matter of opening an air-conditioned room. People troubled by heat know they may come here.

During heavy rain, people can check information here once and make their next decision calmly.

The staff also understand, at least minimally, what to say and where to connect people beyond their own role.

That is the auxiliary wheel of the community center.

A community center is not a huge disaster-prevention facility.

It cannot protect everyone.

The air-conditioned rooms are limited, staff numbers are limited, and opening hours are constrained.

Even so, simply rephrasing its role from “a place to borrow air conditioning” to “a place that supports people a little during heat and heavy rain” can slightly change residents’ anxiety.

In Part 4, we have looked at small urban summer places across two world lines: the station front, the shopping street, the park, and now the community center.

This community center is especially an auxiliary wheel of words and procedures.

It is not major construction.

It is not flashy equipment.

But a sign at the entrance, panels inside, words of guidance, a reception list, and a rough map can slightly change the role the same community center can play during heat and heavy rain.

Perhaps world lines begin bending not only through equipment, but also through how the role of a place is put into words.
