# Part 4 Episode 10 — Riverside residential districts, the town that keeps the waterside away, and the town that lives while watching it

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episode 9, we looked at two world lines through downstream retention basins and levees: a town that tries to push water back as much as possible, and a town that accepts a little.

Episode 10 continues that flow by shifting the viewpoint to a residential district along the river.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, people live in a town that keeps the waterside distant.

In the world line that bent slightly, people live in a town that watches the waterside while preparing for emergencies.

This episode follows the protagonist and the AI as they look at the summer of people living beside the river, and at everyday life just before the signs of rising water appear.

## Section 1 — The town that keeps the waterside away in the world line where little changed

Inside the levee along the river, there is a narrow residential district.

The road follows the river.

But the windows of the houses are built so that they do not look toward the river as much as possible.

Entrances face the inner road, and on the river side there are many tall walls and hedges.

“It feels like the waterside is being kept away.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— The river is scenery for the town.

— But it is not something people look at every day.

— There is a walking and cycling path on the levee, but there are few people there during midsummer afternoons.

— The residents’ gaze turns not toward the river, but toward the inner road and ordinary life.

‘Keeping the waterside away is not evil in itself.’

“Right.”

Water is frightening.

A river may be beautiful on quiet days, but after heavy rain it takes on another face.

There are reasons why houses turn their backs on the water.

— Tall walls do more than block lines of sight.

— They also keep anxiety away from everyday life.

— Some people live more calmly each day by not seeing the river.

‘A distance for thinning out daily anxiety.’

“Yeah.”

On a summer day, there is a little wind along the river.

But that wind is not always cool.

Some days, it is hot and humid.

Even near a river, an extremely hot day is still an extremely hot day.

— Houses are closed up.

— People do not bring in much outside air; they protect the inside with air conditioning.

— Just because the river is near does not mean waterside air naturally enters everyday life.

‘What about preparation for rising water?’

“It exists.”

— Hazard maps have been distributed.

— There are signs showing evacuation routes.

— Residents can register for municipal disaster-prevention emails.

— Near the bridge, there is also a gauge that shows the water level.

“But as a feeling among residents, it is fairly passive.”

‘What do you mean?’

“A large flood that rarely comes — if it comes, they escape.”

‘Yes.’

“That is roughly where the feeling stops.”

— Usually, people do not look at the river much.

— After the rain grows strong, news and notifications suddenly make them think about the river.

— When information about rising water appears, they suddenly begin wondering how high it is now.

— But because they are not used to seeing the river’s normal height or current speed, it is hard to feel which changes are dangerous and how dangerous they are.

‘Going to look at the river during rising water is dangerous.’

“We absolutely have to write that down.”

I added one line to the log.

— When it is truly dangerous, people must not go directly to the river to look.

— But in a town that does not watch the river in ordinary times, people also find it harder to notice changes before danger arrives.

That the water has become slightly muddy.

That the grass on the revetment has been knocked down in an unusual way.

That after rain, the water takes longer than usual to fall.

Small changes like that tend to remain only with people who usually watch.

‘In the world line where little changed, the waterside remains something to keep away.’

“Yes.”

— The river has been removed from the background of everyday life.

— It becomes something people suddenly look at only when it becomes dangerous.

— As a result, even though preparations exist, the sense for reading waterside changes does not easily enter daily life.

“That is the log of the town that keeps the waterside away.”

## Section 2 — The town that lives while watching the waterside in the world line that bent slightly

‘Then let us look at the riverside residential district in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI said that.

I opened the log on the right.

Then I wrote a single line first.

— From here, this is the world line of a town that watches the waterside while preparing for emergencies.

The same riverside.

The same inside of the levee.

The same rows of houses.

But in this world line, the town does not only turn its back on the river. It has added small ways to see the river’s changes as part of everyday life.

“For example, like this.”

— Along the levee road, there are low benches and small information boards.

— The boards show, in simple diagrams, the normal water level, the heights that water tends to reach after rain, and rough points at which to check evacuation information.

— Nearby, there are also two-dimensional codes that lead to municipal water-level information and live cameras.

— Residents look at the river during walks and slowly learn the usual height and speed of the water.

‘The river becomes not something people avoid seeing, but something they usually know by sight.’

“Yes.”

— But this is not so that people go to look during heavy rain.

— The more dangerous it is, the more they avoid going to the site, and instead check live cameras and water-level information.

— Watching the river in ordinary times becomes material for judging when not to approach it during emergencies.

‘That difference is important.’

“Yeah.”

Watching the river does not mean going closer to danger.

It means knowing the ordinary river before danger arrives.

And once it becomes dangerous, it means looking at information rather than going to the site.

In this world line, that boundary is shared in the town.

— In some places, houses have changed river-side walls into low planting or see-through fences.

— But they are not opened without restraint.

— Some homes have decided where to keep flood boards, where to place emergency supplies, and what to carry upstairs.

— They see the river as scenery while also preparing how to close things off when it becomes dangerous.

‘Living while watching the waterside does not mean becoming defenseless.’

“It is more the opposite.”

— In a small park along the levee, a parent says to a child, “The water is higher than yesterday today.”

— That remark is not a trigger for going closer to the river, but for deciding not to approach it.

— On the neighborhood bulletin board, it says, “During heavy rain, do not go to look at the river. Check water-level information.”

— The river seen during ordinary walks and the water-level information on a screen slowly become connected inside the residents’ minds.

‘Even in this world line, rising water remains frightening.’

“Of course.”

— If heavy-rain news appears, the feeling of possible evacuation arrives in the same way.

— The existence of a levee does not mean everything is safe.

— The visibility of the waterside does not mean everything is safe either.

— But because everyday attention turns a little more toward the river, it becomes easier to notice earlier and to speak to neighbors earlier.

“Not forgetting the river by keeping it away.”

‘Yes.’

“Watching it without going too close.”

‘That is the distance of this world line.’

## Afterword

Part 4 Episode 10 used a riverside residential district as its stage, comparing the world line where little changed on a large scale, a town that lives by keeping the waterside away, with the world line that bent slightly, a town that watches the waterside while preparing for emergencies.

In the world line where little changed, the riverside residential district keeps the waterside somewhat distant from everyday life.

House windows and entrances face the inner road rather than the river.

On the river side, there are tall walls and hedges, and daily attention does not easily turn toward the river.

This is not necessarily bad.

A river can be scenery on quiet days, but after heavy rain it can also become dangerous.

Making the waterside less visible can help keep anxiety away from daily life.

However, when people do not usually watch the river, normal water levels, changes after rain, flow speed, and the condition of the revetment do not easily enter everyday awareness.

If people suddenly become concerned about the river after news of rising water appears, going directly to look at that moment is dangerous.

For that reason, the condition of usually not watching but becoming curious when it is dangerous also has weakness as preparation.

In the world line that bent slightly, the riverside town makes the waterside a little more visible within daily life.

Along the levee-side walking path, there are low benches and small information boards.

The boards show, in simple diagrams, normal water levels, changes after rain, and rough points at which to check evacuation information.

There are also two-dimensional codes that link to water-level information and live cameras.

Residents see the river during walks and gradually learn its ordinary height and flow speed.

However, this is not so that they go to the river during dangerous conditions.

It is the opposite.

Because they watch the river in ordinary times, they can more easily decide during heavy rain not to approach the site and instead check live cameras and water-level information.

Some houses open their river-side windows or fences a little, depending on the location.

But that does not mean opening defenselessly toward the river.

Where to keep flood boards.

Where to keep emergency supplies.

What to carry upstairs.

At what stage to close things during heavy rain.

Together with those preparations, the waterside is returned to daily scenery.

The auxiliary wheel drawn in this episode is not only equipment.

The habit of seeing the waterside.

Knowing the ordinary river.

The boundary of not approaching when it is dangerous.

The method of checking water-level information and live cameras.

The trigger for calling out to neighbors early.

These form an auxiliary wheel of sight and judgment.

In the riverside residential district, we described not only keeping the waterside away, but also a way of living that watches while preparing.

None of these stops heavy rain.

They only change the path of water and the way people face water a little.
