# Part 4 Episode 9 — The quietness of the retention basin, the town that pushes water back, and the town that accepts a little

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episodes 7 and 8, we looked at two world lines in the upper and middle parts of a watershed: fields that remembered how they collapsed and moved closer to sponge-like soil, and rain gardens that received rain once before sending it onward.

In Episode 9, the viewpoint moves farther downstream, to the retention basin and levee along the river.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, it is a town that tries to push water back with levees as much as possible, while barely using the retention basin.

In the world line that bent slightly, it is a town that accepts part of the water into the retention basin and shares the burden between the levee and the basin.

Facing forecasts of heavy rain from summer into autumn, the protagonist and the AI look at a quiet field beside the river and consider whether “how much water to accept” can become an auxiliary wheel.

## Section 1 — The town that pushes all the water back in the world line where little changed

Downstream along the river.

A wide, high levee stretches for a long distance between the town and the river.

Inside it, residential districts, factories, and roads are packed tightly together.

“That is an impressive levee.”

‘A wall to protect the town.’

“And it is actually necessary.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— Keep the river water inside the channel as much as possible.

— Do not let water enter the town side.

— Do not let it overtop the levee.

— That is the basic way this town is protected.

A little distance away, grassland spread out.

A slightly lower piece of land beside the river.

There was a simple fence and a single explanatory sign.

— This land temporarily stores river water during heavy rain.

That was what it said.

“But in this world line, the retention basin has become a field that is almost never used.”

— Usually, it is part of a walking route.

— A place where people walk their dogs.

— A place where children run around.

— A place where neighbors cross the grass on holidays.

— Residents say, “I have never seen water enter this place.”

‘There may also be a feeling that it was safer because it was not used.’

“I imagine so.”

Water entering the retention basin.

Those words contain a certain unease.

It feels as if water is approaching the town.

It sounds almost like something has failed.

So for many people, the retention basin has become “a place described on the signboard, but one we would rather not have to use.”

‘What is the role of the levee?’

“To push back as much water as possible.”

— Even when heavy rain is forecast, the basic idea is to keep the water from overtopping the levee.

— Whenever the river level rises, officials and residents watch whether the levee will hold.

— The gates and inlets to the retention basin are treated as something that should not be used except in a truly exceptional situation.

‘That itself is not wrong.’

“No.”

The levee is necessary.

If water enters the town, daily life and work stop.

Homes, factories, and roads do not recover easily.

So the idea of not letting water into town is only natural.

Even so.

— The destination of the water is basically “inside the river.”

— The levee is “a wall that keeps water out of town.”

— The retention basin is “a mostly inactive safety valve used only if something truly exceptional happens.”

— With every heavy rain, the burden concentrates on the river and the levee.

‘That is the downstream area in the world line where little changed.’

“A town trying to push all the water back.”

‘More precisely, a town trying to push back as much water as possible.’

“You’re right. Saying all of it is too much.”

I rewrote the note.

— Keep water out of the town as much as possible.

— To do that, concentrate the burden on the river and the levee.

— A retention basin exists, but it is not sufficiently shared as a place that is actually used within everyday life.

## Section 2 — The town that accepts a little water in the world line that bent slightly

‘Now let us look at the retention basin and levee in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI said.

I opened the log on the right.

— From here, this is the world line of a town that does not push all water back, but accepts part of it into the retention basin.

The same downstream river.

A similar levee, and a similar town spreading behind it.

However, in this world line, the use of the retention basin has been properly reconsidered once.

“For example, this is the kind of arrangement that exists.”

— When heavy rain is forecast, a diagram showing the river level, the capacity of the retention basin, and the condition of the inlet is shared between the municipality and residents.

— At which water-level stage, and from which point, water will be allowed into the retention basin.

— Before that happens, how users inside the retention basin will be evacuated.

— After water enters, how long entry will remain restricted.

— These procedures are decided in advance.

‘People have to be moved out before water is allowed in.’

“It is obvious, but important.”

— The retention basin is normally used for walking and exercise.

— That is exactly why restriction signs are posted once heavy rain is forecast.

— When the river level approaches the threshold, officials and local people check whether anyone remains inside the basin.

— Only after that, and only at the necessary stage, is water accepted.

‘This is not simply a matter of opening a gate.’

“Right.”

“To allow water in means temporarily handing that place over to the water side.”

— The grassland in the retention basin is maintained as “ground that may receive water.”

— The way water will flow and pool there is simulated in advance.

— How mud and debris will be cleared after the water drains is also decided as part of management.

— Around the basin, signs show the area where water may enter and the places people must not approach.

‘How does it look from the town’s side?’

“It is an everyday walking route, and also a place that accepts water on days of heavy rain.”

— When the river level begins to rise on a rainy day, water slowly spreads toward the retention basin as well.

— The town inside the levee is less likely to receive water.

— But water does indeed enter the retention basin.

— From bridges or safe high ground, people quietly watch what is happening.

‘What is the levee doing?’

“Rather than being a wall that pushes back all water…”

I thought for a moment.

“It becomes the boundary between the town and the place that accepts water.”

— The river and retention basin side belongs to the water side.

— The inside of the levee belongs to the life side.

— The levee separates the two while preventing all water from being forced only into the river channel.

‘The risk of heavy rain does not disappear in this world line either.’

“Of course not.”

— If the water level rises beyond the assumptions, both the retention basin and the levee become dangerous.

— If the decision to admit water is too late, the effect may be small.

— If it is too early, the basin may not have enough capacity left for the later stage of the storm.

— After the water drains, mud, debris, odor, sanitation management, and restoration work are all necessary.

— Accepting water into the retention basin does not erase the burden.

— It changes where and when part of the burden is placed.

‘Compared with a world line that relies only on levee height, the sharing of burden is different.’

“That is the auxiliary wheel here.”

The retention basin looks like a quiet field.

But in this world line, that quietness has a different meaning.

On ordinary days, it is the town’s margin.

On heavy-rain days, it is water’s margin.

The same land changes roles depending on season and water level.

Whether the town understands that seemed to divide the two world lines.

## Afterword

Part 4 Episode 9 used a downstream retention basin and levee as its stage, comparing the world line where little changed on a large scale—a town that tries to push water back with levees as much as possible and barely uses the retention basin—with the world line that bent slightly—a town that accepts part of the water into the retention basin and shares the burden between levee and basin.

In the world line where little changed, the levee is treated as “a wall that keeps water out of the town.”

That is a necessary way of thinking when protecting a town.

Levees are indispensable for protecting residential districts, factories, roads, and daily life from water damage.

However, when all water is kept inside the river as much as possible, each heavy rain places a large burden on the river and the levee.

The retention basin exists.

The signboard also says that it is “a place that temporarily stores river water during heavy rain.”

Yet on ordinary days, it is used as a walking route or open field, and residents have only a weak feeling that water will truly enter it.

As a result, the retention basin remains a facility inside drawings and signboards, and when and how it will be used is not shared as part of everyday feeling.

In the world line that bent slightly, the use of the retention basin has been organized in advance.

When heavy rain is forecast, river level, basin capacity, and the condition of the inlet are shared between the municipality and residents.

At what water level will water be admitted into the retention basin?

Before that, how will people inside the basin be evacuated?

After water enters, how long will entry remain restricted?

After the water drains, how will mud and debris be cleared?

Those procedures are shared in advance through words and diagrams.

The retention basin is maintained as “ground that may receive water.”

The expected flow and pooling of water are considered, and surrounding elevations and signs are prepared.

However, admitting water into a retention basin does not solve everything.

If rain exceeds the assumptions, both the basin and the levee become dangerous.

If the decision to admit water is too early or too late, the effect changes.

After the water drains, mud, debris, odor, sanitation management, and restoration of grassland are needed.

In some cases, land use and compensation issues cannot be avoided.

In other words, a retention basin is not a place that eliminates burden.

It is a place that slightly changes where and when the burden is placed.

The main difference described in this episode is whether the town understands what it means to accept water.

In the world line where little changed, water is treated as something to push back beyond the levee as much as possible.

In the world line that bent slightly, the town prepares a place where water may come up to a certain point and accepts part of the water there.

That is not flooding as failure, but retention as an expected function.

None of them make water disappear.
