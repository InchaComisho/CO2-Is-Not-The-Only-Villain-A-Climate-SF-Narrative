# Part 4 Episode 11 — The fishing-port morning, the port that reads water temperature, and the port overtaken by it

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episode 10, we compared two world lines through a residential district along a river: a town that lives by keeping the waterside distant, and a town that lives while watching the waterside.

In Episode 11, the viewpoint finally returns to the sea, through the morning of a fishing port.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, it is “a port trying to barely keep up with changing water temperature.”

In the world line that bent slightly, it is “a port that reads water temperature a little ahead of time and rearranges fishing and daily life around it.”

On a quay where a little morning coolness still remains, the protagonist and the AI watch sea-surface temperature, the movement of fish, and the difference in the port’s judgment.

## Section 1 — The port overtaken by water temperature in the world line where little changed

It is still early morning at the fishing port.

The boats lined up along the quay sway quietly, still a little wet.

The sound of nets being bundled.

The sound of polystyrene boxes being carried.

The distant sound of engines.

All of it mixes with the smell of the sea.

“I’d like to say it’s a good morning, but…”

I opened the reality-line log.

— Sea-surface temperature is higher than in an ordinary year.

— The way fish gather is gradually changing.

— Fish that used to be common in this port until the previous year are not very visible this morning.

— On the other hand, fish that had not appeared in large numbers before are suddenly appearing more often in the boxes.

‘In the world line where little changed on a large scale, the port is the one chasing the change.’

“Right.”

The fishers read the sea through experience.

The color of the tide.

The direction of the wind.

The height of the clouds.

Fish-finder signals.

The position of tide lines.

The way birds fly.

Those are kinds of information that have entered their bodies over a long time.

“But this year’s movement of water temperature cannot quite be followed by the usual intuition alone.”

‘That does not mean intuition is useless.’

“Exactly. That is different.”

— Experience still matters.

— But the sea on which that experience was built is shifting little by little.

— A reading that worked in the same period, same place, and same tide last year misses slightly this year.

— That mismatch happens not once, but again and again.

On the port bulletin board, a sheet says, “Today’s main fish.”

But that sheet is based on information from several days earlier.

It is slightly late for the change in this morning’s sea.

‘Being overtaken by water temperature—that wording fits.’

“Yeah.”

Some days, boats return to port with less catch than expected.

At the market, some fish rise in price.

At the same time, fish that suddenly appear can be difficult to handle.

Some days, not enough ice has been prepared.

Some days, the processing schedule no longer matches.

“This year is hard to read.”

Someone says it.

The next day, someone else says almost the same thing.

— This year is hard to read.

— This year is different.

— In the past, we could read it a little better.

Those words gradually increase in the mornings of the port.

‘News of marine heatwaves and El Niño flows through.’

“Yeah.”

‘But the operation of the port itself does not change immediately.’

“That’s the point.”

— Sea-surface temperature news is seen on television and smartphones.

— But that information is not immediately built into morning judgment at the port.

— Departure criteria, target fish, ice and refrigeration, processing, and shipping procedures still retain much of their older form.

— Even when the movement of fish changes, the port’s system moves a little late.

‘Auxiliary wheels exist, but they are still falling behind.’

“They know change is happening, but they are slow in moving it into the port’s actual hands.”

‘That is the reality-line port.’

“A port overtaken by water temperature.”

## Section 2 — The port that reads water temperature in the world line that bent slightly

‘Then let us open the fishing port in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI says this.

I open the log on the right.

First, I write one line.

— From here on, this is the world line of a port that reads water temperature a little ahead of time.

The same morning.

The same quay.

The same line of boats.

The sea is still harsh in the same way.

However, in this world line, the port’s judgment has not only “intuition,” but also a single “map of water temperature.”

“For example, like this.”

— In the port office, a simple forecast map of sea-surface temperature is posted.

— Trends in water temperature over the next several days, tide movement, and changes in how fish are gathering are roughly color-coded.

— Fishers’ experience, fish-finder signals, the previous day’s catch, and observations from satellites and buoys are gathered into a single port memo.

— In the morning meeting, fishers and port staff look at the map and make small adjustments: where to go today, what to target, how much ice to prepare.

‘The forecast map is not a perfect answer.’

“Of course.”

— Forecasts miss.

— Fish do not move exactly as expected.

— Sudden wind and tide change the plan.

— Even so, it is possible to make decisions that include change, rather than departing only on the assumption that “it should be the same as last year.”

‘How does Blue Pulse relate here?’

“I have to write that carefully.”

I stopped for a moment.

Blue Pulse was not suddenly installed near this port.

The stage of Blue Pulse is a much more distant sea.

Off Chile.

And in the what-if line, part of the waters off Peru.

It is a story about the water temperature and circulation of the distant Pacific.

— In this world line, the monitored Blue Pulse demonstration that began off Chile has expanded into part of the waters off Peru.

— In one year, during a season when El Niño development had been expected, there was a sea area where the period of sea-surface warming ended a little earlier than predicted.

— However, that is not treated as proof that Blue Pulse alone caused it.

— Natural variability, winds, currents, the state of deeper water, observation error.

— The record is handled cautiously while including all of those factors.

‘The port reads water temperature while including those distant records too.’

“Right.”

— A distant Pacific change is not magic that immediately changes the fish at this port.

— But if broad ocean temperature trends shift slightly, they may influence how fishing grounds and target species are read weeks or months later.

— The port treats that not as a confirmed answer, but as one piece of judgment material.

‘Rather than a port that reads water temperature, it is a port that reads how water temperature changes.’

“That is closer.”

In this world line, the morning conversations at the port are a little different.

“Today is not the same as the same day last year.”

“With this water temperature, maybe we shouldn’t narrow the target to one fish.”

“Let’s prepare a little more ice.”

“Maybe we should plan two possible fish for processing.”

— Before fishing, the target expands from one species to two possibilities.

— Departure time shifts slightly not only by tide, but by water-temperature change.

— Ice and refrigeration preparations begin earlier than usual.

— The market and processing plant are told in advance: this fish may increase today.

‘Not a great magic.’

“Only a slight change in the port’s hands.”

— Even in this world line, the sea is harsh.

— In years of high water temperature, fish do not return as they once did.

— There are days with poor catches.

— Forecasts miss.

— Even with Blue Pulse, sea-surface temperature does not simply return to the way it was.

— Still, the port itself has changed slightly, from a port overtaken by water temperature to a port that reads a little of how water temperature changes.

‘The port itself has auxiliary wheels.’

“Yes.”

“Not auxiliary wheels for ruling the sea, but auxiliary wheels for making judgment keep up with a changing sea.”

## Afterword

Part 4 Episode 11 used a fishing-port morning to compare two world lines from the viewpoint of the protagonist and the AI: the port overtaken by water temperature in the world line where little changed on a large scale, and the port that reads water temperature a little ahead of time in the world line that bent slightly.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, the fishers read the sea through experience and intuition.

The color of the tide.

The direction of the wind.

The height of the clouds.

Fish-finder signals.

The position of tide lines.

The way birds fly.

Those field senses remain important.

However, in years when sea-surface temperature is higher than usual and the effects of marine heatwaves and El Niño overlap, the number of situations that cannot be read by past experience alone increases.

A reading that worked last year in the same period, same place, and same tide misses slightly this year.

Bulletin-board information falls slightly behind this morning’s sea.

The targeted fish do not appear, while other fish suddenly increase.

Ice, refrigeration, processing, and shipping schedules cannot keep up.

As a result, the phrase “This year is hard to read” is repeated again and again in the port.

This is not a story about fishers’ experience becoming meaningless.

It is a story about the premise of the sea on which that experience rested slowly changing.

In the world line that bent slightly, the port has a “map of water temperature.”

A simple sea-surface temperature forecast.

Tide movement.

Trends in how fish gather.

The previous day’s catch.

Satellite and buoy observations.

Fish-finder signals.

Fishers’ experience.

Those are gathered into a single port memo and checked at the morning meeting.

The forecast map is not a perfect answer.

It can be wrong.

Fish do not move exactly as expected.

Even so, compared with departing only on the premise that “it should be the same as last year,” the port can make decisions that include change.

It does not fix the target fish to one species, but keeps multiple possibilities.

It shifts departure time slightly according to water temperature and tide movement.

It prepares ice and refrigeration earlier.

It tells the market and processing plant in advance what species may change.

Those small acts of moving judgment forward become the port’s auxiliary wheels.

This episode also clarified the distance from Blue Pulse.

Blue Pulse has not been installed immediately near this port.

Its stage is the distant Pacific of the what-if line, expanding from the waters off Chile toward part of the waters off Peru.

In that world line, a monitored small-scale demonstration has proceeded, and in one year, in a sea area where El Niño development had been expected, a record remains that the period of sea-surface warming ended a little sooner than predicted.

However, it cannot be concluded that Blue Pulse alone caused that.

Natural variability, wind, currents, the state of deeper water, and observation error must all be considered carefully.

The port treats even that distant record not as a confirmed answer, but as one piece of judgment material.

What matters is not ruling the sea.

It is making the port’s judgment catch up a little with the changing sea.

In Part 4, we have looked at the sponge-like upstream field, the midstream rain garden, the downstream retention basin, and the riverside residential district.

None of the auxiliary wheels drawn there fully controlled nature.

They changed the path of water a little.

They bent the peak of how things break a little.

They saw change a little earlier.

The fishing port in this episode is the same.

It does not completely lower sea-surface temperature.

It does not return fish to the way they were.

It simply reads a little earlier how water temperature is changing, and moves fishing, refrigeration, processing, and shipping judgment slightly forward.

That is the auxiliary wheel called “the port that reads water temperature.”

Next time, we will place sea-surface temperature news, the world line Blue Pulse did not reach, and the world line Blue Pulse may have reached more directly side by side.
