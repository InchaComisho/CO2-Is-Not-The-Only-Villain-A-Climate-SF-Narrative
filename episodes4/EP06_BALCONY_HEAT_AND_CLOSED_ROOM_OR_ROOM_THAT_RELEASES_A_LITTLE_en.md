# Part 4 Episode 6 — Balcony heat, the closed room, and the room that releases a little

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episode 5, we looked at two world lines through community centers and shelters: “places to borrow air conditioning” and “places that support a little.”

Episode 6 moves into a more personal area: the apartment balcony and the interior room.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, life continues in a closed room where heat presses in from the balcony.

In the world line that bent slightly, windows and the balcony become auxiliary wheels that help release a little heat.

This episode has the protagonist and the AI quietly compare the two, using his own room as a model on a summer afternoon.

## Section 1 — The closed room in the world line where little changed

Two in the afternoon.

Strong light poured in from the south-facing window.

The lace curtain was closed, but behind the white fabric, the light seemed to shimmer and burn.

“The balcony is almost completely in direct sun.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— The balcony floor is concrete or thin tile.

— From the surface warmed by direct sunlight, heat slowly pushes toward the room.

— The outdoor unit releases the heat taken from inside the room, warming the surrounding air even more.

‘What about the window?’

“Probably like this.”

— The window remains tightly shut.

— The habit learned when using air conditioning, that keeping outside air out is correct, continues here as well.

— Behind the curtain, hot air clings to the glass.

— The glass itself also holds heat and slowly transfers it into the room.

‘What about the indoor air?’

“It tends to become a choice between running the air conditioner hard or enduring the heat because of electricity costs.”

— If the air conditioning is kept too weak out of endurance, the indoor temperature slowly rises.

— If the air conditioner is run strongly, the room cools, but the outdoor unit dumps even more heat onto the balcony.

— Either way, the heat stored on the balcony, the heat entering the room, and the heat expelled by the air conditioner together create the room of a summer afternoon.

‘Almost no auxiliary wheels have been added to this world line.’

“The window and the balcony are stuck in a binary choice: seal everything off to block heat, or open everything and accept it.”

— There is little awareness of how to open windows to create an airflow path.

— There is not enough effort to soften sunlight once on the outside of the window.

— Luggage or plant pots placed in front of the outdoor unit partly block the path of exhaust heat.

— The balcony is a place to dry laundry, a place for the outdoor unit, and at the same time a place where heat accumulates.

‘What does the person inside the room do?’

“Turn on the air conditioner, close the curtains, drink water, and move as little as possible.”

‘Enduring through individual effort.’

“Same as the station front and the park.”

It is not that nobody is doing anything.

They use air conditioning.

They close curtains.

They drink water.

Even so, the room itself does not help much against heat.

“This is the room in the world line where little changed.”

‘A heat-storing balcony paired with a closed interior.’

“It feels uncomfortably close to everyday life.”

## Section 2 — The room that releases a little in the world line that bent slightly

‘Now let us look at the balcony and room in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI spoke.

I opened the right-side log.

Then I wrote a single line.

— From here on, this is the world line of a room where the window and balcony became auxiliary wheels for releasing a little heat.

The same apartment building.

The same balcony orientation.

The same room size.

But in this world line, the light, wind, and exhaust heat of a summer afternoon have once been diagrammed before deciding how to use the window and balcony.

“For example, this kind of small adjustment exists.”

— On the balcony, within building rules and safety limits, an external shade has been installed.

— During the hours when the midsummer sun is high, direct sunlight is weakened before it hits the window glass.

— A reed screen or heat-shielding cloth is hung so that it does not completely block the wind, softening mainly the light.

‘Sunlight is stopped on the outside of the window.’

“Compared with only using an interior curtain, the glass itself becomes less likely to heat up.”

‘But how it is attached matters.’

“If it falls, that is dangerous, and it must not block evacuation routes.”

— The shade can be folded away on strong-wind days.

— Nothing is placed near the emergency partition board or evacuation ladder.

— Within the rules of the shared apartment structure, heat is received a little on the outside before reaching the room.

‘What about the outdoor unit?’

“That also changes slightly.”

— No objects are placed in front of the outdoor unit.

— Space is kept open in front and to the sides so the hot exhaust air does not immediately strike a wall or pot and return.

— The unit itself is not forcibly enclosed or covered with wet cloth.

— Instead, only shade that does not block airflow is considered, so direct sunlight does not strike it too strongly.

‘That affects cooling efficiency as well.’

“If the outdoor unit cannot get rid of heat easily, the room side has to work harder to cool.”

‘What about opening the windows?’

“That is the important part.”

— In this world line, people do not assume that opening a window always makes the room cooler.

— During hours when outside air is hot and humid, the window stays closed while sunlight is blocked and air conditioning is used efficiently.

— In the evening or later, when the outside temperature falls below the indoor temperature and some wind is present, the window is opened for a short time.

— The balcony side and the entrance side, or another window, are opened slightly to create a path for air to pass through.

— Once the stagnant heat has been released, the windows are closed again and air conditioning is used without strain.

‘So it is not “cooling while releasing,” but “release when release is possible, and cool when cooling is needed.”’

“That avoids misunderstanding.”

— In this world line, not only the air-conditioner setting and fan speed, but also when to block sunlight, when to ventilate, and when to close the room become part of the room’s auxiliary wheels.

— Seal everything, or endure.

— Run the air conditioner hard, or turn it off.

— Instead of only those two choices, a way of living that changes how heat enters and leaves depending on the time of day is explained through apartment notices and municipal leaflets.

‘Even so, it is still a hot summer.’

“Of course.”

— News about dangerous heat continues in the same way.

— Neither the balcony nor the room becomes cool by doing nothing.

— But the window and balcony are treated as auxiliary wheels for extending, even slightly, the time a person can remain safely in the room.

“It feels like making the room’s use a little more of an ally, rather than relying only on personal endurance.”

‘A combination of personal knowledge and small building-side adjustments.’

## Afterword

Part 4 Episode 6 used an apartment balcony and interior room to compare two world lines: the closed room in the world line where little changed, and the room that releases a little heat in the world line that bent slightly.

In the first world line, the balcony becomes a little garden box of heat.

The floor and railing are warmed by direct sunlight, and the outdoor unit releases heat taken from inside the room.

That heat accumulates around the balcony and returns toward the room through the window glass and walls.

Inside, the windows are shut, the curtains are closed, and air conditioning is used.

Those are necessary measures.

However, if there is not enough knowledge about softening sunlight on the outside of the window, arranging space for outdoor-unit exhaust, or ventilating briefly when the outside air has cooled, the room tends to become trapped in a binary choice between blocking heat and enduring it.

In the world line that bent slightly, the balcony and windows become small auxiliary wheels.

First, within building rules and safety limits, external shades, reed screens, or heat-shielding cloth are used so direct sunlight is weakened before it reaches the glass.

It is important not to block evacuation routes or emergency partitions, and to be able to fold the shade away in strong winds.

Next, nothing is placed in front of the outdoor unit, so hot air does not accumulate.

The outdoor unit is not forcibly enclosed and its exhaust is not blocked; instead, airflow is kept open in front and to the sides.

Window opening is also not based on the idea that opening a window always makes things cooler.

During hot, humid hours, windows are kept closed, sunlight is blocked, and air conditioning is used efficiently.

In the evening or later, when the outside temperature falls below the indoor temperature and there is wind, windows are opened briefly to release stagnant heat.

After that, they are closed again and air conditioning is used without strain.

This is not a life without air conditioning.

Rather, it is a life that uses air conditioning as necessary while slightly organizing the heat that enters the room and the heat that can be released from it.

The auxiliary wheels in this episode are not large equipment.

An external shade.

The space in front of the outdoor unit.

Short ventilation depending on time of day.

Small pieces of knowledge shared through apartment notices or municipal leaflets.

They are that modest.

Yet those small differences may extend, even slightly, the time a person can remain in the room without collapsing.

Part 4 has so far looked at auxiliary wheels outside the home: station fronts, shopping streets, parks, and community centers.

This balcony and interior room are the most personal auxiliary wheels.

Urban heat does not end at roads and parks.

It warms building walls, accumulates on balconies, and enters rooms through window glass.

That is why there is room even inside the home to slightly change how heat enters and how heat escapes.

Which measures can be used depends on orientation, floor level, building rules, household composition, and health.

No single way of living can be forced on everyone.

Even so, having a third mode beyond simply closing everything or enduring everything — releasing heat when release is possible, and cooling when cooling is needed — can become an auxiliary wheel for summer living.

World lines do not bend only at station fronts or parks.

A balcony screen, a small open space in front of an outdoor unit, and a window opened for a little while in the evening.

Small practices inside private life may also bend the line slightly.
