# Part 4 Episode 12 — Witnessing the summer that continues, and the summer that bent slightly

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episode 11, we compared two world lines through the sea and a fishing port: “a port overtaken by water temperature” and “a port that reads water temperature a little ahead of time.”

Episode 12 is the final episode of Part 4.

Here, the auxiliary wheels we have looked at separately—in the city, the watershed, and the sea—are gathered once more, as the protagonist witnesses both “the world line that continues as it is” and “the world line that bent slightly.”

## Section 2 — The log of the summer that bent slightly

“Then let us summarize the world line that bent slightly.”

I opened the folder on the right.

—The station front became a place where people could wait for a few minutes through shade, wind, and mist.

—The shopping-street mist became not merely a tool for bustle, but assumption-like equipment that operates on dangerous-heat days when conditions allow.

—The park bench had its position shifted slightly after watching where shadows fell.

—The community center became a “heat countermeasure rest point,” where the reason people could come and what they could do there were put into words.

—The balcony stopped being only a heat box, and became a small auxiliary wheel for softening sunlight, releasing exhaust heat, and venting heat at the right time of day.

‘The urban summer gained more places that support a little.’

“Yes.”

The heat did not disappear.

A day of extreme heat remained a day of extreme heat.

The station front, shopping street, and park did not become cool paradises.

Still.

“I can no longer stand here” turns into “I can wait here for a little while.”

“I should not sit down today” turns into “I can sit for a moment before going home.”

“A box where people borrow air conditioning” turns into “a place to recover once.”

The difference is small.

But being small does not mean being meaningless.

“The watershed was the same.”

## Afterword

Thank you for reading through the final episode of Part 4.

Part 4 was the part that placed “the world line that continues as it is” beside “the world line that bent slightly,” and followed, through the dialogue between the protagonist and the AI, how auxiliary wheels entered different places in the city, the watershed, and the sea.

The main points drawn in this part are these three.

### 1. The urban summer

In familiar places—the station front, the shopping street, the park, the community center, and the balcony—we described small shifts from “event auxiliary wheels” toward “assumption auxiliary wheels.”

At the station front, shade, wind, and mist create a place where people can wait for only a few minutes.

In the shopping street, mist is treated not merely as “bustle,” but as assumption-like equipment that operates on dangerous-heat days when conditions allow.

In the park, the positions of benches, tree shade, and drinking water are adjusted a little.

At the community center, the role is put into words not as a box with air conditioning, but as a place where people can recover once during dangerous heat and heavy rain.

On the balcony, sunlight, exhaust heat from the outdoor unit, and the timing of ventilation are adjusted a little.

None of these places erase extreme heat.

Even so, we saw that they can become places where people can stand for a few minutes, rest a little, or stretch the time they can spend without collapsing.

### 2. The watershed summer

Through upstream slopes and fields, midstream rain gardens, downstream retention basins, and riverside residential districts, we placed auxiliary wheels in the direction of not only sending water away as quickly as possible, but also receiving it once somewhere, remembering it, and transforming it into how land and life are handled.

Upstream, the memory of collapse was moved into how grass is left and how fields are worked.

Midstream, rain gardens received roof water once in the garden.

Downstream, retention basins were positioned as places where water may come, so that the burden would not concentrate only on levees.

In riverside residential districts, we described a way of living that does not merely keep the waterside distant, but watches it in ordinary times and, when danger comes, stays away and checks information.

None of these completely prevent water damage.

They do not stop heavy rain.

They are auxiliary wheels that change a little how water passes through and how people face it.

### 3. The summer of the sea

Through the fishing port, we described a world line in which the port does not merely try to catch up with changes in sea-surface temperature, but reads how water temperature is changing and moves its decisions a little earlier, bending the way fishing and daily life break.

In the world line where things did not move much on a large scale, fishers’ experience and intuition remain.

However, the sea that supported that experience has changed, and the information on the bulletin board and decisions about fishing slowly fall behind.

In the world line that bent slightly, the port has a map of sea-surface temperature and uses observation values, forecasts, sonar responses, the previous day’s catch, and fishers’ experience together in its morning decisions.

In the distant Pacific, there is also a world line where monitored Blue Pulse demonstrations expanded from off Chile to part of the waters off Peru.

However, that is not magic that completely cools the sea.

It is not a device that erases El Niño.

The port treats those records as one piece of judgment material, while looking carefully at natural variability and observation errors as well.

If the conclusion of Part 4 is put in the protagonist’s words, it becomes this:

Auxiliary wheels are not omnipotent.

But in the city, the watershed, and the sea, they reach little by little into a range that is better than the world line where things did not move much on a large scale.

Their reach is small, partial, and incomplete.

Even so, they can bend the direction and pace of breakdown a little.

What Part 4 revealed was “the range that can be reached.”

Part 5 will move on to the next questions: how to continue these auxiliary wheels, how to support them, and how to preserve them.

Who inspects them?

Who pays for them?

Who updates the records?

Who decides when to stop them?

Who hands them over to the next person?

With the “range reached” seen in Part 4 as its foundation, the story will now look at “the conditions for continuing.”

Part 4 comes to a break here.

In the next Part 5, from another angle, we will continue to depict the same world and the slightly bent possibility within it.
