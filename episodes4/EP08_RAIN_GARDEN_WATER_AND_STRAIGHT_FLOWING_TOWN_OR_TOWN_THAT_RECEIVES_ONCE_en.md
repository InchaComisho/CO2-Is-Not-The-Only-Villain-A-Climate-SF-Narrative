# Part 4 Episode 8 — Rain-garden water, the town that sends it straight through, and the town that receives it once

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episode 7, we looked at two world lines through upstream slopes and fields: a slope whose memory of collapse remained vague, and a slope that remembered how it collapsed and moved closer to sponge-like soil.

In Episode 8, the viewpoint moves slightly downstream, to a midstream town with rain gardens.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, it is a town that sends rain as quickly as possible into gutters and the river.

In the world line that bent slightly, it is a town that receives rain once in gardens before sending it onward.

This episode has the protagonist and the AI use a late-summer shower and autumn rain to consider whether the path of water can become an auxiliary wheel.

## Section 1 — The straight-flowing town in the world line where little changed

A residential area in the middle reaches of the river.

Two-story houses stand in rows, and narrow roads run like a net.

There are gardens.

But the soil has been compacted underfoot, and when rain falls, water quickly bounces off the surface.

“The gutters are well maintained in this town.”

I opened the reality-line log.

— Concrete gutters continue along both sides of the roads.

— Rainwater falling from roofs runs down through drainpipes.

— That water passes along the edge of each property and flows into the gutter almost without hesitation.

— The gutters continue uninterrupted toward the river several hundred meters away.

‘At first glance, it is proper drainage.’

“It really is proper.”

‘It is necessary to keep water out of houses.’

“No question there.”

A sudden evening shower arrives.

At first, only a few drops.

Soon, the sound striking the roofs grows stronger.

Water spreads thinly across the road surface and is quickly drawn into the gutters.

— As the rain strengthens, gutter water levels rise sharply in a short time.

— Water from household downspouts gathers along the roadside at almost the same timing.

— The river level also rises sharply during the latter half of the rain.

‘This is the world line where the town has no rain gardens.’

“Right.”

— Gardens are places for ornamental trees.

— Places to hang laundry.

— Places to put storage boxes.

— Places to pull weeds on weekends.

— But they are not thought of much as paths for water when rain falls.

“Water is treated as something to send outside as quickly as possible.”

‘That itself is an old urban idea.’

“It is easy to understand if the goal is to protect the house.”

‘But what happens if we look at the whole town?’

“Every house sends water out quickly in the same way.”

‘Yes.’

“That water gathers in the gutters and river during the same time period.”

‘Yes.’

“As a result, both the amount and speed left to the river increase.”

— In this town, the detailed movement of rain is watched mainly by the municipality and specialists.

— For many residents, rain becomes something that ends once it is sent outside the house.

— After water flows from the garden to the gutter, where it joins other water and when it reaches the river are hard to see in everyday life.

‘That is the straight-flowing town.’

“It looks well organized, but it is also a town where water does not stop even once.”

## Section 2 — The town with rain gardens in the world line that bent slightly

‘Now let us look at the midstream town in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI said.

I opened the log on the right.

Then I wrote one line first.

— From here, this is the world line of a town that receives rain once in gardens before sending it onward.

A similar residential area.

The same two-story houses.

The same narrow roads and gutters.

But in this world line, some household gardens have become gardens for rain—rain gardens.

“What kind of garden is that?”

I opened my rain-garden notes.

— A rain garden has a shallow depression.

— The ground is mixed with soil, sand, and organic matter that allow water to soak in a little more easily.

— Around it are grasses and low shrubs that can tolerate both some moisture and some dryness.

— An outlet is also prepared so that excess water can escape to the gutter and water does not remain there indefinitely.

‘So it is different from a pond.’

“Different.”

‘It is not a place that holds water all the time.’

“It receives water for a while after rain, then slowly loses it.”

‘What about maintenance?’

“That is needed too.”

— If fallen leaves clog it, water flow worsens.

— If the soil becomes too compacted, water no longer soaks in easily.

— If water remains too long, hygiene problems can appear.

— So a rain garden is not finished once it is built; grass must be cut, leaves removed, and the way water remains must be checked from time to time.

‘An auxiliary wheel only becomes an auxiliary wheel when it is maintained.’

“Same as the station-front mist.”

‘What happens to the water from the roof?’

“In this world, not all of it goes straight to the gutter. Some enters that depression once.”

— Part of the downspout is directed not toward the gutter, but toward the rain garden.

— When a sudden shower arrives, water from the roof enters the rain garden and collects in the shallow depression.

— Some soaks into the ground, some passes around plant roots, and only the excess flows out to the gutter a little later.

‘How does the gutter water level change?’

“I cannot state it definitely, but…”

I chose my words carefully.

“On a street where several houses with rain gardens are connected, part of the rainwater remains in gardens for several tens of minutes to several hours.”

‘Yes.’

“That shifts the timing at which water enters the gutters all at once.”

‘Yes.’

“At the street scale, the shape of the peak may become a little gentler.”

‘It does not necessarily change the river level as a whole by much.’

“I will not say that.”

— In this world line too, news of dangerous heavy rain still airs in the same way.

— If the rain gardens fill up, excess water flows into the gutters.

— If heavy rain continues for a long time, both gutters and the river rise.

— Rain gardens alone cannot stop flooding.

— Still, compared with a town where all water flows into gutters at once, a town where water visits gardens once before entering the gutters may be able to change the way water gathers a little.

‘That is the role of rain gardens as auxiliary wheels.’

“There is also another difference from the side of everyday life.”

— In a house with a rain garden, when people look out the window on a rainy day, they can see roof water collecting in the depression and gradually decreasing.

— A child learns, “Water comes to the garden once, then goes to the river.”

— Adults also become aware for a moment of where this water came from and where it is going.

‘The path of water becomes visible inside daily life.’

“Exactly.”

A rain garden is not merely equipment.

It is not a box that stops water.

It creates a place where rain can be seen once on its way from a house to the river.
