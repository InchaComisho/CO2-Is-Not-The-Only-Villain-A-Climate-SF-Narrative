# Part 4 Episode 3 — Shopping-street mist between “bustle” and “assumption”

## Foreword

In Part 4 Episode 2, we placed side by side the summer of a station-front rotary in the world line where little changed on a large scale and the summer of the station front in the world line that bent slightly.

In Episode 3, we move only a little, to the covered shopping street and the mist installed beneath its roof.

In the world line where little changed on a large scale, summer wavers between annual budgets and available labor, with mist treated as an event feature for creating bustle.

In the world line that bent slightly, summer includes mist that runs quietly on the days it is needed, as ordinary infrastructure serving as an auxiliary wheel against heat.

This episode has the protagonist and the AI examine that difference from both the field and institutional sides.

---

“Getting from the station to the shopping street may be today’s hardest part.”

A short walk beyond the rotary stood an old arcade.

A metal frame.

A roof like frosted glass.

Panels whose color had changed in places, left unreplaced.

‘It blocks most of the direct sunlight.’

“But it also tends to trap wind and heat.”

I stopped once at the entrance.

The stabbing sunlight outside weakened.

That did not mean the air inside was cool.

Heat and moisture gathered beneath the roof and slowly clung to my body.

“Let’s set Part 4 Episode 3 here.”

‘The shopping-street mist.’

“The thing between bustle and assumption.”

‘A fitting subject.’

---

## Section 1 — The “bustle mist” of the world line where little changed

Once again, posters for summer events were attached to the arcade columns.

A cooling festival.

Shopping-street appreciation day.

An evening market.

Under one of those titles, in small letters, it said “Mist installation.”

“The usual thing.”

‘Mist installed only on event days.’

I opened the reality-line log.

— The nozzles and narrow pipes hang from the ceiling for only a few days each summer.

— On preparation day, shopping-street members carry ladders, extend hoses, and inspect pumps and nozzles.

— When the festival ends, cleanup begins, and the pipes and nozzles return to storage.

“The official purpose is usually creating bustle.”

‘That is probably what the subsidy application says too.’

“Increasing visitors.”

‘Yes.’

“Extending their time in the area.”

‘Yes.’

“Revitalizing the shopping street.”

‘Yes.’

“Heat countermeasures are added in small print at the edge of the paperwork.”

‘Institutionally, they are not the main purpose.’

In practice, some people do come to cool down.

— While the mist is running, children pass beneath it again and again.

— People who have finished shopping stop for a moment.

— Shopkeepers step outside saying, “It’s a little better than usual today.”

‘It is not ineffective.’

“No. At least in that time and place, it helps someone.”

‘But only on event days.’

“That’s the point.”

— Even on days of high heatstroke risk, the mist does not run unless an event is scheduled.

— Even while extreme heat dominates the news, some years the installation itself is canceled because the budget is tight or there are not enough people to set it up.

— Even if today is hotter than yesterday, what decides whether the equipment runs is not the heat, but the event calendar and the single-year budget.

‘That is the shopping-street mist of the world line where little changed.’

“It does serve as an auxiliary wheel.”

‘Yes.’

“But it has not become part of everyday assumptions.”

‘It is not a system that runs when needed, but a project that runs on scheduled days.’

“So it responds to the calendar, not the heat.”

That difference felt larger than I had expected.

---

## Section 2 — The “assumption mist” of the world line that bent slightly

‘Then let us open the shopping street in the world line that bent slightly.’

The AI said.

I wrote one line in the log on the right.

— From here begins the record of a world line in which bustle mist changed slightly into assumption mist.

The same arcade.

The same shopping street.

The same kind of hot summer.

But in this world line, the role of mist has shifted halfway from event decoration to public safety equipment.

“It may not look very different.”

— Narrow piping runs along beams near the roof.

— Small nozzles are placed at regular intervals.

— The difference is that the piping is fixed as shared shopping-street infrastructure rather than temporary equipment.

‘Who operates it?’

“The shopping street and the municipality probably divide the roles.”

— The municipality provides information on temperature, humidity, wind speed, and heatstroke risk.

— The shopping-street office checks equipment condition and pedestrian volume, then activates the mist during periods that meet the operating conditions.

— If something abnormal occurs, on-site staff can stop it.

‘It does not spray unconditionally just because an alert was issued.’

“If humidity is too high or the wind too weak, it could make things less comfortable.”

‘A wet floor also creates a slipping risk.’

“If droplets drift toward products, shops are affected too.”

‘Water quality and nozzle hygiene must also be managed.’

“Becoming ordinary infrastructure does not mean leaving it on all the time.”

‘It means the conditions for running and stopping it have been defined.’

I imagined the other shopping street.

— Under normal conditions, the mist is off.

— When temperature, humidity, and wind conditions align and heatstroke risk rises, fine mist quietly begins.

— If droplets are not evaporating sufficiently and may reach floors or products, the spray rate is reduced or the system is stopped.

— It is used together with shade, airflow, seating, and water-refill points rather than relied on alone.

‘What does the paperwork call it?’

“Probably this.”

— Safety measures for visitors and employees during high temperatures.

— Shared equipment to reduce local heat risk.

— Mobility support for pedestrians, including older adults and children.

‘The word bustle would not disappear completely.’

“It doesn’t need to.”

Children still play beneath the mist.

People finishing their shopping still take a slight detour, saying, “Let’s go home this way today.”

A shopkeeper looks up at the piping and says,

“The risk is high today, so let’s run it at the usual setting.”

‘The language changed.’

“It is no longer, ‘Can we afford to put it out this year?’”

‘It became, “Today is a day to run it.”’

“The existence and operation of the equipment have entered everyday life.”

On festival days, lights, music, and decorations are added.

But the operating conditions for the mist are not pushed beyond safe limits just because it is an event.

The same ordinary equipment is used within safe and comfortable conditions.

— Festival staging is added to the mist.

— The mist does not exist only for the festival.

‘A world line that retained bustle while shifting slightly toward assumption.’

“There is no need to stop holding events.”

‘No.’

“Change equipment usable only for events into equipment usable in daily life too.”

‘That is the difference.’

## Section 3 — What the protagonist says after comparing the two mists

I overlaid the two arcades in my mind.

On the left:

Mist hung only on summer-festival days.

A single-year subsidy.

Shopping-street members responsible for installation and removal.

A world line where operation depends on budgets, labor, and event schedules.

On the right:

Mist fixed as arcade infrastructure.

A system that runs after checking temperature, humidity, and wind.

A world line where responsibility for inspection and shutdown has been assigned.

On event days, a little bustle is added on top.

‘How are they different?’ the AI asked.

“On the left, whether there is mist this year changes greatly from year to year.”

I answered after thinking for a moment.

“Will the budget be approved?”

‘Yes.’

“Will there be people to install it?”

‘Yes.’

“Is the equipment still working?”

‘Yes.’

“Can the event be held?”

‘Yes.’

“Only when all of those align does the mist finally appear.”

‘And on the right?’

“If heat risk and operating conditions align, it basically runs.”

‘The auxiliary wheel is no longer an event option.’

“It became equipment for responding to dangerous heat.”

— The mist on the left supports an event.

— The mist on the right supports people in continuing everyday life.

— Both have meaning.

— But they reach different days and different groups of people.

‘Even so, it does not reach people who do not enter the arcade.’

“True.”

‘Nor does it cool the entire shopping street.’

“Some days, humidity or wind conditions prevent operation.”

‘Maintenance costs are also necessary.’

“Water, electricity, cleaning, inspection, repairs.”

‘Once infrastructure becomes an assumption, responsibility becomes an assumption too.’

“We need to state that clearly.”

For equipment to become established means it no longer depends solely on annual goodwill.

At the same time, it means someone accepts continuing responsibility.

Who inspects it?

Who pays?

Who starts it?

Who stops it?

Unless those questions are settled, even fixed piping eventually becomes unused equipment.

‘Being installed and continuing to function are not the same.’

“So even the world line where things moved still contains the possibility of stopping.”

‘Of course.’

“Still, one thing is different from the left.”

‘What?’

“Continuation does not rest only on the determination of a few specific people.”

‘It shifted slightly into institutions and management.’

“So that is the true form of assumption mist.”

I added short lines to both logs.

— This is the log of a world line where mist remains an event feature, wavering under annual conditions.

— This is the log of a world line where mist became ordinary infrastructure and quietly began operating on the days it was needed.

“This story will not say that only one is the correct answer.”

‘But you have a preference.’

“I do.”

I looked toward a bench deeper inside the arcade.

An older man with shopping bags at his feet slowly moved a hand fan.

A little farther away, a shop employee wiped sweat from his forehead while arranging products.

The shopping street is not used only by visitors on festival days.

People who pass through every day.

People who work here every day.

People who rest while shopping.

People who feel anxious about going outside precisely because the day is hot.

“Rather than a world where cheerful mist appears only on event days…”

‘Yes.’

“I want to preserve, as the better auxiliary wheel, the world where it runs quietly on dangerous days after checking conditions.”

‘A very Part 4 choice.’

“It isn’t flashy.”

‘An assumption does not need to be flashy.’

“Maybe it is complete only when it becomes ordinary equipment.”

I looked up at the arcade roof.

In the reality line, there was still no fixed piping.

Only beams, an old roof, and summer-festival posters.

Even so, what should be attached here?

Who should support it?

Under what budget purpose should it be funded?

A single choice could make the same shopping-street summer slightly different.

World lines do not diverge only when dramatic machines begin operating.

Should the purpose field on the application say “creating bustle”?

Or “local safety measures during high temperatures”?

Perhaps a world line can begin bending from wording that small.

Part 4 Episode 3 ends here.

## Afterword

Part 4 Episode 3 used the covered shopping street and its mist to compare the bustle mist of the world line where little changed with the assumption mist of the world line that bent slightly.

In the first world line, mist is installed for summer festivals, appreciation days, evening markets, and similar events.

The main subsidy purposes are creating bustle, increasing visitors, and revitalizing the shopping street.

It also provides heat relief, but institutionally that is not the central purpose.

Therefore, whether the mist runs is not determined by heat risk alone.

Was funding approved?

Are people available to install and remove it?

Can the equipment be prepared?

Will the event go ahead as planned?

Only when those conditions align does the mist operate.

It is an auxiliary wheel that helps people on event days.

In the world line that bent slightly, mist is fixed as shared shopping-street infrastructure.

However, it still does not operate unconditionally whenever a heatstroke alert is issued.

Temperature.

Humidity.

Wind speed.

Pedestrian volume.

Effects of droplets on floors and products.

The hygienic condition of nozzles and water.

Those conditions are checked, and the system is used when evaporative cooling can be obtained and safe operation is possible.

If humidity is too high, wind is too weak and droplets linger, or wet flooring raises the risk of falls, the spray rate is reduced or operation is stopped.

Becoming ordinary infrastructure does not mean being left on continuously.

Conditions for starting.

Conditions for stopping.

The people who inspect it.

The parties who pay.

Responsibility during abnormal operation.

Those things are defined in advance.

Mist is also not expected to cool the entire shopping street by itself.

Shade beneath the arcade.

Airflow.

Seating.

Water-refill points.

Shop air conditioning and ventilation.

Mist is used as one local heat countermeasure in combination with those elements.

On event days, lighting, music, and decoration are added.

But the mist is not intensified beyond safe operating conditions simply because an event is being held.

Bustle is added to ordinary infrastructure; the mist itself is no longer special equipment that exists only on festival days.

The main difference described in this episode is not the shape of the mist or the amount sprayed.

For what purpose is the equipment treated?

Who bears responsibility for installation and management?

Is there a mechanism that allows it to run on the days it is needed?

Are there a budget and management structure that will still exist next year?

That is the difference.

Event mist also has value.

It is easier to start and can let local people experience the effect.

But because it depends on annual budgets, labor, and goodwill, it may not exist when needed.

Mist as ordinary infrastructure requires greater initial expense and coordination, and creates continuing maintenance responsibility.

Installation does not guarantee permanent function.

If inspections stop and funding ends, fixed piping also becomes unused equipment.

Even so, continuation can be moved away from the determination of a few individuals and into institutions and management.

The protagonist does not declare either line the only correct answer.

One world line wavers each year with event mist.

The other has mist as ordinary infrastructure, quietly running on needed days.

He records both.

Yet he personally wants to leave behind, as the better auxiliary wheel, the world where mist runs quietly on dangerous heat days after checking conditions, rather than only appearing as cheerful fog on festival days.

World lines are not divided only by large machines or dramatic decisions.

Does a subsidy application say “creating bustle”?

Or “local safety measures during high temperatures”?

Is the equipment treated as an annual project?

Or as an assumption supporting everyday urban life?

Differences in such small words and institutions can gradually bend the same shopping-street summer in another direction.
