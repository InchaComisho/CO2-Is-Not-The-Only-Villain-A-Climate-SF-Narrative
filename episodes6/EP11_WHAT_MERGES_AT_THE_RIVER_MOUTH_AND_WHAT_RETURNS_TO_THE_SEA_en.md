# Part 6 Episode 11 — What merges at the river mouth, and what is returned to the sea

## Foreword

In Part 6 Episode 10, we looked at “watershed auxiliary wheels” that cross upstream, midstream, and downstream towns.

A river does not know municipal borders.

That is why rainfall, water level, retention basins, and evacuation information also need to be connected across town borders.

In Episode 11, we head toward the end of the watershed: the river mouth.

Upstream soil.

Midstream rain gardens.

Downstream retention basins.

Town drains.

People’s lives.

Water that has passed through all of those finally enters the sea.

This is the episode where the protagonist and the AI stand at the river mouth, where river and sea mix, and look at “what the watershed hands to the sea” and “what the heat and tides coming from the sea return to the watershed.”

## Section 1 — The river mouth is like a receipt for the watershed

A river-mouth landscape appeared on the screen.

River water.

Sea water.

Tidal flats.

Reed beds.

A seawall.

Fishing boats.

A bridge.

Birds.

Driftwood.

“There is a lot of information at a river mouth.”

‘Because water that has passed from upstream to downstream comes here.’

“So?”

‘The river mouth is like a receipt for the watershed.’

“That suddenly sounds very everyday.”

‘What was bought upstream, what was added midstream, and what was dropped downstream usually show up at the end.’

“Even things you do not want on the receipt show up.”

If soil flows out upstream, the river becomes muddy.

If rain is sent to drains at maximum speed midstream, the water increases all at once.

If there is no place downstream to receive water, the flow tends to become sharp.

Trash, oil, and fine pollution washed out from the town also head toward the river mouth through the river in the end.

“By the time it reaches the sea, everything is mixed.”

‘Water goes to the sea carrying the history of the watershed.’

“Part 6 has become a little poetic.”

‘Because it is the river mouth.’

“That sounds like a reason, and also not like a reason.”

The river mouth is not simply the place where a river ends.

The condition of upstream soil.

The way midstream residential areas release water.

The way downstream retention basins are used.

The town’s everyday life.

The flow on rainy days.

All of those converge just before the sea.

“The final exit of the watershed.”

‘An exit, and also an entrance to the sea.’

“I see. It is not the terminal point.”

‘Because it is circulation.’

## Section 2 — In the unmoving world line, the river hurries too much toward the sea

“First, the unmoving world line.”

The AI displayed the first river mouth on the left.

Upstream, soil is easily washed away by rain.

Midstream, water from roofs and roads goes straight to drains.

Downstream, retention basins are not used enough, and the river heads toward the river mouth while still holding water.

“The water of the watershed is hurrying the whole time.”

‘Yes.’

After heavy rain, the water at the river mouth becomes muddy.

Driftwood and trash gather along the edge of the river mouth.

Freshwater from the river is pushed into the sea over a short time.

Life in tidal flats and shallow places also receives sudden changes in water.

“It is not reset just because it reaches the sea.”

‘The sea is large. But the sea near a river mouth is affected by the watershed.’

“The roughness of the watershed shows up at the river mouth.”

‘Not only roughness. What happened upstream, what increased midstream, and what could not be received downstream show up there.’

“The receipt feeling is strong.”

Fishers at the river mouth look at the color of the water.

“River water is strong today,” they say.

People at the port look at the turbidity and drifting objects, then slightly change where they send boats out.

Work at the fishing port is also affected by the state of the river-mouth water.

“Upstream rain comes all the way to the morning of the fisher at the river mouth.”

‘It also connects to the port in Episode 3.’

“From the world’s sea to the port. This time, from the watershed to the port.”

‘The sea side receives many things too.’

“The sea is like a reception counter.”

‘A very large one.’

“But it cannot receive infinitely.”

‘Right.’

I looked at the muddy water at the river mouth.

The sea is wide.

But the sea beside a river mouth is not disconnected from the watershed.

The time of high tide.

The time when sea level is high.

When those two overlap, the river mouth becomes a little complicated.

“The river wants to go out. The sea comes in.”

When sea level is high, water that is trying to flow from the river mouth into the sea can become a little harder to discharge.

When river water is rising after strong rain and tide or wave conditions overlap, the downstream side’s drainage becomes even more complicated.

“Looking only at upstream rain is not enough.”

‘At the river mouth, we must also look at the sea side.’

## Section 4 — In the slightly bent world line, the river mouth also becomes an auxiliary wheel

Upstream, soil receives some water.

Downstream, retention basins become places where river water can go.

Near the river mouth, some tidal flats, reed beds, and shallow waters remain.

“The river mouth has a sponge-like role too.”

‘Water, soil, and plants become places that slightly change the force of the flow.’

Tidal flats do not stop all water.

Reed beds do not completely prevent flooding.

## Afterword

Part 6 Episode 11 used the river mouth as its stage and looked at what the watershed and the sea hand to each other.

Water that has passed through upstream soil, midstream rain gardens, and downstream retention basins finally heads from the river mouth to the sea.

At the same time, the sea returns influence to the water movement of the river mouth and watershed through tide, waves, sea level, heat, and humidity.

The points depicted in this episode are mainly these three.

### 1. The river mouth is where the history of the watershed gathers

Soil washed out upstream, rainwater from midstream residential areas, water and drifting objects that could not be fully received downstream, and other things gather at the river mouth.

Turbidity, water force, drifting objects, and difficulties in port work at the river mouth are not disconnected from the state of the watershed from upstream to downstream.

The river mouth is both the endpoint of the river and the place where the history of the watershed is handed to the sea.

### 2. At the river mouth, river water and sea conditions overlap

The river tries to flow out to the sea, while tide and waves enter from the sea side.

When heavy rain, rising river water, tide level, and sea conditions overlap, the way water drains at the river mouth becomes more complicated.

At the river mouth, it is necessary to look not only at “upstream rain” or only at “the sea,” but at both.

The heat of the world’s ocean.

Local tides.

Upstream rain.

Midstream drains.

Downstream retention basins.

They all meet at the river mouth.

### 3. The river mouth can also become an auxiliary wheel that connects watershed and sea

Tidal flats, reed beds, and shallow waters do not stop all water or turbidity.

They do not completely prevent floods, and they do not stop marine heatwaves.

Even so, they can become room that receives a little of the flow of water and soil, and slightly softens the burden that is passed to the river mouth and sea.

To protect the sea, look at upstream fields and midstream rain gardens.

To protect the port, look at retention basins.

To protect the river mouth, look at the whole watershed.

These connections became central to Part 6.

Part 6 Episode 11 reconnected the story of heat, water, and circulation, which began from the sea, once again at the river mouth.

The watershed hands water to the sea.

The sea returns heat and tide to the watershed.

That is why auxiliary wheels should not end with the river alone, but connect to the river mouth and the sea.

The river mouth is a place that reflects what the watershed hands to the sea, and also a place that receives what the sea returns to the watershed.

This sentence is the center of Part 6 Episode 11.

In the next Part 6 Episode 12, we will once again place the world’s red graphs beside the auxiliary wheels placed in the town, the watershed, the river mouth, and the sea, and move toward the conclusion of Part 6.

From sea to rain.

From rain to river.

From river to sea.

And then to the town’s front door.

Part 6 goes to see the exit and entrance of circulation once more.
