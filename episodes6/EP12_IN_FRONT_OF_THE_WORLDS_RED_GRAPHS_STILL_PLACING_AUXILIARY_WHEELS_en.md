# Part 6 Episode 12 — In front of the world’s red graphs, still placing auxiliary wheels

## Foreword

In Part 6 Episode 11, we stood at the river mouth and looked at what water that had passed through upstream, midstream, and downstream hands over to the sea, and what the sea returns to the watershed as heat, tides, and humidity.

Episode 12 is the final episode of Part 6.

Throughout Part 6, we looked at “red graphs” such as global average temperature, CO₂ concentration, emissions, sea-surface temperature, El Niño, and marine heat waves, while returning again and again to the town, watershed, port, and river mouth.

The world’s large lines will not bend quickly.

Regional auxiliary wheels are small.

Still, being small does not necessarily mean being meaningless.

This is the episode where the protagonist and the AI place the world’s red graphs beside the auxiliary wheels that remain in his own town one last time, and set down the answer of Part 6.

“So we cannot look at the world’s red graphs and say, ‘It is enough if the town does its best.’”

‘You are getting close to the answer of Part 6.’

## Afterword

Thank you for reading through the final episode of Part 6.

Part 6 placed the town, watershed, and sea auxiliary wheels that had continued through Part 5 on the same screen as the world’s “red graphs”: CO₂ concentration, emissions, global average temperature, sea-surface temperature, El Niño, and marine heat waves.

The points organized in Part 6 are mainly these four.

### 1. The world’s red graphs cannot be bent by regional auxiliary wheels alone

CO₂ emissions reduction is necessary.

To keep the entire world from accumulating still more heat, efforts to reduce emissions cannot be stopped.

Regional mist systems, rain gardens, retention basins, and port water-temperature charts cannot directly lower global average temperature or CO₂ concentration.

Regional auxiliary wheels do not replace global emissions reduction.

### 2. That is why CO₂ reduction and auxiliary wheels must not be set against each other

CO₂ reduction is a measure to keep the Earth from continuing to accumulate heat over the long term.

Auxiliary wheels are measures that slightly bend the way already-arriving heat, heavy rain, and sea changes break things locally.

Either one alone is not enough.

It is not CO₂ reduction or auxiliary wheels.

It is CO₂ reduction and auxiliary wheels.

That was the center of Part 6.

### 3. Sea, rain, watershed, river mouth, and town are not disconnected

The heat held by the sea overlaps with city heat and the strengthening of rain through air and humidity.

Rain passes through upstream, midstream, and downstream, and finally returns to the sea through the river mouth.

The sea returns heat and tides to the watershed, and the watershed hands the sea the history of water and soil.

In Part 6, we reexamined this circulation as “a map for placing auxiliary wheels.”

From sea to rain.

From rain to river.

From river to sea.

And then to the town’s front door.

That line is not cut.

### 4. Auxiliary wheels are also systems that divide time and burden

Upstream soil, midstream rain gardens, and downstream retention basins shift the water peak little by little.

Using that time, the town can circulate contact, begin evacuation, and hand support first to those who need help.

By dividing roles and connecting information across town borders, world-scale heat and water problems can be brought closer to a form that is not carried only by one person’s goodwill.

Auxiliary wheels are not only facilities.

They divide water.

They divide time.

They divide information.

They divide roles.

They divide burden.

They are also mechanisms for doing that.

Part 6 did not arrive at the conclusion that “the world is harsh, so regions are meaningless.”

Nor did it arrive at the conclusion that “regional ingenuity alone can save the world.”

The world’s graphs are red.

Even so, regional auxiliary wheels have not disappeared yet.

The conclusion of Part 6 lies between those two statements.

The world’s graphs are red.

Even so, there are still places left to place auxiliary wheels.

In the next Part 7, the “connections between the world and regions” drawn in Part 6 will be examined more concretely as points of contact among institutions, work, everyday life, ports, watersheds, and cities.
