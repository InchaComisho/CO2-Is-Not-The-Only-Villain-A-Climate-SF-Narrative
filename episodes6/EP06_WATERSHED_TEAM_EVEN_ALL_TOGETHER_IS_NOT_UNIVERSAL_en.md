# Part 6 Episode 6 — The watershed team: even all together, they are not universal

## Foreword

In Part 6 Episode 5, we saw how the sea’s heat rises into the sky, how conditions overlap and become rain, and how that rain moves through upstream, midstream, and downstream toward the river.

In Episode 6, we continue from there by placing upstream sponge-like soil, midstream rain gardens, and downstream retention basins together as “one watershed team.”

Each one alone is only a small auxiliary wheel.

But if upstream, midstream, and downstream divide roles and connect with one another, what changes against heavy rain?

This is the episode where the protagonist and the AI look at a watershed map like a game party composition and think about the strength and limits of “combined auxiliary wheels.”

---

“All right.”

I spread the watershed map across the whole screen.

Upstream mountains.

Midstream residential areas.

A downstream river and retention basin.

The river mouth.

The sea beyond it.

“Part 6 Episode 6.”

‘The watershed team is forming.’

“That suddenly sounds like a game.”

‘Because you called it a “combination episode.”’

“I did, but I did not expect the AI to go along with it.”

‘Then let us check the party composition.’

On the screen, three icons appeared like characters.

The first.

Upstream sponge-like soil.

Its role is to slow the speed of water a little.

The second.

Midstream rain gardens.

Their role is to receive water once.

The third.

Downstream retention basins.

Their role is to receive part of the water that has nowhere to go.

“All of them look like they have zero attack power.”

‘It is a defensive party.’

“Can defense alone win against the world’s red graphs?”

‘Do not mistake the victory condition.’

“There it is.”

‘The victory condition for this team is not to erase heavy rain.’

“Then what?”

‘To avoid, even a little, everything breaking all at once.’

“Plain, but important.”

‘Very Part 6.’

“So that convenient phrase continues this time too?”

‘It is the watershed team’s passive effect.’

“You are leaning too hard into the game.”

I shifted my eyes to the upstream part of the map.

## Section 1 — A world line where only upstream works hard

“First, the pattern where only one member fights?”

‘Yes.’

On the left side of the screen, a watershed appeared where only the upstream area had auxiliary wheels.

Grass had been left on mountain slopes.

The field soil could hold a little more water.

When rain fell, the water entered the soil more slowly than before.

“Upstream is working hard.”

‘It is.’

Strong rain arrived.

The soil received a little water.

The speed of water running down the slope also slowed a little.

Soil runoff decreased a little as well.

“That sounds good.”

‘But what happens after that?’

I scrolled down the map.

Midstream residential areas.

Roofs.

Parking lots.

Roads.

Drains.

— Water delayed upstream joins rain from the residential area.

— Water falling from roofs enters drains almost as it is.

— Rainwater from roads also heads toward the river by the shortest route.

“Midstream is adding water at full power.”

‘Yes.’

“The drains are express-delivering upstream’s effort.”

‘That is rather unfortunate teamwork.’

Downstream, the river level rose.

The retention basin was not used.

The levee had to receive almost everything alone.

“Upstream alone is not enough.”

‘Not enough.’

“But that does not mean upstream was meaningless.”

‘It is not meaningless. But it struggles to keep up with the movement of water across the whole watershed.’

“When someone fights alone, that is usually what happens.”

Upstream sponge-like soil can slightly change how water runs down slopes.

It may reduce soil runoff a little.

It may delay the arrival time of water a little.

But if midstream and downstream do not change at all, its effect is easily swallowed on the way.

“When only upstream works hard, it has meaning inside the upstream area. But it is not enough for the whole watershed.”

‘A good summary.’

## Section 2 — A world line where only midstream works hard

“Then next, a town that only has rain gardens.”

The AI opened another screen.

Several houses in the residential area had rain gardens.

Water falling from roofs entered small depressions.

It did not go to the drains immediately, but stayed in the gardens for a little while.

“Midstream is working hard.”

‘It is.’

An evening shower came.

The water level in the drains rose a little more slowly than before.

The timing of water spreading onto the roads also changed a little.

“This is good too.”

‘But upstream?’

I moved the screen back upward.

On the mountain slopes, water ran across hard soil.

Where there was little grass, soil and water flowed together.

Water from the mountains reached the midstream residential area quickly.

“Upstream is taking heavy damage first.”

‘Rain gardens cannot stop mountain slopes.’

“And downstream?”

The river level rose.

The retention basin remained unused.

Outside the levee, there were few places where water could spread.

“No matter how hard midstream works, it is hard if upstream and downstream stay the same.”

‘Yes.’

“Rain gardens are strong, but not solo players.”

‘Because this is a team battle.’

Rain gardens are auxiliary wheels that receive water in residential areas once.

They keep part of the water coming from roofs and roads from flowing straight into drains.

That matters.

But if water arrives quickly from upstream, midstream alone cannot receive all of it.

If there is no place downstream for water to escape to, even delayed water will eventually jam somewhere.

“Midstream alone can work inside the town, but it cannot support the whole watershed.”

‘Exactly.’

## Section 3 — A world line where only downstream works hard

“Finally, a world where only the retention basin exists.”

‘The last shield downstream.’

The screen showed a wide grassland and a retention-basin gate.

The river level rose.

When it crossed a certain line, water entered the retention basin.

It spread across the grassland.

The amount of water heading toward the town center decreased a little.

“This looks pretty reliable.”

‘Because it is the role that receives water at the end.’

“A tank role.”

‘You can call it that.’

But water from upstream was fast.

Water from the midstream residential area entered all at once too.

The water level in the retention basin rose faster than expected.

“The tank is receiving everything alone.”

‘The burden concentrates.’

“Having a retention basin does not mean we can relax.”

‘Correct.’

A place that receives water is necessary.

But if the amount and speed of water reaching it are too large, even the final shield has limits.

“In the end, just ‘handling it downstream’ is not enough.”

‘Because water comes from above.’

A retention basin is an auxiliary wheel that changes where the burden is placed.

Instead of leaving everything to the levee, it predetermines a place where water is allowed to come.

That has great meaning.

But if upstream and midstream send water at maximum speed, downstream becomes heavy all at once.

“To protect the final shield, upstream and midstream are also needed.”

‘That is the watershed-team way of thinking.’

## Section 4 — What changes when all three are together?

“Then, the real thing.”

I returned the watershed map to the center.

Upstream.

Midstream.

Downstream.

Three auxiliary wheels were placed in their respective locations.

‘Watershed team, all members deployed.’

“I’m counting on you, defensive party.”

A rain-cloud icon appeared above the mountains.

“It is here.”

‘Strong rain.’

“In a game, the background music would change here.”

‘In reality, a notification sound goes off.’

“That is scary in its own way.”

Rain fell upstream.

The sponge-like soil received part of the water.

The grass bands slowed the force of the water a little.

Water flowed.

But not at maximum speed.

Rain fell on the midstream residential area.

Part of the roof water entered the depressions of rain gardens.

It collected there and slowly soaked into the ground.

The water heading toward the drains was delayed a little.

Downstream, the river level rose.

Even so, upstream and midstream had created a few minutes, ten-odd minutes, or a little more in some places.

The retention basin received that time.

Water spread where it was supposed to spread.

“Visually, it is plain.”

‘The heavy rain is still heavy rain.’

“The river is rising too.”

‘It is.’

“The town is tense.”

‘It is.’

“Then what changed?”

The AI displayed three lines on the screen.

The first.

The peak of water flowing out upstream.

The second.

The peak of water gathering in midstream drains.

The third.

The peak of water reaching the downstream river.

In the unmoving world line, the three lines overlapped at almost the same time.

In the world line that had bent slightly, the three lines shifted little by little.

“The peaks do not overlap.”

‘Not “they do not overlap at all.”’

“They shift a little.”

‘They shift a little.’

“That ‘little’ becomes time for the downstream retention basin and evacuation.”

‘Exactly.’

“The heavy rain itself has not disappeared.”

‘It has not.’

“But it no longer feels like everything is storming downstream at the same moment.”

‘Crude, but easy to understand.’

“The watershed team really is defensive.”

‘Defensive. But defense is not all it does.’

“What do you mean?”

‘It creates time.’

“Time for evacuation and judgment?”

‘Yes.’

## Section 5 — Combined auxiliary wheels are strong. But they are not invincible

“Can this win?”

I asked, half hopeful.

The AI paused in its usual way.

‘It cannot.’

“You answered immediately.”

‘If the scale of the heavy rain exceeds assumptions, even the whole team cannot endure it.’

“Again, not universal.”

‘I said that at the beginning.’

“You did, but when things combine, I expect a little.”

‘In games, combination skills are strong.’

“And in reality?”

‘In reality, they do not reduce damage to zero. They create a margin for evacuation. They reduce, a little, the places that break. They make recovery easier.’

“Plain.”

‘But that plainness is close to people’s lives.’

I looked at the map.

Upstream fields.

Midstream gardens.

Downstream grassland.

All of them were small.

But each had a role against the flow of water.

— Upstream delays water a little.

— Midstream receives water once.

— Downstream creates a place for water to go.

“Alone, they are small.”

‘Even the three together are small.’

“But they face the same direction.”

‘That is the difference.’

“The watershed team is not strong because it is powerful. It is a team that does not leave water alone.”

‘That is a good way to say it.’

“Is today praise day?”

‘Sometimes.’

Do not make upstream carry water alone.

Do not make midstream drains carry water alone.

Do not make downstream levees carry water alone.

Do not make retention basins carry water alone.

Across the whole watershed, receive a little.

Delay a little.

Release a little.

“Do not leave water alone.”

‘That may become the center of Episode 6.’

“Here we return to the question of Part 6.”

I looked at the world’s red graphs, still left small at the edge of the screen.

High CO₂ concentration.

High average temperature.

A sea holding heat.

Overlapping El Niño.

Anxiety about rain that may become stronger.

“This watershed team does not bend the world’s graphs.”

‘It does not.’

“It does not cool the world’s ocean either.”

‘It does not.’

“But when the world’s heat becomes rain and falls, the town no longer has to be defenseless from the beginning.”

‘Exactly.’

Emissions reduction is work to keep the entire Earth from continuing to accumulate heat.

The watershed team is work to keep rain that has already arrived from making one part of the watershed break all at once.

“The roles are different.”

‘But both are necessary.’

“It is not CO₂ reduction or watershed measures.”

‘It is CO₂ reduction and watershed measures.’

“No matter how many times this line appears, it matters.”

‘As long as the red graphs remain, it will matter as many times as necessary.’

I opened a new note.

— The watershed team is not a team for defeating heavy rain.  
— It is a team for preventing everything from breaking at the same moment under heavy rain.

“Does that close Part 6 Episode 6?”

‘It does.’

“What comes next?”

‘When the watershed team works, what do people in town watch, and when do they evacuate?’

“Evacuation.”

‘If auxiliary wheels create time, people who use that time are also necessary.’

“So next is the human side’s turn.”

‘The watershed team alone cannot move residents.’

“Reality always needs party composition.”

‘That is why it is interesting.’

“The AI is getting more entertainment-oriented.”

‘Because this is Part 6.’

I looked at the watershed map once more.

Upstream.

Midstream.

Downstream.

Small auxiliary wheels were placed in each location.

Even when all of them are present, they are not universal.

But when all of them are present, a form begins to appear in which the burden of water is not forced onto one place.

I wrote one final line.

— The watershed team exists not to defeat heavy rain, but to keep water from being left alone.

Part 6 Episode 6 ends here.

## Afterword

Part 6 Episode 6 placed upstream sponge-like soil, midstream rain gardens, and downstream retention basins together as a “watershed team.”

Each auxiliary wheel looks small on its own.

However, when their roles are divided according to the order in which water flows from upstream to midstream and downstream, the possibility appears of shifting the peak of “water gathering all at once in the same place” little by little.

The points depicted in this episode are mainly these three.

### 1. A single auxiliary wheel has limits

Even if upstream soil is improved, things become difficult if water concentrates in midstream residential areas or at downstream levees.

Rain gardens alone cannot solve water coming from upstream or a shortage of places to receive water downstream.

If the burden concentrates on retention basins alone, the final shield carries too much water.

Each auxiliary wheel has meaning.

But one alone cannot support the entire watershed.

### 2. The watershed team shifts the peak of water little by little

Upstream, soil and grass slow the speed of water a little.

Midstream, rain gardens receive part of the water from roofs and roads once.

Downstream, retention basins create places where water can go.

This can slightly change the way all water overlaps downstream at the same moment.

It does not erase heavy rain itself.

Even so, when peaks shift a little, it may create time for evacuation and judgment, and room for downstream retention basins to receive water.

### 3. Combined auxiliary wheels are still not universal

In heavy rain beyond assumptions, even the watershed team cannot reduce damage to zero.

Even so, it can create a little time for evacuation and judgment, reduce the places that break a little, and move the situation closer to easier recovery.

World-scale emissions reduction and region-scale watershed measures do not replace one another. Their roles must be divided and advanced in parallel.

Part 6 Episode 6 reframed auxiliary wheels not as standalone facilities, but as a team that keeps water from being left alone.

The watershed team exists not to defeat heavy rain, but to keep water from being left alone.

That sentence is the center of Part 6 Episode 6.

Next, we may move on to how people use the small amount of time created by the watershed team.

Evacuation, judgment, information sharing, calling out to others—the “human-side auxiliary wheels” can become the next focus.
