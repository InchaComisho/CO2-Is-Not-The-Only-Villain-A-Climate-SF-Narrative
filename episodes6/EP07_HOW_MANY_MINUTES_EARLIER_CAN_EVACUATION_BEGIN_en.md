# Part 6 Episode 7 — How many minutes earlier can evacuation begin?

## Foreword

In Part 6 Episode 6, we placed upstream sponge-like soil, midstream rain gardens, and downstream retention basins together as a “watershed team.”

None of them is universal.

But each can create a little time before water gathers all at once in one place.

In Episode 7, we look at who uses that “little time,” and how.

Even if auxiliary wheels delay the water peak, their meaning becomes thin if people do not move.

People who watch the river.

People who notify others.

People who hesitate.

People who evacuate first.

This is the episode where, on an evening when heavy-rain notifications ring, the protagonist and the AI look at how “moving a few minutes earlier” may change the way a town breaks.

## Afterword

Part 6 Episode 7 depicted “how the people of the town use the little time created by the watershed team.”

Upstream sponge-like soil, midstream rain gardens, and downstream retention basins do not erase heavy rain.

However, they can slow the flow of water a little, shift the peak a little, and create time that may be used for evacuation and judgment.

The points depicted in this episode are mainly these three.

### 1. The first enemy of evacuation is hesitation time

Even if notifications and hazard maps exist, people hesitate if they do not know whether they personally should move right now.

In the unmoving world line, families begin making decisions only after the water level has risen, which makes it easier for the start of evacuation to be delayed.

Having information and being able to make a decision are not the same thing.

If information is not connected to one’s own life, “we will watch a little longer” piles up even after notifications arrive.

### 2. Auxiliary wheels create both water margin and decision margin

When watershed auxiliary wheels shift the water peak a little, the town can move more easily in stages while watching upstream, midstream, and downstream conditions.

“Evacuate now.”

“Begin preparing.”

“Elderly households move first.”

“Districts along the river move early.”

Such judgments can be shared as stages, not as a simple binary.

Auxiliary wheels do not create only water-level margin.

They create decision margin too.

Turning water time into human time.

That is the major viewpoint of this episode.

### 3. Auxiliary wheels are not “a reason not to evacuate”

The idea that auxiliary wheels mean safety is dangerous.

Auxiliary wheels create time to move people who need evacuation a little earlier, pass messages around, and call out to someone.

Those few minutes or ten-odd minutes can become actions such as taking a bag, calling family, notifying a neighbor, or avoiding a dangerous road.

When rain exceeds assumptions, it may still not be in time.

That is precisely why auxiliary wheels must be treated not as “a reason not to run,” but as “a reason to begin moving early.”

Part 6 Episode 7 reframed auxiliary wheels not only as water mechanisms, but also as mechanisms that create time so people do not leave someone behind.

The few minutes created by auxiliary wheels exist not to stop water, but so people do not leave someone behind.

That sentence is the center of Part 6 Episode 7.

Next time, the story can move toward who receives that time first.

Elderly people, households with small children, people along the river, people without cars, people with health concerns, and others show that the time created by auxiliary wheels does not reach everyone in the same way.
