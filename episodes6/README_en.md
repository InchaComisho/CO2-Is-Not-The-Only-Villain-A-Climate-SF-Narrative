# Episodes 6 / Part 6 — English Guide

**Shosetsuka ni Naro series index:** [Full series](https://ncode.syosetu.com/s5141k/)  
**Repository language guide:** [Japanese index](README.md) | English | [Arabic guide](README_ar.md)

Part 6 places the regional auxiliary wheels built through Part 5 on the same screen as the world’s red graphs.

Station-front mist, park benches, rain gardens, retention basins, and fishing-port water-temperature charts.

They have slightly bent the direction and pace of regional breakdown.

But at the global scale, CO₂ concentration, greenhouse-gas emissions, average temperature, and sea-surface temperature remain high.

Part 6 is not a declaration that regional auxiliary wheels can save the world all at once.

Instead, it asks what regional auxiliary wheels can still mean when the global curve does not bend quickly.

```text
A region cannot bend the global CO₂ curve all at once.
But precisely because the global curve will not bend quickly, regional auxiliary wheels become necessary.
```

## Part 6 Concept

Part 6 begins after Part 5 has followed the lifespan of auxiliary wheels inside a region.

The question is no longer only whether auxiliary wheels can remain inside a town.

The question becomes what they mean when the world-scale graphs remain red.

- **Global graphs:** CO₂ concentration, greenhouse-gas emissions, global temperature, sea-surface temperature.
- **Regional auxiliary wheels:** mist, benches, rain gardens, retention basins, water-temperature charts.
- **Tension:** regional measures are small, but small does not automatically mean meaningless.
- **Viewpoint:** CO₂ reduction remains necessary, but heat, water, soil, forests, seas, and cities must also be watched as circulations.
- **Core question:** when the global curve does not bend quickly, what can regional auxiliary wheels still do?
