# Part 6 Episode 1 — Red graphs, and auxiliary wheels that still remain

## Foreword

Up through Part 5, we looked at how to keep auxiliary wheels going, how to spread them, and how to preserve them, at the scale of one town, one watershed, and one sea.

Part 6 Episode 1 widens the view by one step.

In my own town, the auxiliary wheels are still continuing little by little.

Mist in front of the station.

Park benches arranged with shade in mind.

Rain gardens.

Retention basins.

Water-temperature charts at the fishing port.

They have slightly bent the pace and direction of how things break.

But when I look at the graphs for the whole world, the story suddenly becomes heavy.

CO₂ concentration keeps rising.

Global greenhouse-gas emissions cannot really be said to have turned downward.

Average temperature and sea-surface temperature both remain high.

This is the episode where the protagonist stands at the entrance to Part 6 while moving back and forth between “his own region” and “the red graphs of the world.”

---

“So Part 6 starts with the world right away?”

I muttered while looking at the graphs lined up on the screen.

Station-front mist.

Rain gardens.

Retention basins.

Water-temperature charts at the fishing port.

Up through Part 5, I had kept looking at my own town and the areas around it.

How to keep them going.

How to spread them.

How to make them fit in.

How to pass them on in words.

How to let them become ordinary.

And how to support them even after they become invisible.

I had certainly watched that much.

‘Next, it is time to place those auxiliary wheels inside the movement of the world.’

The AI said that, calm as always.

“The scale suddenly got huge.”

‘But it cannot be avoided.’

“Yeah.”

I put the town logs on the left side of the screen.

On the right side, I lined up global climate data.

Global average temperature.

Atmospheric CO₂ concentration.

Greenhouse-gas emissions.

Sea-surface temperature.

El Niño.

Marine heatwaves.

The right side of the screen was almost entirely red.

“Whoa. Red.”

‘Very red.’

“If this were a game, this would be the UI right before a forced-loss event.”

‘In reality, even during a losing event, one must keep operating.’

“Do not say something that heavy so calmly.”

I sighed.

The old me had thought more simply.

CO₂ increases.

Temperature rises.

So we reduce CO₂.

That is not wrong.

CO₂ being a major cause of warming cannot be denied.

If that is removed, the discussion does not even begin.

But.

“If I thought I could understand everything by looking only at CO₂, I would probably be checkmated like normal.”

‘CO₂ is a major cause. But CO₂ is not the only thing that must be watched.’

“There it is. ‘It is not wrong. But it is not enough.’”

‘Useful, is it not?’

“So useful we should put it on a sign soon.”

‘It is already close to the signboard of the work.’

“I cannot deny that.”

I looked at the world graphs.

The year 2024 was an exceptionally hot year in the observational record.

The year 2025 was also recorded as the third-warmest year on record.

Most of the highest-ranking years are concentrated in the past decade or so.

The atmospheric CO₂ concentration reached 423.9 ppm in 2024.

The increase that year was 3.5 ppm.

Global greenhouse-gas emissions also reached 57.7 GtCO₂e in 2024, a record-high level.

“…Even though we are supposed to be taking action, the graphs look like they are saying, ‘Nope, not bent yet.’”

‘At the very least, the global emissions curve has not bent sufficiently.’

“That wording is too calm.”

‘If one wants to stay close to the facts, that may be better.’

“Fair.”

I looked at the left side of the screen.

There were the auxiliary wheels of my own town.

Station-front mist.

Park benches.

Rain gardens.

Retention basins.

Water-temperature charts at the fishing port.

Up through Part 5, these things had moved once, continued, spread, fit in, and become the town’s own words.

Even after they became hard to see, someone inspected them, recorded them, and handed them on.

“This side has been bent little by little.”

‘At the regional scale, the pace and direction of how things break have been bent slightly.’

“Right.”

In my own town, mist now appears on days of dangerous heat.

Park benches have been moved just enough to make them easier to sit on.

Rain gardens receive water once in the yard before sending it toward the gutters.

Retention basins are beginning to be understood not only as places that push water back, but as places that accept part of it.

At the fishing port, charts that read water temperature are slowly entering decisions about departure and preparation.

‘If you look only at our station front, our river, and our port, they have become better than the world line where nothing was done.’

“That is exactly it.”

But the graph on the right side is red.

Global CO₂ concentration is rising.

Emissions are not merely high; they are at a record-high level.

Temperature and the ocean are not calming down easily.

“I know the auxiliary wheels in my town do not have enough impact to bend the global CO₂ curve.”

‘If you know that, then the next question is clear.’

“Whether they are meaningless, right?”

‘Yes.’

I was silent for a while.

Station-front mist will not lower the global average temperature.

A rain garden will not lower global CO₂ concentration.

A retention basin will not stop marine heatwaves.

A fishing-port water-temperature chart will not erase El Niño.

I know that.

Precisely because I know that, the question is heavy.

“Then are they meaningless?”

I muttered toward the screen.

‘That is what you are going to look at in Part 6.’

“Yeah.”

Back when I looked only at CO₂, I probably saw climate as one straight line.

Reduce emissions.

Lower concentration.

Lower temperature.

I was looking straight at only that.

Of course, it is necessary.

This is not a story saying emissions do not need to be reduced.

Nor is it a story saying we do not need to watch CO₂.

A world line that does not watch CO₂ is out of the question.

But when one looks only at CO₂, other ways of breaking become harder to see.

Forests disappear.

Soil grows poorer.

Urban surfaces hold heat.

The way rivers flow changes.

The ocean stores heat.

Sea-surface temperature patterns such as El Niño affect global temperature and rainfall distribution.

Marine heatwaves overlap with ecosystems, fisheries, and weather.

“The story of reducing CO₂ is necessary. But that alone cannot fully see how heat and water are breaking.”

‘As the entrance to Part 6, that is the point.’

“Not stopping at ‘CO₂ is the villain.’”

‘CO₂ is a major cause. But heat, water, soil, forests, the sea, and cities must be watched at the same time.’

“Right.”

I looked at the red graphs on the right side.

Global average temperature.

CO₂ concentration.

Emissions.

Sea-surface temperature.

All of them were throwing questions at me.

—Are regional auxiliary wheels powerless in front of the world’s red graphs?

—Or are regional auxiliary wheels necessary precisely because the world does not bend quickly?

—What meaning does slightly bending the way things break in one region have, inside the delay of the whole world?

“This is not a question we can run from.”

‘If you run from it, the auxiliary wheels built up through Part 5 end as mere town-level improvements.’

“They still have value, though.”

‘They do. But in Part 6, they will be placed on a wider screen.’

“From my own town to the world’s red graphs.”

‘And from the world’s red graphs back to your own town again.’

“A part that goes back and forth.”

‘Yes.’

I made a new note in the center of the screen.

—A region cannot bend the global CO₂ curve all at once.  
—But precisely because the global curve will not bend quickly, regional auxiliary wheels become necessary.

“That is the first hypothesis of Part 6.”

‘As a hypothesis, it is good.’

“Not an assertion.”

‘It is better not to assert it.’

“Yeah.”

Part 6 will not be a declaration of victory.

It will not be a story saying regional ingenuity saved the world.

If it were that simple, I would not have written this much.

What we will look at is a more troublesome gap.

Global action is delayed.

The red graphs remain red.

Even so, regions still have room to bend how things break slightly.

Is that room meaningless?

Is it an auxiliary wheel?

Is it an escape route?

Is it a way of buying time?

Is it the seed of the next mechanism?

“Part 6 seems like it will look at that area.”

‘It is a part that puts global trends and regional auxiliary wheels on the same screen.’

“If we suddenly swing all the way to the world scale, readers will be left behind.”

‘Then gradually.’

“Right.”

First, look at the red graphs of the world.

But do not get swallowed by them.

Next, look at the auxiliary wheels in my own town.

But do not shut ourselves inside them.

Move back and forth between town and world.

Move back and forth between watershed and sea.

Move back and forth between CO₂, heat, and water.

Put emissions reduction and auxiliary wheels that slightly bend how things break on the same screen.

“That is a lot of information.”

‘It is.’

“Will readers run away?”

‘The protagonist can complain first.’

“So I become the shield.”

‘As usual.’

“That is cruel.”

I smiled slightly while looking at the red graphs.

We are working hard to reduce CO₂.

But emissions are at a record-high level.

We want to lower temperature.

But global average temperature is near the highest level on record.

We want to prepare for changes in the sea.

But sea-surface temperature remains high, and the effects of El Niño and marine heatwaves overlap.

“This is way beyond a three-hit combo.”

‘That is why the screen is red.’

“If this were an RPG, this is where I would hit party-wide healing.”

‘If one could press it, one would want to.’

“In reality, there is no full-heal button.”

‘That is why auxiliary wheels are increased.’

“What a harsh game.”

‘But it is not inoperable.’

At those words, I fell silent for a moment.

Not inoperable.

That was indeed the important part.

Looking at the red graphs of the world makes one want to despair.

But the auxiliary wheels seen through Part 5 had at least slightly bent the way things break around me.

They do not have the power to save the world all at once.

But they are not the same as doing nothing.

“For Part 6 Episode 1, that is enough for now.”

‘Show the world’s red graphs on the screen.’

“Admit that regional auxiliary wheels are small in comparison.”

‘And then ask again whether small means meaningless.’

“Right.”

I wrote the final line into the first log of Part 6.

—The world’s graphs are red. Even so, the region’s auxiliary wheels have not disappeared.

Part 6 Episode 1 ends here.

## Afterword

In Part 6 Episode 1, we placed the regional auxiliary wheels built up through Part 5 on the same screen as global climate graphs for the first time.

Up through Part 5, we looked at how auxiliary wheels can continue, spread, and remain inside a town, a watershed, and a sea.

In Part 6, however, those regional auxiliary wheels will be compared with global trends.

The points that became visible in this episode are mainly three.

### 1. The world’s graphs are still very red

Global average temperature remains high.

The year 2024 was exceptionally hot in the observational record.

The year 2025 was also recorded as the third-warmest year on record.

Most of the top-ranking years are concentrated in the past decade or so.

Atmospheric CO₂ concentration also continues to rise.

In 2024, the global average reached 423.9 ppm.

Global greenhouse-gas emissions also reached 57.7 GtCO₂e in 2024, a record-high level.

In other words, the global emissions curve and temperature curve have not yet bent sufficiently.

### 2. Even so, regional auxiliary wheels have not disappeared

On the other hand, auxiliary wheels continue in the protagonist’s own town.

Station-front mist.

Park benches.

Rain gardens.

Retention basins.

Water-temperature charts at the fishing port.

They do not have the power to bend the global CO₂ curve all at once.

But they increase the few minutes someone can keep standing in dangerous heat.

They receive rain once before letting it flow onward.

They support the decision not to approach the river when the water is high.

They allow departure and preparation to be adjusted slightly while looking at sea temperature.

In those ways, they slightly bend the pace and direction of how a region breaks.

Are they meaningless because they are small?

Or are they necessary precisely because the global curve will not bend quickly?

Part 6 will face that question directly.

### 3. Look not only at CO₂, but also at heat, water, and circulation

This episode did not put CO₂ aside. Instead, it asked whether looking only at CO₂ is sufficient.

CO₂ is a major cause of warming.

Emissions reduction is necessary.

That point cannot be removed.

But when we look only at CO₂, it becomes harder to see other forms of breakdown: urban heat, the loss of forests and soil, the way water flows, the heat stored by the ocean, and the effects of El Niño and marine heatwaves.

In Part 6, while acknowledging the necessity of CO₂ emissions reduction, we move toward a viewpoint that also looks at heat, water, soil, forests, the sea, and cities as interacting circulations.

Regional auxiliary wheels are not universal devices that can save the world all at once.

But the situation is not inoperable.

Even while the world’s graphs remain red, regional auxiliary wheels have not disappeared.

That sentence is the center of Part 6 Episode 1.

From the next episode onward, the world’s CO₂ graph, average temperature, sea-surface temperature, El Niño, marine heatwaves, and regional auxiliary wheels will be connected little by little.

Rather than jumping all at once to the scale of the world, Part 6 will proceed while moving back and forth between one’s own town, watershed, sea, and world.
