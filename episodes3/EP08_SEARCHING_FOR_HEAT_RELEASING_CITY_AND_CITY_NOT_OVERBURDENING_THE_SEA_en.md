# Part 3 Episode 8 — Searching on the same summer map for a city that releases heat and a city that does not push too much heat onto the sea

## Foreword

In Part 3 Episode 7, we placed heavy-rain years and quieter years side by side, and looked at the viewpoint of the people who continue soil auxiliary wheels, along with the difficulty of continuing them.

In Episode 8, the viewpoint shifts to air and cities.

Extreme heat.

Heatwaves.

Urban heat islands.

Exhaust heat from air conditioning.

Places where heat accumulates inside a city, and places where it is being released, even slightly.

Against the backdrop of Japan’s summer news, this episode has the protagonist begin searching on the same summer map, including the sea, for a city that keeps pushing heat outward and a city that has mechanisms to release heat.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading.

In Part 3 Episode 8, against the backdrop of Japan’s extreme-heat news — dangerous heat, heatwaves, and urban heat islands — we placed sea heat and city heat back onto the same summer map.

Here, sea heat means high sea surface temperatures and marine heatwaves around Japan.

City heat means urban heat created by asphalt and concrete storing heat, poor ventilation, and exhaust heat from buildings and air-conditioning equipment.

These two do not arise from one single identical cause.

Nor is urban air-conditioning exhaust heat itself the main cause of marine heatwaves.

However, the sea, the air, and the city exist inside the same circulation in which heat is received, stored, and moved elsewhere.

Episode 8 avoided oversimplifying that connection, and instead revisited it through a familiar structure: cooling the inside of one’s own room by pushing heat outside into the city.

The first point confirmed in this episode was the difference between a city that releases heat and a city that traps heat.

Tree shade.

Watersides.

Wind corridors.

Mist used under appropriate conditions.

Ground that does not store too much heat.

Insulated and solar-shielded buildings.

Efficient cooling equipment.

Where those elements are combined, fragments of a heat-releasing city appear.

On the other hand, where there is no shade, wind is blocked, the ground and buildings store heat, and outdoor-unit exhaust is concentrated, heat becomes difficult to release.

Mist cooling was also not described as equipment that cools anywhere as long as it is installed.

Where humidity is high and wind is weak, mist may not evaporate sufficiently and its effect may weaken.

Therefore, mist needs to be combined with conditions such as shade, wind corridors, water supply, hygiene management, temperature, and humidity.

From stations to hospitals.

From schools to evacuation facilities.

From shopping streets to parks.

If cooling equipment combined with shade and wind can be placed along routes that people must walk even during extreme heat, mist moves closer to being part of life-protecting infrastructure rather than a simple summer event.

We also reframed wind corridors as invisible infrastructure.

Differences in wind caused by building placement and road structure appear on drawings as only lines.

But in the actual city, they appear as differences in bodily temperature: this alley is easy to breathe in, or this intersection feels stagnant.

Wind speed.

Ground-surface temperature.

The times when shade appears.

Places where residents actually felt hot.

If those can be overlaid on a map, then just as a watershed map showed how water moves, it may become possible to visualize where heat gets stuck in a city and where it escapes.

The protagonist’s role was also organized again here.

He is not an urban-planning expert.

He is not in a position to decide building placement or infrastructure investment.

Even so, he can be a citizen walking with a heat map.

Which roads are scorching?

Which alleys have wind?

Which parks become refuges?

Which roads lack shade?

By gathering such bodily experiences, he can stand on the side that thinks about what auxiliary wheels could enter this city.

Part 3 Episode 8 was a rough cataloging episode for air and urban auxiliary wheels: mist cooling used under appropriate conditions, wind corridors, tree shade, watersides, permeable or water-retentive ground, building insulation and solar shielding, and reduction and dispersion of cooling exhaust heat.

In Part 3 Episode 9, the protagonist and the AI will take this catalog and visit places that are actually placing mist and shade as heat countermeasures, or regions trying to incorporate wind corridors and green spaces into urban planning.

There, through the air of specific places and the voices of people, we will depict where air auxiliary wheels work, and where they stop when people try to expand them.
