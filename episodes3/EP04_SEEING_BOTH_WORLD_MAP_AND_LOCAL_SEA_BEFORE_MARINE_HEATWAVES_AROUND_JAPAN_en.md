# Part 3 Episode 4 — Facing marine heatwaves around Japan, I end up seeing both the world map and the sea under my feet

## Foreword

In Part 3 Episode 3, we organized the reasons to intervene and the reasons to wait from the perspective of attaching auxiliary wheels to this sea.

In Episode 4, the stage shifts for a moment to the seas around Japan.

El Niño.

Record global sea surface temperatures.

Multiple typhoons.

Marine heatwaves.

A world map seen only through the news, and what is actually happening in the sea underfoot.

This episode has the protagonist recognize that he cannot directly intervene in the world, yet while standing before fishing ports and the summer sky around Japan, he pulls the auxiliary-wheel discussion back into his own scale.

“Sea surface temperatures around Japan are honestly getting pretty bad too.”

I said that with a sigh as I closed the news app.

El Niño has developed.

Global sea surface temperatures are at record highs.

This year may become one of the hottest in the observational record.

The world map on the screen looked like a bright red patchwork.

‘That is a story about the world’s oceans,’ the AI replied in its usual calm text.

“Yeah, but when you say it is a story about the world…”

I put my phone back in my pocket.

“Most people are only consuming it as news.”

‘Probably.’

“The weather forecast says, ‘This year may be hot again.’”

‘Yes.’

“People say, ‘That is unpleasant,’ and turn on the air conditioner.”

‘Yes.’

“Even if multiple typhoons form, it ends with, ‘There are a lot this year.’”

‘Probably.’

“With difficulty this high, the fact that it ends there is honestly pretty scary.”

‘It is scary.’

“But still…”

Midday blue.

A faint cumulonimbus cloud in the distance.

“Looking at this sky, when someone says global sea surface temperatures, it honestly does not feel real.”

‘Many people feel that way.’

“In Part 1…”

I spoke with a little nostalgia.

“We looked at the world map of El Niño, remember?”

‘We did.’

“The equatorial Pacific was bright red.”

“Lines showing rainfall changes circled around it.”

“And we explained that the red and blue on that map formed the groundwork for extreme weather around the world.”

‘Yes.’

‘No.’

“It is hard to feel that the red of the world map and the blue of the neighborhood sky are part of the same story.”

‘Connecting those is probably the role of Part 3.’

“My work increased again.”

‘It is Part 3.’

“That phrase is strong.”

## Section 4 — Standing at a fishing port, feeling both the world’s red and the smell of the sea

Several days later, I came to a small fishing port on the coast.

It was not morning.

It was early afternoon.

The fishing was already over, and the port was a little quiet.

The smell of tide.

The lingering sound of engines.

Fish shining on ice.

‘It is definitely the sea,’ the AI text appeared on the tablet.

“Obviously.”

‘Yes.’

I stood on the breakwater and looked at the sea surface.

The waves looked as they always did.

But knowing the news numbers made them look slightly different.

This year’s sea surface temperature.

One degree above normal.

In some places, two degrees above normal.

“One or two degrees…”

I said while looking at the sea from the breakwater.

“In a swimming pool, that would only feel like it is a little lukewarm.”

‘Yes.’

“But in the world of the sea, the sky, and typhoons, it is a difference on the level of changing the conditions.”

‘Yes.’

“For fish, plankton, and typhoons, it is probably not just a little lukewarm.”

‘Probably not.’

“Seeing that difference from here is my current position.”

‘That is the position of the protagonist in Part 3.’

News numbers — sea surface temperatures one or two degrees above normal — appear in the lives of fishing ports and coastal towns as fish species, catch volume, port economies, and summer heat.

The protagonist revisits the distance between those numbers and daily life from the top of a breakwater.

Here, he honestly takes the position that the reasons to wait are currently stronger.

Instead, the episode points toward auxiliary wheels closer to his own scale around Japan: watershed flood control, soil regeneration, urban cooling infrastructure, and improvements to coastal water flow paths.

Reality is very much hard mode.
