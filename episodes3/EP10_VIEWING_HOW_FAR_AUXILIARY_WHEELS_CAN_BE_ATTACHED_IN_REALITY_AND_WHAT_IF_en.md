# Part 3 Episode 10 — Looking once at how far auxiliary wheels can be attached, in both reality and a what-if world

## Foreword

From Part 3 Episode 1 through Episode 9, we examined the auxiliary wheels of the sea, soil, and air from the perspective of where they could realistically enter society and where they were still difficult to introduce.

In Episode 10, the protagonist pauses to summarize the whole picture.

Ocean-side auxiliary wheels remain, in present reality, at the stage of concepts, research, and limited consideration.

Soil and watershed auxiliary wheels, and air and urban auxiliary wheels, have already begun moving in small real-world settings.

This episode gives the protagonist an opportunity to put his own interim answer into words for Part 3’s question — how far auxiliary wheels can be attached to society — while distinguishing between the point reached in present reality and the extension imagined in a world where they actually moved.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading.

In Part 3 Episode 10, the protagonist and the AI organized once more how far the auxiliary wheels of the sea, soil, air, and cities have reached present reality, and where concepts and what-if scenarios begin.

The most important point was not to confuse the reality line with the what-if line.

For deep-ocean aeration and Blue Pulse, the present world and the alternate world depicted in Part 4 are treated separately.

In present reality, there are concepts, research, and possibilities for small-scale consideration of human assistance to ocean circulation and ocean heat.

However, full operation across broad sea areas at a scale capable of affecting ocean heat or the development conditions of El Niño is not yet treated as realized.

The development in which Blue Pulse begins off Chile and expands toward Peru belongs to the alternate world of Part 4, where the auxiliary wheels actually moved.

It does not mean that present technology and institutions have already reached that point.

Soil auxiliary wheels occupy a different position.

Sponge-like soil.

Cover crops.

Rain gardens.

Permeable pavement.

Retention areas.

Watershed maps and continuous records.

Each may be small in scale, but each can be moved inside present reality.

An upstream farmer watches the history of how things collapse while changing soil care.

A midstream resident watches water be received once in a rain garden.

A municipality adds what happened after heavy rain to its watershed map.

They have not been introduced into every watershed.

Even so, as something better, soil auxiliary wheels have already begun reaching small real places.

Air and urban auxiliary wheels stand between events and infrastructure.

Some, such as mist installed every year by shopping streets, continue through human effort and single-year subsidies.

Others, such as fixed-pipe mist in redevelopment areas and wind corridors built into plans, have begun becoming infrastructure as part of thermal-environment design.

They are easier to introduce into newly designed districts.

They are harder to introduce into cities where buildings and roads are already fixed.

Even so, existing cities may introduce them gradually in priority areas such as station fronts, hospital surroundings, school routes, and places often used by older people.

The interim answer is as follows.

Ocean auxiliary wheels remain close to concepts, research, and limited consideration in present reality.

Soil and watershed auxiliary wheels have begun reaching small real places.

Air and urban auxiliary wheels have begun becoming infrastructure in some districts.

None has reached every sea, every watershed, or every city.

Still, not reaching everything is not the same as nothing existing.

Auxiliary wheels are not universal.

Ocean auxiliary wheels do not make El Niño disappear completely.

Soil auxiliary wheels do not prevent every flood or landslide.

Air and urban auxiliary wheels do not turn extreme heat into a comfortable summer.

But if the goal is not to restore everything exactly as it was, but to bend the speed and direction of collapse a little, there is room for action in present reality.

The protagonist’s role was also clarified again.

He is not the person who designs, decides, or constructs the auxiliary wheels.

He records how far they have reached in the reality line, where they stop, and what constraints different people carry.

Then, in Part 4, he will depict as a what-if line a world where Blue Pulse and the sea-soil-air trinity actually moved, asking what changed, what did not, and what new problems emerged.

Part 3 is the part that looks at the reality before auxiliary wheels are attached.

Part 4 is the part that looks at a what-if that includes the reality after they are attached.

That boundary became a little clearer in this episode.

Part 3 Episode 10 is not the final answer to how far auxiliary wheels can be attached to society.

It is an interim answer that honestly records the present position.
