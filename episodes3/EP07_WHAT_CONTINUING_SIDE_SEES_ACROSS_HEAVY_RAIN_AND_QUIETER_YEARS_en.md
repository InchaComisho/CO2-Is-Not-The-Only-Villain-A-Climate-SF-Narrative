# Part 3 Episode 7 — When heavy-rain years and quieter years are placed side by side, what does the side that keeps going see?

## Foreword

In Part 3 Episode 6, we visited the farmer who made soil into a sponge, the resident who made a rain garden, and the municipality drawing the watershed map, all within the same watershed, and saw soil auxiliary wheels beginning to move in the field.

In Episode 7, we follow those attempts across time and ask how they look different in years of heavy rain and years that are not quite as severe.

A year when damage occurred, and a year when things barely held.

This episode looks at differences that are difficult to measure in numbers, while the protagonist listens more deeply to why people continue the auxiliary wheels and why continuing is difficult.

---

“Being told every year that this year is abnormal is already normally abnormal.”

Watching the news before the end of the rainy season, I muttered that.

Heavy rain.

Extreme heat.

Linear precipitation bands.

Marine heatwaves.

Every year comes with phrases such as unprecedented and record-breaking.

‘Abnormality is becoming normal,’ the AI displayed calmly.

“And in the middle of that…”

I took out a copy of the watershed map.

“How do the people moving soil auxiliary wheels in this river see a heavy-rain year and a year that is not quite as severe?”

‘A good focus.’

“Episode 7 is where I go ask that.”

‘Probably.’

## Section 1 — The upstream farmer remembered not whether damage happened, but the history of how things collapsed

I went back to the upstream field.

“How is the rain this year?”

The farmer thought for a moment before answering.

“Better than last year.”

“In the news, both last year and this year were called abnormal rain.”

“Yes.”

“But they feel different?”

“They do.”

The farmer pointed to a slope beyond the field.

“This place collapses very easily.”

“Yes.”

“During a major rain several years ago, it collapsed badly.”

“Was that the one in the news?”

“It did not receive a single line in the news.”

“Right.”

“But for us, it was a major incident.”

“I understand.”

“After that, we gradually changed how we cared for the soil.”

“The sponge plot.”

“Yes.”

“What happened during the heavy rains after that?”

“There were years when it did not collapse, and years when it collapsed a little.”

“Yes.”

“In the news, every one of those years is simply heavy rain.”

“Right.”

“But the history of how it collapsed is different.”

‘History,’ the AI displayed in small text.

## Section 2 — Seeing not zero damage, but the years that barely held

“How do you remember the history of how it collapsed?”

The farmer counted on his fingers.

“How much collapsed in which year.”

“Specifically?”

“One year, it collapsed badly.”

“Yes.”

“Another year, it collapsed a little.”

“Yes.”

“There was also a year when it hardly collapsed at all.”

“And then you compare before and after changing the soil?”

“Yes.”

The farmer nodded.

“Of course, the way the rain falls is different every year, so we cannot compare them simply.”

“That is the difficult part.”

“It is not as if it stopped collapsing in every year.”

“Yes.”

“But I feel there have been more years when it barely held.”

“That is a difference that never appears in the news.”

“No.”

The farmer smiled.

“The news reports either major damage or no notable damage.”

“The middle is rarely discussed.”

“Yes.”

“But the people who continue in the field are looking at that middle.”

“The years that barely held.”

“Yes.”

“The years when some soil remained that would previously have washed away.”

“Yes.”

“The years when a slope that would previously have collapsed endured this time.”

“I think we can continue because we remember years like that.”

That sentence made the phrase something better, which we had used since Part 2, a little more concrete.

Better does not mean damage becomes zero.

It means holding a little longer than before.

Losing a little less.

And someone remembering that difference.

## Section 3 — The owner of the midstream rain garden watched the garden during a heavy-rain year

I went down to the midstream town.

In front of the rain garden.

The same house as before.

The same garden as before.

But today was after rain.

A little moisture still remained in the low center of the garden.

“How was the heavy rain this year?”

The owner smiled slightly.

“I will start with the garden.”

“Please.”

“On days of heavy rain, everyone looks outside through the window.”

“Yes.”

“We do too.”

“Yes.”

“Before, we watched the road and the gutter.”

“To see whether water was overflowing.”

“Exactly.”

“Now, we also watch the garden.”

“One more thing has entered your view.”

“Yes.”

The owner pointed to the center of the garden.

“We watch water from the roof enter here, collect a little, and then slowly recede.”

“Yes.”

“When I see that, I think that without this place, all of that water would have gone to the gutter at once.”

“I see.”

“Then I turn on the news, and they say the river is rising because of heavy rain.”

“Yes.”

“I do not think one house changed anything.”

“Right.”

“But I imagine on my own that if there were a hundred gardens like this in this town, the river might have been a little different.”

“And that imagination is part of why you continue?”

“Maybe.”

‘The effect has not been proven,’ the AI displayed in the corner of the screen.

“I know.”

‘But the owner has become aware of the amount and speed of water leaving the house.’

“That alone changes how things look.”

By making a rain garden, this person had added one more place from which to watch rain.

Not only the road.

Not only the gutter.

Not only the river.

They watched their own garden receive water.

That gaze itself may also have been part of the soil auxiliary wheel.

## Section 4 — The municipal staff member recorded subtle changes inside the line

In the meeting room at town hall, the watershed map had been slightly updated.

“It has been updated.”

The staff member nodded.

“Yes. We added results from interviews and field checks after the heavy rain.”

“What changed?”

“For example, here.”

The staff member pointed to one slope.

“This place has long been prone to collapse, but this time it did not develop into a major failure.”

“That is the slope the upstream farmer mentioned.”

“Probably that area.”

“Can you say it was because of the soil management?”

The staff member did not nod immediately.

“Not yet.”

“I thought so.”

“The amount and pattern of rainfall are different every time.”

“Yes.”

“It is difficult to isolate one initiative and say this worked.”

“But you record it.”

“Yes.”

The staff member pointed to another place.

“Here, there were reports that water collected more easily than before.”

“Did the risk increase?”

“We cannot conclude that. But the way water gathers may be changing slightly.”

“If water flow changes upstream or midstream, it may affect other places too.”

“That is possible.”

“So the changes are not necessarily all positive.”

“Correct.”

The staff member looked over the whole watershed map.

“That is why we need records across the whole watershed.”

“It does not end because one place improved.”

“No.”

“What happened midstream and downstream after water was delayed slightly upstream?”

“How did water gathering change in places where rain gardens or permeable pavement increased?”

“When a retention area was used, how much was the water level suppressed?”

“We look at such things together with several years of heavy-rain events.”

“That takes time.”

“It does.”

“But if we want to see what is changing inside this line, we have no choice but to continue.”

The map did not contain the correct answer.

Instead, it contained what had been observed.

What had been heard.

What was still unknown.

What should be checked next.

Those things were being added little by little.

I opened my notebook.

“The upstream farmer continues while watching the history of how things collapsed.”

“The rain-garden owner continues while watching water enter the garden once.”

‘Yes.’

“The municipal staff member continues while recording subtle changes inside the line.”

‘Yes.’

“Almost none of those differences appear in a single number such as total damage in a heavy-rain year.”

‘Correct.’

“But for the side that keeps going, those differences matter.”

‘Probably.’

“Still, we cannot decide from feeling alone that the measure worked.”

‘That is also important.’

“Preserve the field’s lived experience.”

‘Yes.’

“At the same time, continue observation and recording.”

The resident’s observation.

The municipality’s records.

None of them alone provides the answer for the whole watershed.

## Section 7 — The difficulty of continuing must also be stated clearly

‘How does the difficulty of continuing look to you?’ the AI asked.

“Honestly, very difficult.”

I became serious.

“The farmer carries labor and yield risk.”

‘Yes.’

“Cover crops, compost, and changes in tillage do not continue by themselves.”

‘Yes.’

“The rain-garden owner cleans and maintains it personally.”

‘Correct.’

“The municipal staff member keeps updating the map little by little despite limited budget, staff, and time.”

‘Yes.’

“None of it is work that receives applause.”

‘Probably not.’

“It hardly appears in the news.”

‘No.’

“And what appears as an outcome is something like a difference in how things collapsed in a heavy-rain year, water entering the garden once, or one more record added to the map.”

‘Yes.’

“Yet they continue.”

‘Yes.’

“But I do not want to package that only as beautiful effort.”

‘Why?’

“Because if the burden stays uneven, it will eventually stop.”

‘Correct.’

“Only farmers carry the extra work.”

“Only residents maintain the gardens.”

“Only municipal staff keep records with too few people.”

‘Yes.’

“Then the side that keeps going becomes exhausted.”

‘That is why support systems are needed.’

“Subsidies, technical support, shared recording systems.”

‘Yes.’

“Episode 7 does not build those systems yet, but I want to write that we must not place everything on the goodwill of those who continue.”
