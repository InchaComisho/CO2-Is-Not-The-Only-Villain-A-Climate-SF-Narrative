# Part 3 Episode 11 — Where should the branch point be placed between an unchanged reality and a world where things moved?

## Foreword

In Part 3 Episode 10, we organized how far the auxiliary wheels of the sea, soil, and air had reached in the present reality line, and where concepts and what-if scenarios began.

In Episode 11, we step back and look at the whole picture once more.

Up to this point, we write the reality line.

From here on, we write a world line in which the auxiliary wheels actually began to move.

A reality that continues without moving.

And a world line in which something began to move.

This episode is where the protagonist, as a citizen who writes stories, decides to divide his work into two forms: a reality-line log and a what-if-line log.

---

“How much of Part 3 is actually a story about reality?”

Scrolling through the folder, I asked in a slightly serious voice.

A concept for helping circulate the sea.

Sponge-like fields and rain gardens in watersheds.

Urban mist and wind corridors.

El Niño and marine heatwaves in the news.

Dangerous heat and heavy rain.

‘All of them either exist or could exist within the present world line,’ the AI displayed in the corner of the screen.

“Right.”

“But from Part 4 onward, I also plan to write about a world line that has not yet happened in reality.”

‘Yes.’

“I think Part 3 Episode 11 is where I draw that boundary myself.”

‘A boundary.’

“A line that prevents reality and imagination from being mixed together.”

‘Important.’

“But imagination is not meaningless just because it is imagination.”

‘Then the question is how to write the two differently.’

---

## Section 1 — Separating the reality-line log from the what-if-line log

“First, I want to decide the terminology.”

I opened my notebook.

“Until now, I called everything a story. But from Part 4 onward, I will divide it into two.”

‘How?’

“A reality-line log and a what-if-line log.”

‘Clear enough.’

“The reality-line log includes…”

I wrote them down one by one.

“News that is actually happening now.”

“Auxiliary wheels that are actually operating now.”

“Things currently proposed as concepts.”

“Things currently stopped before institutions, budgets, or agreement.”

‘Yes.’

“And the what-if-line log includes…”

“A world where a stalled concept moved into demonstration.”

“A world where small initiatives spread into other regions.”

“A world where institutions and budgets made different choices from the present.”

‘Yes.’

“A world line in which Blue Pulse actually began, and the trinity of sea, soil, and air started operating in earnest across several marine areas, watersheds, and cities.”

‘That is the what-if world portrayed in Part 4.’

“In Part 3 Episode 11, I want to draw the line myself and say: up to here, I wrote the reality-line log; from here on, I will also use the what-if-line log.”

‘That becomes the branch point.’

---

## Section 2 — The branch point for ocean auxiliary wheels is whether a concept advances into demonstration

“Let’s start with the sea.”

I laid out the ocean notes made in Parts 1 and 3.

Deep-ocean aeration.

Ocean circulation.

Deep cold water.

Nutrients.

Ocean heat.

Fisheries.

Ecosystems.

National borders.

Costs.

Responsibility.

“In the present reality line…”

I chose my words carefully.

“Blue Pulse is a concept asking whether human intervention could slightly assist ocean circulation.”

‘Yes.’

“Which marine area could be tested?”

“At what scale could ecosystem impacts be monitored?”

“Who would authorize it, and who would be responsible for stopping it?”

‘Yes.’

“We are still at the stage of organizing those conditions.”

‘That is correct.’

“So in the reality line, demonstration has not yet begun off Chile.”

‘That distinction must be explicit.’

“In the Part 4 what-if line…”

I opened a new page.

“Blue Pulse, which had remained a concept, advances to a small-scale demonstration in a limited marine area off Chile.”

‘Yes.’

“The observation area is defined.”

“Operating conditions are defined.”

“Stop conditions are defined.”

“Fishers, researchers, and public authorities share monitoring data.”

‘Yes.’

“If no major problem appears, the operating range is reviewed gradually.”

‘And only at a later stage is expansion toward waters off Peru considered.’

“Exactly.”

“It does not suddenly place equipment in the center of El Niño.”

‘Of course not.’

“So the first ocean branch point is…”

I drew a line in my notebook.

— Does Blue Pulse remain only a concept?

— Or does it advance into a monitored demonstration in a limited marine area?

‘That is a natural boundary between the reality line and the what-if line.’

“Beyond that lies a second branch.”

‘Whether it proceeds beyond waters off Chile.’

“Yes.”

“Does it expand toward waters off Peru based on the demonstration results?”

“Does it remain limited to waters off Chile?”

“Does a problem emerge and stop the plan itself?”

‘Even inside the what-if line, there are further branches.’

“So a world where things moved is not a world where success was predetermined.”

‘It is a world where movement began, not one where success was guaranteed.’

“I need to remember that in Part 4.”

## Section 3 — The soil and watershed branch point is whether one watershed expands into multiple watersheds

“The soil and watershed story is closer to daily life.”

I opened the pages about the sponge field, rain garden, and watershed map.

“In the present reality line…”

“Upstream on one river, there is a farmer trying to make the soil more sponge-like.”

‘Yes.’

“In the middle reaches, there is a rain garden that receives rainfall once.”

‘Yes.’

“The municipality adds what happened after heavy rain to the watershed map.”

‘Yes.’

“Similar initiatives may also be scattered across other regions.”

‘Probably.’

“But each moves separately, without becoming a nationwide system or shared network.”

‘That is as far as the reality line goes.’

“In the Part 4 what-if line…”

I imagined several watershed maps placed side by side.

“Multiple municipalities share records collected in their own watersheds.”

‘Yes.’

“What kind of soil management was used?”

“Where were rain gardens placed?”

“Which rice fields were used as temporary retention areas?”

“During which heavy rain did a place hold, and where did a new burden appear?”

‘Yes.’

“One successful example is not copied unchanged across the whole country.”

‘Regional differences matter.’

“Each watershed takes home only the parts that fit its terrain, soil, and way of life.”

‘A loose form of sharing.’

“The soil and watershed branch point is…”

I wrote it down.

— Do small attempts remain inside a single watershed?

— Or do multiple watersheds advance to the point of sharing records and failures?

‘A branch closer to present reality than the ocean one.’

“It feels like the bigger wall is not technology, but institutions and continuity.”

‘Probably.’

“Do they continue only through someone’s goodwill?”

“Or are they supported by budgets, staff, and recording systems?”

‘That is also a branch.’

---

## Section 4 — The air and urban branch point is whether temporary equipment becomes a premise of the city

“Air and urban auxiliary wheels have a double branch point.”

I opened the mist and wind notes.

“The first is whether they advance from events to infrastructure.”

‘Yes.’

“In the current reality line…”

“A shopping street hangs mist lines every year and removes them every year.”

‘Yes.’

“They continue through subsidies, staff labor, and local goodwill.”

‘Yes.’

“Meanwhile, some redevelopment areas already include fixed mist piping, shade, greenery, and wind corridors from the design stage.”

‘Correct.’

“Up to here can be written as part of the reality-line log.”

‘Yes.’

“In the what-if line…”

“Priority heat-protection routes are created around stations, hospitals, school routes, and streets often used by older residents.”

‘Yes.’

“Shade, rest areas, wind, and mist used under appropriate conditions are arranged as one continuous route.”

‘It becomes a line instead of isolated points.’

“Exactly.”

“The second branch is the wind corridor.”

‘Yes.’

“In the present reality line…”

“Some newly built districts include wind corridors in their plans.”

‘Yes.’

“In existing cities, residents remember cool roads and hot roads through bodily experience.”

‘They do.’

“But that experience has not sufficiently returned to planning documents or budgets.”

‘That is where the line has stopped.’

“In the what-if line…”

“Residents’ bodily experience.”

“Ground-surface temperature.”

“Wind speed.”

“The times when shade appears.”

“Locations of heatstroke cases.”

‘Yes.’

“An urban heat map combining those layers is used as evidence for redevelopment and road-improvement decisions.”

‘Wind corridors stop being an unusual idea.’

“They become a somewhat ordinary option in urban planning.”

‘That is the branch.’

“So the air and urban branch point is…”

I wrote it down.

— Do heat countermeasures remain annual events?

— Or do they enter the premises of urban design, management, and budgeting?

## Section 5 — There is meaning in writing the reality line that does not move

‘How far will you write the reality line?’ the AI asked.

“Until the end of Part 3, I have written it as a reality-line log.”

I thought back over the episodes.

At sea, concepts collided with real conditions.

In soil and watersheds, I listened to people continuing small auxiliary wheels.

In air and cities, I saw the difference between events and infrastructure.

“In Part 3 Episode 11, I want to consider whether there is meaning in continuing to write the reality line that does not move.”

‘Is there?’

“Yes.”

‘Where?’

“As a record of a world that continued without major change.”

‘A record.’

“El Niño occurs again.”

‘Yes.’

“Sea surface temperature reaches another record high.”

‘Yes.’

“Extreme heat and heavy rain become news again.”

‘Yes.’

“Some farmers and residents continue soil auxiliary wheels.”

‘Yes.’

“Urban mist is installed only during summer again.”

‘Yes.’

“But no major institutional change or ocean demonstration begins.”

‘That is the unchanged reality line.’

“It is not a world where absolutely nothing happens.”

‘Small movement exists.’

“But small movement does not spread.”

‘Yes.’

“It is not easy to decide whether that world line should simply be called a failure.”

‘Why?’

“Because the reasons it did not move are not always laziness.”

‘Correct.’

“There was no budget.”

“Agreement could not be reached.”

“Risk could not be evaluated sufficiently.”

“The people maintaining it became exhausted.”

“Another disaster or issue took priority.”

‘Yes.’

“As a result, it moved only this far.”

‘The reality line needs to preserve those reasons too.’

“Not only the fact that it did not move, but why it did not move.”

‘That becomes the basis for comparison.’

“If I write only the what-if line, the people who did not move may look simply foolish.”

‘That would not be accurate.’

“In reality, there are reasons people cannot move.”

‘Yes.’

“Only after writing those reasons can we see the difference from the world line that moved.”

## Section 6 — The meaning of leaving the world line that moved as a story

“And then I will also write the world line that moved.”

In my mind, I overlaid the sea off Chile, watershed maps, and city plans.

Blue Pulse moves from concept to small-scale demonstration.

Multiple watersheds share records of success and failure.

Urban heat maps return to the plans for the next redevelopment.

‘Yes.’

“At this point, that world line is still a what-if.”

‘Yes.’

“But if I leave the what-if as a story, someone reading later may think: this option might also have existed.”

‘A log of possibility.’

“Exactly.”

“Even if reality did not choose that option…”

‘Yes.’

“If its structure remains inside a story, the next generation may be able to pick it up again.”

‘There is no guarantee.’

“No.”

‘But if it is not preserved, it cannot be picked up.’

“Exactly.”

I voiced the one concern that remained.

“But I do not want the world line that moved to become a utopia.”

‘Of course not.’

“If Blue Pulse operates, responsibility for monitoring and stopping it appears.”

‘Yes.’

“If watershed auxiliary wheels spread, burdens of maintenance and evaluation appear.”

‘Yes.’

“If urban cooling spreads, problems of water, electricity, hygiene, and management appear.”

‘Yes.’

“When something begins moving, new possibilities of failure move too.”

‘That too is part of the world line that moved.’

“Not a world that succeeded.”

‘A world that chose.’

“I like that phrase.”

I closed the folder.

‘Yes.’

“Ocean auxiliary wheels remain close to concepts and consideration.”

‘Yes.’

“Soil and watershed auxiliary wheels are moving in small real settings.”

“Air and urban auxiliary wheels stand between events and partial infrastructure.”

‘Yes.’

“In the world line that moved…”

‘To compare them?’

“Not to decide which world was correct.”

‘Then why?’

“To see where the branch points were.”

‘Yes.’

“World lines do not suddenly split all at once.”

‘They separate through the accumulation of small choices.’

“I think those small branches are what Part 4 will examine.”

I created a new folder for Part 4.

I typed its name.

— Part 4: Reality-Line Log / What-If-Line Log

There was nothing inside yet.

I created only two empty folders.

One was called The World That Did Not Move.

The other was called The World That Began to Move.

Both were worlds chosen by humans.

Both had reasons.

Both had failures.

And both might still contain another choice.

I stared at the two folders for a while, then quietly closed the screen.

Part 3 ends here.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Part 3.

In Part 3 Episode 11, we organized the range visible in the present reality line and the extension of the what-if line portrayed in Part 4, clarifying the boundary between Parts 3 and 4 for the question of how far auxiliary wheels can be attached to society.

Three major decisions were made in this episode.

The first was the branch point between the reality line and the what-if line.

For ocean auxiliary wheels, the setting does not treat Blue Pulse as already operating off Chile.

In the reality line, there is a concept for introducing human assistance into ocean circulation and ocean heat, while technology, ecosystems, fisheries, costs, responsibility, and stop conditions remain under consideration.

In the Part 4 what-if line, that concept advances into a monitored small-scale demonstration in a limited marine area off Chile.

Therefore, the first branch point is not whether it reaches waters off Peru from Chile, but whether the concept advances into demonstration at all.

Even if demonstration begins, success is not promised.

Does it continue?

Does it stop?

Does it remain limited to waters off Chile?

Does it later expand toward waters off Peru?

Even inside the what-if line, there are multiple branches.

For soil and watershed auxiliary wheels, the branch point is whether they remain small attempts within one watershed or advance to a stage where multiple watersheds share records and failures.

One successful example is not spread unchanged to every region.

Each watershed learns only the parts that fit its terrain, soil, land use, rainfall, and way of life.

Whether such loose sharing develops becomes the difference between the reality line and the what-if line.

For air and urban auxiliary wheels, the branch point is whether they remain temporary heat countermeasures or enter the premises of urban design, management, and budgets.

Mist installed each year as an event.

Fixed equipment incorporated from the redevelopment design stage.

Cool roads remembered through residents’ bodily experience.

Urban heat maps combining ground-surface temperature, wind speed, shade, and locations of heatstroke cases.

Do these remain separate, or do they connect as evidence for urban-planning decisions?

That becomes the boundary separating the air and urban world lines.

The second decision was the meaning of writing the unchanged reality line.

The unchanged reality line is not a world where absolutely nothing happens.

Small initiatives exist.

Farmers continue caring for the soil.

Residents maintain rain gardens.

Shopping streets install mist each summer.

Municipalities update watershed maps with limited staff.

But those efforts do not connect to major institutions or wider systems.

That is the world that continues.

The reasons it did not move are not simply laziness.

There was no budget.

Agreement could not be reached.

Risk could not be evaluated sufficiently.

The people maintaining the effort became exhausted.

Another disaster or issue took priority.

Writing those reasons makes the difference from the world line that moved visible.

The third decision was the protagonist’s own role.

He decided to hold two pens.

One is the pen for the reality line.

Actual news.

Small initiatives moving in the field.

Places where they stop.

Reasons they stopped.

He records them.

The other is the pen for the what-if line.

A world where concepts advanced into demonstration.

A world where scattered auxiliary wheels connected slightly.

A world where new responsibility and failure appeared because the auxiliary wheels moved.

He preserves them as stories.

What matters here is not portraying the world line that moved as a utopia.

When something moves, new responsibilities and risks begin moving too.

Intervening in the sea requires monitoring and stop decisions.

Spreading watershed initiatives creates problems of maintenance cost, evaluation, and regional difference.

Expanding urban cooling creates problems of water, electricity, hygiene, and equipment management.

Part 4 does not portray a world that succeeded.

It portrays a world that made a different choice from reality.

Part 3 examined how far auxiliary wheels could be attached to society from the side of real institutions, daily life, budgets, and responsibility.

Ocean auxiliary wheels remain close to concepts and consideration.

Soil and watershed auxiliary wheels are moving in small real settings.

Air and urban auxiliary wheels stand between events and partial infrastructure.

None has reached everything.

But not reaching everything is not the same as not existing.

In Part 4, carrying the answer from Part 3, we will place The World That Did Not Move beside The World That Began to Move.

Not to decide which was the correct world.

But to see where the small choices that separated the world lines existed: budget, agreement, responsibility, records, and design.
