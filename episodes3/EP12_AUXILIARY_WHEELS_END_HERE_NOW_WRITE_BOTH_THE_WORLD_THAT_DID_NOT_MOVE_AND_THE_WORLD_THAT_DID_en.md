# Part 3 Episode 12 — The auxiliary wheels end here. From now on, I will write both the world that did not move and the world that did

## Foreword

In Part 3 Episode 11, the protagonist decided in his own words that he would separate the auxiliary wheels of the sea, soil, and air into a reality-line log and a what-if-line log.

In Episode 12, as the conclusion of Part 3, he places a final answer for the present world line to the question: how far can auxiliary wheels be attached to society?

At the same time, the story quietly opens an entrance to the two worlds that Part 4 will depict: the reality line in which little changed on a large scale, and the world line in which Blue Pulse advanced to demonstration, eventually reached the waters off Peru, and the Trinity auxiliary wheels spread a little farther.

This episode is where the protagonist draws a line inside himself: up to here is the Trinity in the reality line, and from here onward is the Trinity in the what-if line. With that, he closes Part 3.

## Afterword

Thank you very much for reading Part 3 to the end.

Part 3 Episode 12 placed the final answer of the present world line to the question of how far auxiliary wheels can be attached to society, and opened the entrance to the two world lines that Part 4 will depict.

The present positions of the sea, soil, air, and urban auxiliary wheels examined in Part 3 are all different.

Deep-sea aeration and Blue Pulse, the sea auxiliary wheels, remain at the stage of concepts, research, and examination in the present reality line.

Which waters could support a demonstration?

At what scale could the effects be monitored?

How should fisheries, ecosystems, borders, costs, responsibility, and stop conditions be organized?

The need and the concept are visible, but they have not yet reached the point of going out to sea as a monitored demonstration.

The sequence in which Blue Pulse advances to a demonstration in limited waters off Chile, accumulates observation and adjustment, and eventually leads to consideration of expansion toward Peru belongs to the world line that moved in Part 4.

Soil and watershed auxiliary wheels are already moving at small sites.

Sponge-like soil.

Cover crops.

Rain gardens.

Retention areas.

Permeable pavement.

Watershed maps and continued records after heavy rain.

Each is small in scale and has not spread to every watershed.

Even so, upstream farmers watch the history of how things collapse.

Residents in the middle reaches watch rain being received once in gardens.

Municipalities add observed changes and remaining uncertainties to watershed maps.

Soil auxiliary wheels are not noticeable.

But in the sense of something better, they have already begun moving inside the reality line.

Air and urban auxiliary wheels stand between events and infrastructure.

Shopping streets install and remove mist systems every year, continuing them through staff effort and subsidies.

In redevelopment areas, fixed mist piping, wind corridors, shade, greenery, and ground materials are incorporated from the beginning as heat-environment design.

In newly built districts, air auxiliary wheels are beginning to enter the plans.

In existing cities, however, land, buildings, roads, rights, and costs create constraints. Measures remain partial and are introduced mainly in priority areas such as station fronts, hospital surroundings, and school routes.

The final answer of Part 3 is simple.

Auxiliary wheels are not universal.

They cannot restore everything or prevent every form of damage.

The sea auxiliary wheel cannot eliminate El Niño completely.

The soil auxiliary wheel cannot prevent every flood or landslide.

The air and urban auxiliary wheels cannot turn extreme heat into a comfortable summer.

Even so, if the goal is to bend the pace and direction of breakdown a little, there is still room to act in the present reality.

Not universal.

But not meaningless.

They do not reach everything.

But there are places they can reach.

That is the answer Part 3 reached.

Part 4 will depict two world lines.

One is the world line that did not move much.

Small efforts continue.

Farmers care for soil, residents maintain rain gardens, shopping streets install mist, and municipalities update watershed maps.

But these actions do not connect to large institutions or broad systems.

El Niño, marine heatwaves, extreme heat, and heavy rain continue, while the sea auxiliary wheel remains a concept.

The other is the world line where auxiliary wheels spread a little farther.

Blue Pulse advances to a monitored small-scale demonstration off Chile.

Multiple watersheds share not only success, but records of failure.

In cities, wind, shade, greenery, mist, and heat maps begin entering decisions about design and budgets.

But this world is not a utopia either.

Intervening in the sea creates responsibilities for monitoring and stop decisions.

Watershed auxiliary wheels create burdens of maintenance and effect measurement.

Urban cooling creates problems involving water, electricity, hygiene, and equipment management.

Part 4 is not a comparison between a successful world and a failed world.

It is a comparison between a world that did not move much and a world that began to move after making different choices.

The protagonist will write the two with two pens.

With the reality-line pen, he will record what moved, what stopped, and why it stopped.

With the what-if-line pen, he will record what changed when concepts advanced to demonstrations and auxiliary wheels spread, and what new responsibilities and problems arose.

Not to force either one as the correct answer.

Budget.

Agreement.

Responsibility.

Records.

Design.

Institutions.

The purpose is to see where the small choices that divided the world lines were located.

Part 3 ends here.

In Part 4, the same protagonist and AI will begin writing two slightly different futures as separate logs.
